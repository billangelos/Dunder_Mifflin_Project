# 1. Purpose

This Enterprise Risk Treatment Plan defines Dunder Mifflin's planned response to the 35 enterprise risks documented in [[REG-002 Enterprise Risk Register]].

The plan converts risk assessment results into coordinated management action by identifying:

- Treatment decisions
- Treatment objectives
- Responsible parties
- Implementation priorities
- Required actions
- Dependencies
- Expected evidence
- Validation methods
- Target residual risk

This plan does not represent evidence that planned controls have been implemented.

Planned treatment receives no credit toward current residual risk until the applicable controls have been implemented and appropriately validated.

---

# 2. Relationship to Risk Management

The risk-management lifecycle is:

**Identify → Analyze → Evaluate → Treat → Implement → Validate → Reassess → Monitor**

The principal artifacts supporting this lifecycle are:

- [[RPT-003 Enterprise Risk Assessment]]
- [[REG-002 Enterprise Risk Register]]
- [[RTP-001 Enterprise Risk Treatment Plan]]
- [[REG-003 Enterprise Security Control Inventory]]
- [[REG-004 Finding & Corrective Action Register]]

The risk register records the risk state.

This treatment plan records management's intended response.

---

# 3. Treatment Options

Dunder Mifflin recognizes four principal treatment options.

## 3.1 Mitigate

Implement or improve controls to reduce likelihood, impact, or both.

## 3.2 Transfer

Shift or share specified consequences through mechanisms such as:

- Insurance
- Contracts
- Service agreements
- Indemnification

Risk transfer does not necessarily eliminate operational risk.

## 3.3 Avoid

Discontinue or materially change the activity producing the risk.

## 3.4 Accept

Retain the risk following appropriate management review and authorization.

Acceptance does not mean that the risk has been eliminated.

---

# 4. Treatment Status

Treatment status follows:

**Planned → Approved → Implementing → Implemented → Validation Pending → Validated**

**Corrective Action Required** may be assigned when treatment fails validation or another material deficiency is identified.

An **Implemented** status means the planned action has been put in place.

It does not mean the control has been demonstrated effective.

---

# 5. Implementation Horizons

Treatment activities are organized using the following general horizons:

| Horizon | General Period |
|---|---|
| Immediate | 0–90 days |
| Near Term | 3–6 months |
| Medium Term | 6–12 months |
| Ongoing | Recurring or continuous activity |

Dependencies may require individual actions within a risk treatment to occur in different horizons.

---

# 6. Treatment Prioritization

Priority considers:

- Current residual risk
- Inherent risk
- Business criticality
- Common-control dependencies
- Treatment dependencies
- Expected risk reduction
- Implementation complexity
- Cost
- Resource availability
- Legal and contractual requirements
- Potential effect on multiple risks

Risk score alone does not determine implementation sequence.

# 8. Validation Methods

Treatment effectiveness may be evaluated through:

- Inspection
- Observation
- Independent Performance
- Technical Testing
- Sampling
- Exercises
- Reconciliation

The method selected must be appropriate to the control being evaluated.

Evidence must not be manufactured, backdated, altered, or otherwise presented as proof of control operation when the control did not operate as represented.

---

# 9. Target Residual Risk

Target residual risk represents the expected result of successful treatment.

It is not:

- Guaranteed
- Automatically achieved
- A minimum possible score
- A substitute for validation

Validated post-treatment risk may be higher than, equal to, or lower than the target.

The validated result becomes the current residual risk for the next assessment period.

Historical risk results must be preserved.

## Expected Evidence

- Restoration-test records
- Recovery-test records
- RTO/RPO results
- Recovery procedures
- Corrective actions

## Validation

- Technical Testing
- Independent Performance
- Recovery Exercise

## Example Validation

For a system with:

- RTO: 8 hours
- RPO: 4 hours

Test result:

- Restoration time: 11 hours 37 minutes
- Data loss: 58 minutes

Result:

- RTO: Failed
- RPO: Passed

Later test:

- Restoration time: 6 hours 42 minutes
- Data loss: 47 minutes

Result:

- RTO: Passed
- RPO: Passed

The later result supports control effectiveness under the tested conditions.

It does not automatically establish the target residual risk.

Risk must still be reassessed.

# 47. Treatment Evidence

Treatment completion must be supported by evidence appropriate to the control.

Evidence may include:

- Approved policies
- Standards
- Procedures
- System configurations
- Screenshots
- Logs
- Access records
- Training records
- Test results
- Assessment reports
- Change records
- Contracts
- Review records
- Reconciliation records
- Exercise results
- Remediation records
- Retest results

Documentation stating that a control exists is not automatically evidence that the control operates effectively.

---

# 48. Validation and Reassessment

After treatment implementation:

1. Confirm implementation.
2. Collect appropriate evidence.
3. Validate operation.
4. Determine control effectiveness.
5. Identify deficiencies.
6. Remediate material deficiencies.
7. Retest when required.
8. Reassess likelihood.
9. Reassess impact.
10. Determine validated residual risk.
11. Update [[REG-002 Enterprise Risk Register]].
12. Report material risk movement.

The target residual rating must not be copied automatically into the risk register.

---

# 49. Failed Validation

When treatment fails validation:

- Document the deficiency.
- Determine affected controls.
- Determine affected risks.
- Record material findings in [[REG-004 Finding & Corrective Action Register]].
- Assign corrective action.
- Establish responsibility.
- Remediate.
- Retest.
- Reassess risk.

A failed control may require the current residual risk to remain unchanged or increase.

---

# 50. Treatment Exceptions

When planned treatment cannot be implemented as designed:

- Document the reason.
- Evaluate resulting risk.
- Identify compensating controls where practical.
- Obtain appropriate approval.
- Establish a review date.
- Track the exception.

Exceptions must not be used to silently convert planned mitigation into undocumented risk acceptance.

---

# 51. Risk Acceptance

Residual risk that remains after reasonable treatment may be accepted according to [[POL-005 Risk Management Policy]].

General authority is:

| Rating | Acceptance Authority |
|---|---|
| Low | Risk Owner |
| Moderate | Risk Owner + Appropriate Executive |
| High | CEO following ISRC review |
| Critical | CEO following ISRC review and exceptional justification |

High risk acceptance must be reassessed within 90 days.

Critical risk acceptance must be reassessed within 30 days.

---

# 52. Management Review

The ISRC reviews:

- Treatment priorities
- Treatment dependencies
- High and Critical risks
- Significant validation failures
- Resource conflicts
- Exceptions
- Corrective actions
- Risk movement
- Significant changes in target expectations

Management may reprioritize treatment when business conditions change.

# 53. Continuous Monitoring

Validated controls enter ongoing monitoring according to their risk, criticality, and operating characteristics.

Continuous monitoring does not mean every control is observed continuously or 24 hours a day.

Monitoring may include:

- Automated alerts
- Periodic review
- Scheduled testing
- Access reviews
- Vulnerability assessments
- Restoration testing
- Exercises
- Metrics
- Management review
- Incident analysis

Monitoring frequency should be appropriate to the risk and control.

# 54. Treatment Plan Maintenance

This plan must be reviewed:

- At least quarterly
- Following material risk reassessment
- Following significant incidents
- Following significant findings
- Following material business changes
- Following material technology changes
- When treatment dependencies materially change
- When target residual-risk assumptions are no longer reasonable

Treatment history should be preserved.

# 55. Traceability

Risk treatment must remain traceable through the security program.

The complete lifecycle is:

**Business Requirement → Asset/Process → Classification/BIA → Risk → Treatment → Policy/Standard → Control → Procedure → Implementation → Evidence → Test/Assessment → Finding → Remediation → Retest → Risk Reassessment → Management Decision → Continuous Monitoring**

Traceability relationships are maintained through [[REG-007 Cross-Reference Matrix]].
