# 1. Purpose

This policy establishes security requirements for third parties that provide services to, access systems belonging to, process information for, or otherwise support Dunder Mifflin.

Third-party relationships must receive security oversight proportionate to:

- Business dependency
- Information sensitivity
- System access
- Operational criticality
- Replacement difficulty
- Security exposure
- Potential business impact

Third-party status does not remove Dunder Mifflin's responsibility to understand and manage risk arising from the relationship.

---

# 2. Scope

This policy applies to third parties including:

- Technology providers
- Cloud service providers
- Software providers
- Contractors
- Consultants
- Infinity development contractors
- Information Technology service providers
- Security service providers
- Penetration testers
- Incident-response providers
- Temporary specialists
- Suppliers
- Outside support personnel
- Other organizations receiving access to Dunder Mifflin information, systems, facilities, or significant business processes

Requirements must be applied according to the nature of the relationship and associated risk.

---

# 3. Objectives

Third-party security management must:

1. Identify significant third-party dependencies.
2. Evaluate security risk before significant access or dependency is established.
3. Classify third parties according to risk and business importance.
4. Establish appropriate contractual protections.
5. Protect sensitive information.
6. Restrict third-party access.
7. Establish incident-notification requirements.
8. Evaluate continuity and recovery dependencies.
9. Review significant third parties periodically.
10. Preserve appropriate audit and assessment rights.
11. Manage relationship termination.
12. Support risk reassessment.

---

# 4. Third-Party Governance

Third-party security risk is a shared business responsibility.

Responsibilities may involve:

- Business owner
- Procurement
- CIO
- Information Technology
- Information Security
- Legal and Compliance
- Finance
- Operations
- Human Resources
- Other applicable management

The business function establishing or maintaining a third-party relationship remains responsible for ensuring required review occurs.

Information Technology does not automatically own every third-party risk involving technology.

---

# 5. Third-Party Inventory

Significant third-party relationships must be identifiable.

Records should identify, as applicable:

- Provider
- Service
- Business owner
- Service classification
- Systems supported
- Information accessed
- Access type
- Contract status
- Review date
- Incident contact
- Renewal or termination information

Inventory depth must remain proportionate to the relationship.

---

# 6. Third-Party Classification

Third parties must be classified as:

1. Critical
2. Important
3. Routine

Classification must reflect the potential effect of the relationship on Dunder Mifflin.

---

# 7. Critical Third Parties

A Critical third party is one whose failure, compromise, loss, or inappropriate access could create significant enterprise impact.

Factors may include:

- Support of Critical business processes
- Significant privileged access
- Access to highly sensitive information
- Significant cloud dependency
- Difficult replacement
- Significant recovery dependency
- Significant application dependency
- Potential for broad enterprise impact

Examples may include:

- Major cloud-hosting providers
- Critical technology providers
- Infinity development contractor
- Other providers supporting significant security or recovery capabilities

---

# 8. Important Third Parties

An Important third party provides meaningful business support or access but does not normally create the same enterprise dependency as a Critical provider.

Factors may include:

- Access to Confidential information
- Support of High or Moderate business functions
- Significant operational dependency
- System access
- Meaningful replacement difficulty

---

# 9. Routine Third Parties

A Routine third party presents limited security or operational dependency.

Examples may include relationships involving:

- No sensitive information
- No system access
- Limited operational dependency
- Easily replaceable goods or services
- Ordinary commercial interaction

Routine classification does not eliminate applicable security, confidentiality, or physical-access requirements.

---

# 10. Classification Factors

Third-party classification must consider:

- Information classification
- Type of system access
- Privileged access
- Business criticality
- Service availability dependency
- Data integrity dependency
- Replacement difficulty
- Recovery dependency
- Network exposure
- Financial dependency
- Physical access
- Contractual requirements

Classification must be based on actual relationship risk rather than vendor size or reputation alone.

---

# 11. Initial Due Diligence

Appropriate due diligence must occur before establishing a material third-party dependency or granting significant access.

Due diligence should evaluate relevant matters including:

- Provider capability
- Security practices
- Business reputation
- Service history
- Financial or operational stability where relevant
- Access requirements
- Information handling
- Incident-response capability
- Recovery capability
- Contract terms
- Personnel requirements
- Dependency and replacement considerations

Due diligence depth must correspond to third-party classification and risk.

---

# 12. Due Diligence Limitations

Due diligence provides information for decision making but does not guarantee that a third party is secure.

A completed questionnaire, contract, reference check, or security review must not automatically be treated as proof of effective controls.

Dunder Mifflin must consider the quality and relevance of available evidence.

---

# 13. Contractual Security Requirements

Contracts for significant third-party relationships should establish security requirements appropriate to the service.

Requirements may address:

- Confidentiality
- Information protection
- Access control
- Security incidents
- Notification
- Recovery
- Service availability
- Data return
- Data destruction
- Audit or assessment rights
- Personnel access
- Subcontractors
- Termination

Legal and Compliance must review significant contractual security provisions where appropriate.

---

# 14. Confidentiality Requirements

Nonemployees and outside parties must have an enforceable confidentiality obligation before receiving sensitive information when the nature of the access requires such protection.

This requirement applies based on:

- Information sensitivity
- Type of access
- Business relationship

It does not depend solely on the third party's title or vendor category.

---

# 15. Parties Requiring Confidentiality Protection

Confidentiality protection may be required for:

- Consultants
- Contractors
- Infinity developers
- Information Technology service providers
- Security service providers
- Penetration testers
- Incident-response specialists
- Temporary specialists
- Outside support personnel
- Other parties requiring sensitive access

The agreement or equivalent enforceable obligation must exist before sensitive access is provided.

---

# 16. Ordinary Third-Party Interaction

Ordinary visitors or service providers do not automatically require a confidentiality agreement when they are not given access to sensitive information.

Examples may include:

- Delivery personnel
- Vendor sales representatives
- Ordinary visitors
- Building-maintenance personnel without sensitive access

Appropriate physical-security requirements still apply.

If the relationship changes to require sensitive access, confidentiality requirements must be satisfied before access is provided.

---

# 17. Information Access

Third-party access to company information must be limited to:

- Authorized information
- Legitimate business need
- Appropriate duration
- Appropriate classification
- Required scope

Third parties must not receive broad information access merely because they provide services to Dunder Mifflin.

---

# 18. System Access

Third-party system access must:

- Have an identified sponsor.
- Have documented business need.
- Be appropriately authorized.
- Use individual accounts where practical.
- Follow least privilege.
- Be time limited.
- Receive appropriate authentication.
- Be removed when no longer required.

Access requirements are established in [[POL-003 Access Control Policy]].

---

# 19. Contractor Access Duration

Initial contractor access should not exceed three months unless a shorter period is appropriate.

Continued access requires review.

Renewals must not exceed six months without another review and authorization.

Contractor access must not remain active indefinitely merely because a contract remains open.

---

# 20. Privileged Third-Party Access

Privileged third-party access requires heightened control.

Requirements must include, as applicable:

- Explicit authorization
- Least privilege
- Individual accountability
- Strong authentication
- Defined duration
- Approved administrative method
- Logging
- Change control
- Review
- Prompt removal

Privileged access must be limited to the activities required by the engagement.

---

# 21. Remote Third-Party Access

Remote third-party access must be specifically authorized.

Remote access must not automatically include privileged administration.

Where remote access is permitted, it must use:

- Approved equipment where required
- Approved connectivity
- Appropriate authentication
- Limited access
- Appropriate logging

External remote privileged administration remains prohibited except through an approved exception under [[POL-003 Access Control Policy]].

---

# 22. Infinity Contractor Classification

The Infinity development contractor is classified as a Critical third party.

This classification reflects:

- Application dependency
- Source-code access
- Production support responsibilities
- Potential privileged access
- Knowledge concentration
- Business dependency
- Transferability requirements

The classification does not imply that the contractor currently controls ownership of Infinity assets.

---

# 23. Infinity Ownership

Dunder Mifflin must retain ownership and control of applicable:

- Infinity source code
- Source repositories
- Documentation
- Business data
- Intellectual property
- Work product
- Application backups
- Recovery information

Contractor engagement must not create unnecessary dependency through exclusive control of these assets.

---

# 24. Infinity Contractor Access

Infinity contractor access must:

- Have management sponsorship.
- Be time limited.
- Follow least privilege.
- Use individual identity where practical.
- Use smart-card multi-factor authentication where supported.
- Follow confidentiality requirements.
- Follow secure change requirements.
- Follow development and production separation requirements.

Expected contractor personnel names should be provided at least one business day before scheduled access when practical.

Emergency exceptions must be documented.

---

# 25. Infinity Privileged Production Work

Infinity contractors must not receive general remote VPN access for privileged production administration.

Privileged production or administrative work must occur from the designated controlled contractor workspace at Dunder Mifflin headquarters.

Work must follow applicable:

- Access-control requirements
- Change-control requirements
- Logging requirements
- Secure-development requirements

Detailed requirements are established in:

- [[PROC-004 Contractor Access Procedure]]
- [[PROC-020 Contractor Development Access Procedure]]
- [[STD-009 Infinity Security Standard]]

---

# 26. Third-Party Personnel

Third parties must ensure that personnel assigned to Dunder Mifflin work are appropriately qualified for their assigned responsibilities.

Dunder Mifflin may require:

- Named personnel
- Relevant experience
- Relevant qualifications
- Security instructions
- Confidentiality obligations
- Replacement notification

Requirements must remain proportionate to the engagement.

---

# 27. Personnel Changes

Third parties must notify the appropriate Dunder Mifflin contact when personnel with Dunder Mifflin access:

- Leave the provider
- Leave the engagement
- Change responsibilities
- No longer require access

Dunder Mifflin access must be reviewed and removed promptly when no longer required.

---

# 28. Subcontractors

Critical or Important third parties must not use subcontractors in a manner that materially changes Dunder Mifflin's security exposure without appropriate authorization or contractual allowance.

Where subcontractors receive sensitive access, appropriate security and confidentiality requirements must extend to them.

The primary provider remains responsible for obligations assigned through the agreement unless otherwise established contractually.

---

# 29. Security Incident Notification

Critical and Important third parties must notify Dunder Mifflin of a security incident affecting Dunder Mifflin systems, information, or material services within:

**24 hours of discovery**

Notification must not be unnecessarily delayed while the third party completes its internal investigation.

---

# 30. Initial Incident Notification

Initial notification should include available information concerning:

- Nature of incident
- Date or time discovered
- Systems affected
- Information affected
- Known or suspected Dunder Mifflin impact
- Containment actions
- Current status
- Provider incident contact

Incomplete information may be supplemented as the investigation develops.

---

# 31. Incident Cooperation

Third parties involved in an incident affecting Dunder Mifflin must provide reasonable cooperation.

Cooperation may include:

- Investigation support
- Relevant logs
- Technical information
- Timeline information
- Containment coordination
- Recovery coordination
- Corrective-action information
- Required evidence

Dunder Mifflin retains responsibility for its own incident-management decisions.

---

# 32. Dunder Mifflin Incident Response

Third-party incidents affecting Dunder Mifflin must be evaluated under:

- [[POL-009 Incident Response Policy]]
- [[PLN-001 Incident Response Plan]]
- [[PBK-005 Third-Party Incident Playbook]]

Third-party notification does not replace Dunder Mifflin's incident-classification and escalation process.

---

# 33. Critical Third-Party Review

Critical third parties must receive a security and dependency review at least annually.

The review should consider, as applicable:

- Service performance
- Security incidents
- Access
- Information handling
- Contract requirements
- Recovery capability
- Significant changes
- Assessment results
- Open findings
- Dependency
- Replacement considerations

---

# 34. Important Third-Party Review

Important third parties must receive a security and dependency review at least biennially.

More frequent review may be required following:

- Significant incidents
- Material service changes
- Significant findings
- Increased access
- Increased business dependency

---

# 35. Routine Third-Party Review

Routine third parties do not require the same scheduled security review as Critical or Important providers.

Routine relationships must still be reviewed when:

- Access materially changes.
- Information sensitivity increases.
- Business dependency increases.
- A significant incident occurs.
- Other circumstances indicate increased risk.

---

# 36. Review Evidence

Third-party reviews should produce sufficient evidence to demonstrate:

- Provider reviewed
- Reviewer
- Date
- Scope
- Significant evidence considered
- Findings
- Required actions
- Classification decision
- Follow-up requirements

A statement that a provider was reviewed without supporting evidence may be insufficient for assurance.

---

# 37. Audit and Assessment Rights

Contracts with Critical third parties should provide Dunder Mifflin with appropriate rights to obtain assurance concerning material security requirements.

Depending on the relationship, this may include rights to:

- Request security information
- Review relevant evidence
- Review independent assessment results
- Conduct or commission an assessment
- Investigate material incidents
- Validate corrective action

Audit rights must remain proportionate to the service and practical contractual relationship.

---

# 38. Independent Assessment Evidence

Dunder Mifflin may consider independent third-party assessment or audit reports when evaluating a provider.

Management must consider:

- Scope
- Date
- Independence
- Systems covered
- Exceptions
- Relevance to Dunder Mifflin
- Corrective actions

An external report must not automatically be treated as complete evidence for controls outside its scope.

---

# 39. Penetration Testing Providers

External penetration testers must:

- Have documented authorization.
- Have defined scope.
- Have confidentiality protection before sensitive access.
- Follow agreed testing rules.
- Protect test information.
- Report significant findings.
- Avoid unnecessary operational disruption.
- Return or destroy sensitive information as required.

Testing quality must be evaluated independently of the existence of a confidentiality agreement.

---

# 40. Incident Response Provider

Dunder Mifflin may maintain a retained Digital Forensics and Incident Response (DFIR) provider to support significant incidents.

The provider must have:

- Defined engagement terms
- Appropriate confidentiality obligations
- Defined contact and escalation methods
- Appropriate evidence-handling expectations
- Defined service responsibilities

Retention of a provider does not transfer Dunder Mifflin's incident-management responsibility.

---

# 41. Cloud Providers

Cloud providers supporting significant company systems must be evaluated for matters including:

- Availability
- Security
- Access
- Data protection
- Recovery
- Incident notification
- Service dependency
- Contract terms
- Exit capability

Cloud use must not create an assumption that the provider alone is responsible for Dunder Mifflin security.

---

# 42. Cloud Concentration Risk

Dunder Mifflin must consider concentration risk when multiple important services depend on the same cloud environment or provider.

Concentration may increase the business effect of:

- Provider outage
- Account compromise
- Provider security failure
- Contract dispute
- Service termination
- Regional failure

Concentration must inform continuity and risk decisions.

---

# 43. Cloud Exit and Migration

Critical cloud relationships must support reasonable exit and emergency migration planning.

Planning should consider:

- Data export
- Configuration information
- Application dependencies
- Authentication
- Backups
- Documentation
- Replacement services
- Contract termination
- Recovery requirements

Dunder Mifflin is not required to maintain a continuously operating alternate cloud environment unless business requirements justify one.

Detailed planning is maintained in [[PLN-004 Cloud Exit & Emergency Migration Plan]].

---

# 44. Continuity Requirements

Critical and Important third-party relationships must be evaluated for continuity implications.

Evaluation should consider:

- Service outage
- Provider failure
- Replacement time
- Manual alternatives
- Data availability
- Recovery dependencies
- Communication
- Alternate providers where practical

Continuity requirements must reflect [[RPT-002 Business Impact Analysis]].

---

# 45. Recovery Requirements

Where a third party supports recovery of a significant business service, Dunder Mifflin must understand:

- Provider responsibilities
- Dunder Mifflin responsibilities
- Recovery dependencies
- Required access
- Required information
- Escalation contacts
- Contractual commitments

Provider recovery claims must not automatically replace Dunder Mifflin's own recovery planning.

---

# 46. Supplier Continuity

Business suppliers must be evaluated according to the operational importance of the goods or services provided.

For significant suppliers, Operations should consider:

- Alternate suppliers
- Inventory levels
- Replacement lead time
- Transportation
- Geographic concentration
- Communication
- Contractual flexibility

Security review depth must remain proportionate to the supplier's actual access and dependency.

---

# 47. Service-Level Requirements

Critical or Important technology services should have service expectations appropriate to business need.

Requirements may address:

- Availability
- Support
- Escalation
- Incident notification
- Recovery
- Response time
- Maintenance

Service commitments must be evaluated against actual business requirements rather than selected arbitrarily.

---

# 48. Access Review

Third-party access must be included in applicable access reviews.

Reviews must determine whether:

- The relationship remains active.
- Access remains necessary.
- Access level remains appropriate.
- Accounts remain assigned to authorized individuals.
- Expiration dates remain appropriate.
- Privileged access remains justified.

Unnecessary access must be removed.

---

# 49. Third-Party Authentication

Third parties must use authentication appropriate to the sensitivity and privilege of their access.

Higher-risk access should receive stronger authentication where supported.

Authentication requirements are established in:

- [[POL-003 Access Control Policy]]
- [[STD-005 Authentication & Account Security Standard]]

---

# 50. Change Management

Third parties making changes to Dunder Mifflin systems must follow applicable change-management requirements.

Third parties must not make material production changes outside approved scope merely because they possess technical access.

Requirements are established in [[POL-007 Change & Configuration Management Policy]].

---

# 51. Security Testing

Third-party security testing must be:

- Authorized
- Scoped
- Coordinated
- Appropriately documented

Third parties must not conduct security testing outside authorized scope.

Requirements are established in [[STD-007 Penetration Testing Standard]] and applicable assessment procedures.

---

# 52. Information Return

At relationship termination, Dunder Mifflin information held by a third party must be returned when required.

Return requirements should consider:

- Business need
- Records requirements
- Contract requirements
- Migration
- Recovery
- Information format

Dunder Mifflin must verify that required business information remains accessible before provider access or services are terminated where practical.

---

# 53. Information Destruction

When a third party no longer requires Dunder Mifflin information and retention is not required, the information must be securely destroyed according to applicable requirements.

Dunder Mifflin may require confirmation of destruction for sensitive information.

Backup retention and technical limitations must be addressed contractually or through documented risk decisions where appropriate.

---

# 54. Relationship Termination

Third-party termination must address, as applicable:

- Account disablement
- Privileged access removal
- Remote access removal
- Physical-access removal
- Return of equipment
- Return of information
- Information destruction
- Credential changes
- Documentation transfer
- Source-code transfer
- Knowledge transfer
- Outstanding findings
- Contract obligations

Termination must be coordinated early enough to reduce unnecessary business disruption.

---

# 55. Provider Replacement

Critical third-party relationships must consider replacement difficulty.

Planning should consider:

- Alternative providers
- Required data
- Required documentation
- Configuration
- Credentials
- Technical knowledge
- Contract timing
- Migration effort
- Recovery requirements

Replacement planning should reduce unnecessary dependency on a single provider.

---

# 56. Knowledge Transfer

Third parties supporting important technology must provide documentation and knowledge sufficient for Dunder Mifflin to maintain reasonable operational control.

Critical knowledge must not reside exclusively with an external provider when doing so creates unacceptable dependency.

---

# 57. Infinity Transferability

Infinity must be reviewed at least annually for transferability.

The review must consider whether a qualified replacement development organization could reasonably assume support using Dunder Mifflin-controlled:

- Source code
- Repository
- Documentation
- Configuration information
- Deployment information
- Recovery information
- Business requirements

Results are documented in [[RPT-004 Infinity Transferability Review]].

---

# 58. Insurance and Risk Transfer

Insurance or contractual risk transfer may reduce specified financial consequences.

Risk transfer does not automatically eliminate:

- Operational disruption
- Security compromise
- Customer impact
- Recovery responsibility
- Management responsibility

Transferred risk must be evaluated according to [[POL-005 Risk Management Policy]].

---

# 59. Third-Party Findings

Material third-party deficiencies must be documented.

Examples include:

- Missing required confidentiality protection
- Excessive access
- Expired contractor access
- Missing security review
- Failure to provide required incident notification
- Material contract deficiency
- Recovery weakness
- Unresolved assessment finding
- Unauthorized subcontractor access
- Missing required documentation

Material corrective actions must be tracked in [[REG-004 Finding & Corrective Action Register]].

---

# 60. Third-Party Corrective Action

Corrective action involving a third party should identify:

- Deficiency
- Provider
- Responsible Dunder Mifflin owner
- Required remediation
- Due date
- Expected evidence
- Retest or validation requirement
- Risk if remediation fails

The Dunder Mifflin relationship owner remains responsible for ensuring required follow-up occurs.

---

# 61. Unresolved Third-Party Risk

When a third party cannot or will not correct a material deficiency, Dunder Mifflin must evaluate options including:

- Compensating controls
- Contract modification
- Reduced access
- Service modification
- Provider replacement
- Risk acceptance
- Relationship termination

Risk acceptance must follow [[POL-005 Risk Management Policy]].

---

# 62. Third-Party Metrics

Third-party metrics may include:

- Critical provider reviews completed
- Important provider reviews completed
- Overdue reviews
- Third-party incidents
- Third-party access exceptions
- Expired contractor accounts
- Open third-party findings
- Overdue third-party corrective actions
- Critical providers lacking required contract provisions
- Infinity transferability status

Metric definitions are maintained in [[REG-005 Security Metrics Catalog]].

---

# 63. Third-Party Risk Reassessment

Third-party risk must be reassessed when material conditions change.

Triggers may include:

- Significant security incident
- Material service change
- New sensitive access
- New privileged access
- Major contract change
- Acquisition or ownership change
- Significant financial or operational concern
- Repeated service failure
- Significant assessment finding
- Increased business dependency

Reassessment must use current information.

---

# 64. Assessment

Third-party security controls must be assessed according to risk.

Assessment may include:

- Contract inspection
- Access review
- Evidence review
- Interviews
- Sampling
- Observation
- Technical Testing
- Independent Performance
- Review of independent assessment reports

Assessment depth must reflect provider classification and risk.

---

# 65. Evidence

Third-party security evidence may include:

- Contracts
- Confidentiality agreements
- Due-diligence records
- Access approvals
- Account records
- Review records
- Assessment reports
- Incident notices
- Recovery information
- Corrective-action records
- Termination records
- Destruction confirmation

Evidence must accurately represent actual activity.

---

# 66. Evidence Integrity

Personnel must not:

- Manufacture third-party review records.
- Backdate due diligence.
- Represent an unsigned agreement as executed.
- Record a review that did not occur.
- Conceal known provider deficiencies.
- Represent planned corrective action as completed.

Known deficiencies must be documented and managed.

---

# 67. Risk Relationships

This policy provides significant treatment support for risks including:

- R006 Infinity Application Compromise
- R016 Cloud Service Outage
- R018 Third-Party Technology Failure / Compromise
- R019 Infinity Contractor Dependency / Compromise
- R020 Supplier Disruption
- R027 Backup / Recovery Failure
- R029 Vendor Fraud
- R035 Regional Disaster

Risk treatment is documented in [[RTP-001 Enterprise Risk Treatment Plan]].

---

# 68. Exceptions

Exceptions to this policy require:

- Documented business justification
- Affected requirement
- Associated risk
- Defined duration
- Compensating controls where practical
- Responsible owner
- Appropriate approval

High or Critical residual risk resulting from an exception requires escalation according to [[POL-005 Risk Management Policy]].

---

# 69. Enforcement

Failure by a third party to meet applicable security requirements may result in:

- Corrective-action requirements
- Access restriction
- Suspension of activity
- Contractual remedies
- Service modification
- Provider replacement
- Relationship termination
- Legal action where appropriate

Response must consider business impact and risk.

---

# 70. Policy Review

This policy must be reviewed:

- At least annually
- Following significant third-party incidents
- Following significant assessment findings
- Following material provider changes
- Following significant changes to third-party dependencies
- When third-party governance requirements materially change

The CIO coordinates review.

Material revisions require appropriate approval.

---

# 71. Traceability

Third-party security must support the enterprise security lifecycle:

**Business Dependency → Third Party → Risk Classification → Risk → Treatment → Contract/Policy Requirement → Control → Evidence → Assessment → Finding → Corrective Action → Retest → Risk Reassessment**

Detailed relationships are maintained through [[REG-007 Cross-Reference Matrix]].
