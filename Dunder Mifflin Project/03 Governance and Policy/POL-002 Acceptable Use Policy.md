# 1. Purpose

This policy establishes requirements for acceptable use of Dunder Mifflin information and technology resources.

Company resources are provided primarily to support authorized business activities.

Personnel must use these resources responsibly, securely, legally, and in a manner consistent with Dunder Mifflin policies and business objectives.

---

# 2. Scope

This policy applies to all individuals authorized to use Dunder Mifflin information or technology resources, including:

- Employees
- Executives
- Temporary personnel
- Contractors
- Consultants
- Other authorized users

This policy applies to company resources including:

- Desktop computers
- Laptops
- Servers
- Mobile devices
- Email
- Internet access
- Networks
- Applications
- Cloud-hosted services
- File services
- Removable media
- Printers
- Business records
- Company information
- Authentication credentials

This policy applies regardless of whether the resource is accessed from a Dunder Mifflin facility or an authorized remote location.

---

# 3. General Acceptable Use

Users must use Dunder Mifflin resources in a manner that:

- Supports legitimate business purposes.
- Protects company information.
- Protects customer, employee, supplier, and business information.
- Complies with applicable security requirements.
- Does not interfere with business operations.
- Does not create unreasonable security risk.
- Complies with applicable law and contractual obligations.
- Respects the rights of other users.

Users are responsible for activity performed using their individually assigned accounts and credentials.

---

# 4. Limited Personal Use

Limited personal use of company technology resources may be permitted when the use:

- Is occasional and reasonable.
- Does not interfere with work responsibilities.
- Does not create material cost.
- Does not interfere with system performance.
- Does not create security risk.
- Does not violate company policy.
- Does not involve prohibited activity.

Dunder Mifflin may restrict or discontinue personal use when necessary for business, security, legal, or operational reasons.

---

# 5. Prohibited Use

Company resources must not be used to:

- Conduct illegal activity.
- Commit fraud.
- Harass or threaten others.
- Intentionally access information without authorization.
- Circumvent security controls.
- Introduce malicious software.
- Conduct unauthorized security testing.
- Obtain unauthorized access to external systems.
- Disclose sensitive information without authorization.
- Install unauthorized software.
- Operate unauthorized network services.
- Connect unauthorized network equipment.
- Conceal prohibited activity.
- Interfere with company systems or networks.
- Intentionally damage or destroy company information.
- Conduct unauthorized commercial activity unrelated to Dunder Mifflin business.
- Violate intellectual-property rights.

Users must not use company systems to perform activities that could reasonably expose Dunder Mifflin to legal, security, operational, or reputational harm.

---

# 6. Authentication Credentials

Users must protect authentication credentials.

Users must not:

- Share individually assigned passwords.
- Disclose authentication secrets.
- Allow another person to use their individually assigned account.
- Record passwords in an insecure manner.
- Attempt to obtain another user's credentials.
- Use another person's credentials without specific authorization.

Users who believe credentials may have been compromised must report the situation promptly.

Emergency and specifically authorized shared credentials are governed by applicable access-control procedures.

---

# 7. Account Use

Users must access company resources through accounts authorized for their use.

Users must not:

- Use another person's account.
- Attempt to obtain permissions beyond those authorized.
- Continue using access after the business need ends.
- Attempt to circumvent account restrictions.
- Use privileged credentials for ordinary activity when a standard account is available.

Access requirements are established in [[POL-003 Access Control Policy]].

---

# 8. Privileged Accounts

Personnel with privileged access must exercise additional care.

Privileged accounts must be used only for authorized administrative activities requiring elevated permissions.

Administrators must use separate standard and privileged credentials where required.

Privileged access must not be used to:

- Access information without business need.
- Circumvent security controls for convenience.
- Conceal activity.
- Modify logs without authorization.
- Grant unauthorized access.
- Perform ordinary user activity when privileged access is unnecessary.

Detailed requirements are established in [[PROC-002 Privileged Access Procedure]].

---

# 9. Company Information

Users must protect company information according to its classification.

Users must:

- Access information only when authorized.
- Share information only with authorized recipients.
- Store information using approved resources.
- Protect information during transmission.
- Prevent unnecessary exposure.
- Dispose of information securely when required.

Detailed requirements are established in:

- [[POL-004 Information Classification & Handling Policy]]
- [[STD-001 Information Classification Standard]]

---

# 10. Email

Company email is provided for authorized business communication.

Users must exercise reasonable care when:

- Opening attachments
- Following links
- Responding to unexpected messages
- Sending sensitive information
- Responding to financial requests
- Responding to requests for credentials
- Communicating with unknown external parties

Users must report suspected phishing or suspicious messages.

Users must not use company email to:

- Distribute malicious software.
- Send unauthorized sensitive information.
- Conduct fraudulent activity.
- Impersonate another person without authorization.
- Send threatening or harassing material.
- Circumvent security controls.

---

# 11. Suspicious Email and Phishing

Users must treat unexpected requests involving the following with appropriate caution:

- Passwords
- Authentication information
- Financial transactions
- Banking information
- Sensitive information
- Software installation
- Unexpected attachments
- Unexpected links

Suspected phishing must be reported through established security-reporting channels.

Users should not knowingly forward suspected malicious content to other personnel except as directed for authorized security analysis.

---

# 12. Internet Use

Internet access is provided primarily for business purposes.

Users must not knowingly use company Internet access to:

- Access malicious resources.
- Download unauthorized software.
- Circumvent company security controls.
- Operate unauthorized peer-to-peer services.
- Conduct unauthorized security attacks or scanning.
- Access illegal material.
- Conduct activity prohibited by company policy.

Reasonable personal Internet use may be permitted under Section 4.

---

# 13. Software Installation

Users must not install unauthorized software on company systems.

Software must:

- Have a legitimate business purpose.
- Be appropriately licensed.
- Come from a trusted source.
- Be compatible with company security requirements.
- Receive required approval.

Users must not disable or remove company security software without authorization.

---

# 14. Security Tools

Security testing and administrative tools may only be used by authorized personnel for approved purposes.

Tools capable of:

- Vulnerability scanning
- Password recovery
- Packet capture
- Network discovery
- Exploitation
- Traffic manipulation
- Security-control testing

must not be used against company or third-party systems without appropriate authorization.

Possession of administrative or security knowledge does not independently authorize security testing.

---

# 15. Network Connections

Users must not connect unauthorized network devices to Dunder Mifflin networks.

Examples include:

- Wireless access points
- Routers
- Switches
- Network bridges
- Modems
- Unauthorized computers

Dunder Mifflin does not maintain a general corporate wireless network.

The headquarters lobby guest wireless network is Internet-only and must not be used as a bridge to internal company resources.

Unauthorized wireless infrastructure must be reported.

---

# 16. Remote Access

Remote access to nonpublic company resources must use approved methods.

Users must:

- Use authorized company equipment where required.
- Protect company equipment while traveling.
- Use approved protected connectivity when accessing nonpublic company cloud resources from untrusted networks.
- Prevent unauthorized individuals from viewing sensitive information.
- Secure devices when unattended.

Remote access does not authorize remote privileged administration.

---

# 17. Mobile Devices

Company-issued mobile devices must be used and protected according to company requirements.

Users must:

- Maintain required passcodes.
- Permit required company management.
- Report loss or theft promptly.
- Protect devices from unauthorized use.
- Avoid storing unnecessary sensitive information.
- Cooperate with remote lock or wipe when required.

Users must not intentionally disable required mobile-device security controls.

---

# 18. Personally Owned Devices

General Bring Your Own Device (BYOD) access to company systems is not authorized.

Users must not store or process company information on personally owned devices unless specifically authorized.

Personal devices may be used for emergency safety or personnel communications when directed by management.

Such emergency use does not create general authorization for company data processing on personal devices.

---

# 19. Removable Media

Removable media must be used only when there is a legitimate business need.

Users must:

- Protect sensitive information stored on removable media.
- Use company-approved media where required.
- Prevent unauthorized access.
- Report lost media containing company information.
- Securely dispose of media when required.

Unknown or untrusted removable media must not be connected to company systems.

---

# 20. Company Laptops

Company laptops must remain protected by required security controls.

Users must not:

- Disable full-disk encryption.
- Disable antivirus.
- Disable required screen locking.
- Remove required management software.
- Share the device with unauthorized individuals.
- Leave the device unnecessarily exposed to theft.

Lost or stolen laptops must be reported promptly.

---

# 21. Shared Warehouse Workstations

Shared Windows workstation access may be permitted where justified by warehouse operations.

Shared workstation use does not eliminate the requirement for individual accountability for significant business transactions.

Where required, significant Warehouse Management System (WMS) transactions must be authenticated or otherwise attributed to the individual performing the transaction.

Users must not intentionally perform transactions under another person's identity.

---

# 22. Physical Security

Users must comply with facility-security requirements.

Users must not:

- Permit unauthorized individuals into controlled areas.
- Share access cards or keys without authorization.
- Bypass physical-security controls.
- Leave Restricted information unnecessarily exposed.
- Allow unescorted visitors into areas requiring escort.

Lost access cards or keys must be reported promptly.

---

# 23. Visitors

Personnel sponsoring visitors are responsible for following applicable visitor requirements.

Visitors must not be provided access to company systems or sensitive information unless specifically authorized.

Visitor requirements are established in [[PROC-015 Visitor & Vendor Access Procedure]].

---

# 24. Clear Workspace and Information Exposure

Users handling Confidential or Restricted information must take reasonable steps to prevent unnecessary exposure.

This includes:

- Securing sensitive documents when unattended.
- Preventing unauthorized viewing of sensitive screens.
- Retrieving sensitive documents from printers promptly.
- Avoiding unnecessary discussion of sensitive information in public areas.
- Securely disposing of sensitive paper records.

Controls should remain proportionate to the information and environment.

---

# 25. Printing

Users must exercise care when printing sensitive information.

Sensitive printouts must:

- Be retrieved promptly.
- Be provided only to authorized recipients.
- Be stored appropriately.
- Be securely destroyed when no longer required.

Users should avoid unnecessary printing of sensitive information.

---

# 26. Cloud and External Services

Users must not place company information into unapproved external services when doing so could create material security, confidentiality, legal, or contractual risk.

External services used for company business must receive appropriate authorization.

Users must not bypass approved company systems solely for convenience when doing so would weaken required security controls.

---

# 27. Information Sharing

Company information may be shared externally only when:

- There is a legitimate business purpose.
- The recipient is authorized.
- Information classification permits the disclosure.
- Appropriate confidentiality protection exists when required.
- Applicable contractual or legal requirements are satisfied.

The method used to share information must be appropriate to its sensitivity.

---

# 28. Confidentiality

Users must maintain the confidentiality of information entrusted to them.

Confidentiality obligations may continue after employment or contractual relationships end when required by agreement, law, or the nature of the information.

Users must not use company information for unauthorized personal benefit.

---

# 29. Social Engineering

Users must not provide sensitive information, credentials, physical access, or system access solely because another person claims authority or urgency.

Unusual requests should be verified through an appropriate trusted method.

Personnel should report suspected social-engineering attempts.

---

# 30. Financial Requests

Personnel involved in financial activity must exercise additional caution with requests involving:

- Vendor banking changes
- Payroll banking changes
- Unusual payments
- Urgent transfers
- Refunds
- Credits
- Significant discounts
- Changes to payment instructions

Suspicious or significant changes must receive required authorization and independent verification where applicable.

---

# 31. Data Storage

Company information must be stored in approved locations appropriate to its classification and business use.

Users should avoid unnecessary local storage of sensitive information when approved centralized storage is available.

Users must not intentionally move company information to unapproved storage to bypass security controls.

---

# 32. Backup

Users must not assume that information stored outside approved company locations is backed up.

Important business information must be stored or maintained in a manner consistent with company backup and recovery requirements.

Users must not intentionally interfere with backup processes.

---

# 33. Monitoring and Privacy

Dunder Mifflin may monitor company systems and resources for legitimate purposes including:

- Security
- Operations
- Troubleshooting
- Compliance
- Incident investigation
- Fraud investigation
- Protection of company resources

Monitoring must be authorized and proportionate to legitimate business or security need.

Use of company resources should not create an expectation that business activity is completely private from authorized company review.

This policy does not authorize indiscriminate surveillance.

---

# 34. Security Logs

Users must not intentionally:

- Delete security logs without authorization.
- Modify logs to conceal activity.
- Disable required logging.
- Interfere with security monitoring.

Authorized log management performed as part of normal system administration is permitted.

---

# 35. Incident Reporting

Users must promptly report suspected security incidents.

Examples include:

- Phishing
- Malware
- Credential compromise
- Unauthorized access
- Lost or stolen devices
- Sensitive information exposure
- Suspicious system activity
- Physical-security incidents
- Unexpected security warnings
- Suspected fraud

Users should report suspicious events even when they are uncertain whether an incident has occurred.

---

# 36. Lost or Stolen Equipment

Loss or theft of company equipment must be reported promptly.

The report should include available information concerning:

- Device
- User
- Approximate time of loss
- Approximate location
- Information potentially stored on the device
- Circumstances of the loss

Personnel must cooperate with credential protection, remote lock, remote wipe, or other response activities where applicable.

---

# 37. Security-Control Bypass

Users must not disable, bypass, or intentionally interfere with security controls without authorization.

This includes:

- Antivirus
- Encryption
- Authentication
- Access restrictions
- Firewalls
- Screen locking
- Device management
- Logging
- Security configuration
- Physical controls

When a security control interferes with legitimate business activity, the issue must be reported for review rather than silently bypassed.

---

# 38. Unauthorized Testing

Users must not test the security of company or third-party systems without authorization.

Unauthorized testing includes:

- Port scanning
- Vulnerability scanning
- Password attacks
- Exploitation
- Packet interception
- Denial-of-service testing
- Attempts to bypass authentication
- Attempts to bypass access controls

Authorized security assessment is governed by applicable security-assessment requirements.

---

# 39. Intellectual Property

Users must respect:

- Dunder Mifflin intellectual property
- Customer intellectual property
- Supplier intellectual property
- Third-party copyrights
- Software licenses
- Contractual restrictions

Company resources must not be used to obtain, distribute, or use software or other material in violation of applicable rights.

---

# 40. Company Property

Company equipment must be used responsibly.

Users must take reasonable steps to prevent:

- Theft
- Loss
- Unauthorized use
- Unnecessary damage
- Exposure of company information

Company property must be returned when required.

---

# 41. Business Continuity

During a business disruption, personnel must follow authorized continuity instructions.

Temporary continuity procedures may include:

- Manual processing
- Company-issued cellular communications
- Alternate work locations
- Paper records
- Temporary operating procedures

Continuity conditions do not automatically suspend security requirements.

Where normal controls cannot operate, management must use reasonable compensating controls appropriate to the situation.

---

# 42. Emergency Situations

Protection of human life and safety takes priority over technology and property.

Personnel may take reasonable action necessary to protect themselves or others during an emergency.

Security or business-continuity requirements must not require personnel to remain in an unsafe location to protect company assets.

---

# 43. Third-Party Users

Contractors, consultants, and other third-party users must:

- Use company resources only for authorized purposes.
- Use only authorized accounts.
- Protect authentication credentials.
- Follow applicable information-handling requirements.
- Follow physical-security requirements.
- Report suspected security incidents.
- Comply with contractual security requirements.

Third-party access must end when the authorized business need ends.

---

# 44. Infinity Contractor Use

Infinity contractors must comply with applicable third-party, access-control, and secure-development requirements.

Privileged production or administrative work must occur through approved methods and locations.

General contractor VPN access does not independently authorize privileged production administration.

Detailed requirements are established in:

- [[POL-008 Third-Party Security Policy]]
- [[POL-012 Application & Secure Development Policy]]
- [[PROC-020 Contractor Development Access Procedure]]
- [[STD-009 Infinity Security Standard]]

---

# 45. Security Awareness

Users must complete required security-awareness training.

Training requirements are established in [[POL-006 Security Awareness & Training Policy]].

Users are expected to apply security awareness during normal business activity, particularly when handling:

- Email
- Authentication requests
- Sensitive information
- Financial requests
- External communications
- Unexpected software or files

---

# 46. Reporting Policy Problems

Personnel should report security requirements that are:

- Unclear
- Conflicting
- Impractical
- Outdated
- Creating unintended business risk

Reporting a policy concern does not authorize personnel to disregard the requirement.

Management must review legitimate conflicts and determine an appropriate resolution.

---

# 47. Exceptions

Exceptions to this policy require documented business justification and approval according to [[PRO-009 Security Governance Framework]].

Exception requests must identify:

- Requirement affected
- Business reason
- Associated risk
- Duration
- Compensating controls where practical
- Responsible owner

High or Critical residual risk resulting from an exception requires appropriate escalation.

---

# 48. Violations

Suspected violations may be investigated.

Confirmed violations may result in:

- Security corrective action
- Access restriction
- Management review
- Personnel action
- Contractual action
- Legal action where appropriate

Response must consider:

- Severity
- Intent
- Business impact
- Repetition
- Applicable personnel requirements
- Applicable law

---

# 49. Policy Review

This policy must be reviewed:

- At least annually
- Following material technology change
- Following material business change
- Following significant security incidents
- Following significant assessment findings
- When acceptable-use requirements materially change

The CIO coordinates review.

Material revisions require appropriate approval.
