# 1. Purpose

This policy establishes physical and environmental security requirements for Dunder Mifflin facilities, personnel, information, equipment, inventory, and supporting infrastructure.

Physical and environmental controls must:

- Protect human life and safety.
- Prevent unauthorized physical access.
- Protect sensitive information.
- Protect significant technology.
- Protect inventory.
- Reduce environmental threats.
- Support business continuity.
- Maintain appropriate accountability.
- Support investigation of physical-security events.

Controls must remain proportionate to business need, facility characteristics, and risk.

---

# 2. Scope

This policy applies to:

- Dunder Mifflin headquarters
- Dunder Mifflin branch offices
- Warehouses
- Office areas
- Restricted areas
- Information Technology equipment
- Network equipment
- Company records
- Inventory
- Backup media
- Company equipment
- Employees
- Contractors
- Visitors
- Vendors
- Delivery personnel
- Other persons entering company-controlled areas

Requirements apply within the limits of Dunder Mifflin's authority in leased facilities.

---

# 3. Objectives

Physical and environmental security must:

1. Protect life and safety.
2. Control access to nonpublic areas.
3. Protect Restricted areas.
4. Maintain visitor accountability.
5. Protect significant technology.
6. Protect sensitive information.
7. Protect higher-value inventory.
8. Control physical-access credentials.
9. Reduce fire, water, utility, and environmental risks.
10. Support continuity and recovery.
11. Support incident investigation.
12. Maintain evidence of significant physical-security controls.

---

# 4. Safety Priority

Human life and safety take priority over:

- Systems
- Facilities
- Equipment
- Information
- Inventory
- Revenue
- Other company assets

Personnel must follow emergency instructions and applicable life-safety procedures.

No employee is expected to physically confront an intruder, remain in an unsafe location, or place themselves at unreasonable personal risk solely to protect company property.

---

# 5. Facility Model

Dunder Mifflin operates seven leased facilities:

- Scranton Headquarters
- Scranton Branch
- Syracuse Branch
- Utica Branch
- Rochester Branch
- Nashua Branch
- Akron Branch

Each branch includes local office and warehouse operations.

Physical controls must reflect the actual layout, landlord responsibilities, shared-building conditions, and business functions of each facility.

---

# 6. Leased Facilities

Because Dunder Mifflin occupies leased facilities, some physical and environmental controls depend on property owners or building management.

Examples may include:

- Building exterior
- Common-area access
- Fire systems
- Elevators
- Shared utilities
- Common-area surveillance
- Building maintenance

Dunder Mifflin must understand significant landlord dependencies and manage controls within its own authority.

---

# 7. Security Zones

Facilities must use physical-security zones appropriate to their function.

Zones may include:

1. Public
2. Employee-Controlled
3. Restricted

Not every facility requires identical physical boundaries.

---

# 8. Public Areas

Public areas may include:

- Reception
- Customer-facing areas
- Approved meeting areas

Public access does not authorize access to:

- Employee workspaces
- Warehouses
- Information Technology areas
- Records storage
- Restricted areas

Movement beyond public areas requires appropriate authorization.

---

# 9. Employee-Controlled Areas

Employee-Controlled areas are company work areas not intended for unrestricted public access.

Examples may include:

- General offices
- Sales workspaces
- Administrative workspaces
- Warehouse operational areas

Access must be limited to:

- Authorized employees
- Authorized contractors
- Escorted visitors
- Other persons with legitimate business need

---

# 10. Restricted Areas

Restricted areas contain assets, systems, information, or inventory requiring heightened physical protection.

Examples may include:

- Significant Information Technology spaces
- Network equipment areas
- Sensitive records storage
- Backup-media storage
- Higher-value inventory cages
- Other designated sensitive spaces

Restricted access must be based on business need.

---

# 11. Restricted Area Designation

A space should be designated Restricted when unauthorized access could materially affect:

- Confidentiality
- Integrity
- Availability
- Business operations
- Recovery capability
- Inventory security

Restricted designation must reflect actual risk rather than convenience.

---

# 12. Restricted Area Access

Access to Restricted areas must:

- Have legitimate business need.
- Be appropriately authorized.
- Be limited to required personnel.
- Be reviewed periodically.
- Be removed when no longer required.

Visitors and service personnel requiring access must receive appropriate authorization and supervision.

---

# 13. Physical Access Methods

Dunder Mifflin may use:

- Reception control
- Employee access cards
- Keys
- Locked doors
- Security cages
- Visitor logs
- Escorts
- Other appropriate physical controls

Control selection must reflect facility design and risk.

---

# 14. Reception

Public entrances should be directed through a reception or other controlled point where practical.

Reception personnel support:

- Visitor identification
- Visitor contact
- Access direction
- Employee notification
- Reporting suspicious activity

Reception personnel are not expected to act as armed or physical security officers.

---

# 15. Employee Entrances

Nonpublic employee entrances must remain controlled.

The rear employee entrance uses card-controlled access where applicable.

Employees must not intentionally permit unauthorized persons to bypass access controls.

---

# 16. Access Cards

Access cards must:

- Be assigned according to business need.
- Remain associated with authorized personnel.
- Be protected from unauthorized use.
- Be reported promptly when lost or stolen.
- Be disabled when no longer required.

Employees must not loan individually assigned access cards to unauthorized persons.

---

# 17. Keys

Physical keys providing access to company-controlled or Restricted areas must be managed according to business need.

Key management should include:

- Authorized issuance
- Reasonable accountability
- Return when no longer required
- Response to lost keys
- Replacement or rekeying when risk justifies it

Not every ordinary office key requires an elaborate tracking system.

---

# 18. Physical Access Review

Access to significant Restricted areas must be reviewed at least annually.

Review must determine whether:

- The individual remains authorized.
- Business need remains valid.
- Access remains appropriate.
- Unnecessary access should be removed.

Higher-risk access may be reviewed more frequently when appropriate.

---

# 19. Transfers

When personnel change roles, physical access must be reviewed.

Access associated with the prior role must be removed when no longer justified.

New physical access must be authorized according to the new role.

A transfer must not result in indefinite accumulation of unnecessary physical access.

---

# 20. Terminations

When employment or an applicable contractor relationship ends, physical-access credentials must be recovered or disabled promptly.

Actions may include:

- Disable access card
- Recover keys
- Recover badges
- Recover company equipment
- Remove Restricted-area authorization
- Notify applicable facility personnel

Termination must be coordinated with [[POL-003 Access Control Policy]] and applicable personnel procedures.

---

# 21. Lost Access Credentials

Lost or stolen:

- Access cards
- Keys
- Badges
- Other physical credentials

must be reported promptly.

The responsible manager or facility personnel must evaluate whether:

- Credential disablement is required.
- Locks require replacement.
- Access records require review.
- Additional investigation is necessary.

Response must reflect the risk associated with the credential.

---

# 22. Visitors

Visitors entering nonpublic company areas must:

- Have legitimate business purpose.
- Be associated with an appropriate employee or business contact.
- Follow applicable visitor procedures.
- Remain within authorized areas.

Visitor controls must be proportionate to the area entered.

---

# 23. Visitor Identification

Visitors requiring access beyond public areas should be identified through an appropriate visitor process.

The process may include:

- Visitor name
- Organization
- Host
- Purpose
- Arrival time
- Departure time

Identification requirements may vary according to facility and access.

---

# 24. Visitor Logs

Visitor logs must be maintained where required by facility procedures.

Logs should provide sufficient information to determine:

- Who entered
- Whom they visited
- When they entered
- When they departed where practical

Visitor records must be protected according to their sensitivity.

---

# 25. Visitor Escort

Visitors entering nonpublic or Restricted areas must be escorted when their access does not justify unescorted entry.

The escort must:

- Remain aware of the visitor's location.
- Limit the visitor to authorized areas.
- Prevent unnecessary access to sensitive information or equipment.

Escort requirements must remain proportionate to risk.

---

# 26. Vendors and Service Personnel

Vendors and service personnel must receive access appropriate to the work being performed.

Access must be limited by:

- Area
- Purpose
- Duration
- Information sensitivity

Service personnel must not receive unnecessary access merely because they perform work within a Dunder Mifflin facility.

---

# 27. Sensitive Vendor Access

Outside personnel requiring access to sensitive information or systems must satisfy applicable:

- Authorization
- Confidentiality
- Access-control
- Third-party security

requirements before access is provided.

Requirements are established in [[POL-008 Third-Party Security Policy]].

---

# 28. Ordinary Service Personnel

Ordinary service personnel who do not require sensitive access do not automatically require a confidentiality agreement.

Examples may include:

- Delivery personnel
- Ordinary building maintenance
- Vendor sales representatives
- Other routine visitors

If access changes to include sensitive information, applicable confidentiality requirements must be satisfied before such access is provided.

---

# 29. Warehouse Access

Warehouse areas must be limited to:

- Authorized personnel
- Authorized delivery personnel
- Authorized vendors
- Escorted visitors
- Other persons with legitimate operational need

Warehouse access must support both operational efficiency and inventory security.

---

# 30. Warehouse Entry

Warehouse entry points not intended for unrestricted public access must remain controlled.

Where a warehouse uses a bell or similar notification method for delivery or service personnel, the individual must wait for authorized personnel before entering nonpublic warehouse areas unless otherwise authorized.

---

# 31. Higher-Value Inventory

Each warehouse must maintain a locked security cage or enclosure for higher-value or theft-attractive inventory.

The cage must:

- Remain locked when not actively accessed.
- Be limited to authorized personnel.
- Support inventory accountability.
- Be included in physical-security review.

Operations is responsible for management of warehouse security cages.

---

# 32. Inventory Cage Access

Access to higher-value inventory cages must be based on operational need.

Access must not be granted solely because an employee works in the warehouse.

Unnecessary access must be removed.

---

# 33. Inventory Accountability

Physical security must support inventory accountability through coordination with:

- Warehouse Management System records
- Receiving records
- Shipping records
- Inventory counts
- Reconciliation
- Significant transaction attribution

Material inventory theft should be difficult to accomplish without bypassing physical controls or creating discrepancies in inventory, receiving, shipping, or transaction records.

---

# 34. Information Technology Areas

Significant Information Technology and network equipment must be located in Restricted areas where practical.

Access must be limited to:

- Authorized Information Technology personnel
- Authorized contractors
- Other personnel with documented business need

General employee access is not sufficient justification for access to significant technology spaces.

---

# 35. Network Equipment

Network equipment must be physically protected from unauthorized access.

Protection may include:

- Locked rooms
- Locked cabinets
- Controlled office spaces
- Other appropriate barriers

Protection must reflect the equipment's importance and facility layout.

---

# 36. Server and Infrastructure Protection

Locally maintained servers and infrastructure must receive physical protection appropriate to:

- Business importance
- Information sensitivity
- Administrative capability
- Recovery dependency

Cloud-hosted infrastructure remains subject to provider physical-security controls under the applicable service relationship.

---

# 37. Backup Media

Physical backup media must be protected against:

- Unauthorized access
- Theft
- Loss
- Damage
- Environmental exposure

Backup media must remain encrypted and handled according to [[POL-010 Business Continuity & Disaster Recovery Policy]].

---

# 38. Off-Site Backup Storage

The approved off-site disaster-recovery storage provider must protect stored backup media according to applicable third-party and information-protection requirements.

Dunder Mifflin must maintain sufficient accountability to support its backup rotation and recovery processes.

---

# 39. Sensitive Paper Records

Confidential or Restricted paper records must be physically protected.

Protection may include:

- Locked offices
- Locked cabinets
- Restricted rooms
- Controlled records storage

Sensitive paper must not be left unnecessarily accessible in public or uncontrolled areas.

---

# 40. Printing

Personnel printing sensitive information must:

- Retrieve it promptly.
- Avoid unnecessary unattended output.
- Protect it according to classification.
- Dispose of it securely when no longer required.

Shared printers must not be treated as secure storage locations.

---

# 41. Secure Disposal

Sensitive paper records must be destroyed using an approved secure disposal method when destruction is authorized.

Secure disposal may include:

- Cross-cut shredding
- Approved secure destruction service

Sensitive documents must not be placed intact into ordinary trash when secure destruction is required.

---

# 42. Clear Workspace Practices

Personnel handling sensitive information must take reasonable steps to prevent unnecessary exposure.

Examples include:

- Securing sensitive documents when unattended
- Locking workstations
- Avoiding unnecessary display of sensitive information
- Removing sensitive information from meeting spaces

Requirements must remain proportionate to the workspace and information involved.

---

# 43. Equipment Protection

Company equipment must receive reasonable physical protection against:

- Theft
- Unauthorized use
- Accidental damage
- Environmental damage

Protection requirements apply both inside and outside company facilities.

---

# 44. Portable Equipment

Portable equipment such as laptops and company mobile phones must not be left unnecessarily exposed to theft or unauthorized access.

Personnel must use reasonable care when:

- Traveling
- Working remotely
- Using vehicles
- Staying in hotels
- Working in public locations

Loss or theft must be reported promptly.

---

# 45. Equipment in Vehicles

Sensitive information and valuable company equipment should not be left unattended in vehicles unnecessarily.

When temporary vehicle storage is unavoidable, equipment should be kept out of obvious view where practical.

Vehicle storage must not be treated as long-term secure storage.

---

# 46. Fire Protection

Dunder Mifflin facilities must rely on applicable:

- Building fire-detection systems
- Fire-suppression systems
- Fire extinguishers
- Evacuation procedures
- Landlord fire-safety responsibilities
- Local fire and building requirements

Dunder Mifflin must cooperate with landlord and emergency authorities concerning fire safety.

---

# 47. Fire Response

During a fire or suspected fire:

1. Human safety takes priority.
2. Personnel must follow evacuation and emergency procedures.
3. Personnel must not delay evacuation to save equipment, information, or inventory.
4. Business recovery begins only when the area is safe for authorized access.

Technology recovery must rely on off-site and geographically separated capabilities where necessary.

---

# 48. Fire Risk Reduction

Reasonable fire-risk reduction should include:

- Maintaining clear access to required fire equipment
- Avoiding unsafe electrical practices
- Following building requirements
- Reporting known fire hazards
- Protecting significant records and backups through geographic separation

Physical protection must complement, not replace, off-site recovery capability.

---

# 49. Water Damage

Facilities must take reasonable steps to reduce water-damage risk.

Measures may include:

- Avoiding unnecessary placement of critical equipment beneath known water hazards
- Elevating important equipment or records where practical
- Reporting leaks promptly
- Coordinating repairs with the landlord
- Maintaining geographically separated backups
- Protecting important paper records

---

# 50. Water Incident Response

When water threatens personnel or equipment:

1. Safety takes priority.
2. Personnel must avoid electrical hazards.
3. Appropriate facility or emergency personnel must be notified.
4. Equipment must be moved or shut down only when safe.
5. Business continuity or recovery procedures must be activated when required.

---

# 51. Utility Disruption

Facilities must maintain reasonable plans for disruption of:

- Electrical power
- Heating
- Cooling
- Other significant utilities

Utility disruption must be evaluated according to:

- Expected duration
- Safety
- Business impact
- Equipment impact
- Available alternate operations

---

# 52. Uninterruptible Power Supply

Critical network equipment and selected management and Sales workstations use Uninterruptible Power Supply (UPS) capability providing approximately 30 minutes of temporary power.

UPS capability is intended to support:

- Short-duration interruption
- Orderly shutdown
- Limited continuity

UPS capability must not be treated as long-duration emergency generation.

---

# 53. Extended Power Failure

During extended power failure, personnel must:

- Protect safety.
- Perform orderly shutdown where practical.
- Use approved continuity methods.
- Follow facility instructions.
- Relocate or suspend operations when required.

Company-issued laptops and mobile communications may support selected business activity where cellular service remains available.

---

# 54. Generators

Dunder Mifflin does not require dedicated generators as a general facility control.

Generator capability or other long-duration power solutions should be considered when justified by:

- Business Impact Analysis
- Risk
- Outage history
- Facility requirements
- Cost

Physical-security requirements must reflect actual business need.

---

# 55. Heating and Cooling

Significant technology must operate within environmental conditions appropriate to the equipment.

Heating or cooling failure affecting:

- Safety
- Equipment
- Business operations

must be reported promptly.

Equipment should be shut down when continued operation creates unacceptable risk.

---

# 56. Environmental Monitoring

Environmental monitoring must be proportionate to facility and equipment risk.

Not every office or warehouse requires dedicated electronic environmental sensors.

Higher-risk technology spaces may require additional monitoring when justified.

---

# 57. Building Maintenance

Facility defects affecting security or environmental protection must be reported to:

- Appropriate management
- Building management
- Landlord
- Other responsible party

Examples include:

- Broken locks
- Door failures
- Water leaks
- Lighting failures
- Fire-system concerns
- HVAC failures

Material unresolved conditions must be tracked and escalated.

---

# 58. Common-Area CCTV

Where landlords provide Closed-Circuit Television (CCTV) coverage in building common areas, Dunder Mifflin may use available information in support of legitimate security investigations where permitted.

Landlord CCTV must not be treated as equivalent to company-controlled surveillance.

---

# 59. Internal CCTV

Dunder Mifflin does not maintain general internal CCTV surveillance as a baseline control.

Installation of internal surveillance must require:

- Documented business or security need
- Appropriate management approval
- Privacy consideration
- Legal review where appropriate
- Defined purpose

Cameras must not be installed merely because surveillance technology is available.

---

# 60. Surveillance Privacy

Physical-security monitoring must remain proportionate to legitimate security and business needs.

Monitoring must not become indiscriminate employee surveillance.

Access to surveillance information must be limited to authorized purposes.

---

# 61. Guest Wireless Access Point

The Scranton Headquarters lobby contains one ceiling-mounted wireless access point providing Internet-only guest Wi-Fi.

The guest wireless service must:

- Remain separated from internal corporate access.
- Not provide access to internal business systems.
- Be limited to its approved guest function.
- Remain subject to network-security requirements.

Dunder Mifflin does not operate general corporate wireless networking.

---

# 62. Unauthorized Network Devices

Personnel must not install unauthorized:

- Wireless access points
- Routers
- Switches
- Modems
- Other network devices

Physical inspections and network discovery may be used to identify unauthorized devices.

Network requirements are established in [[STD-002 Network Security Standard]].

---

# 63. Delivery and Receiving Areas

Delivery and receiving activity must be controlled to prevent unnecessary access to:

- Offices
- Restricted areas
- Sensitive information
- Higher-value inventory

Delivery personnel must remain within areas necessary to perform their business purpose unless additional access is authorized.

---

# 64. Shipping and Receiving Records

Shipping and receiving processes must maintain records sufficient to support:

- Inventory accountability
- Investigation
- Reconciliation
- Fraud detection
- Theft investigation

Physical records and system records must support reasonable reconstruction of significant inventory movement.

---

# 65. Vehicles

Company vehicles or vehicles used for company business must be operated by authorized and appropriately qualified personnel.

Vehicle security must consider:

- Equipment
- Information
- Inventory
- Delivery records
- Keys
- Loss or theft

Vehicle-related requirements must support continuity and liability management.

---

# 66. Vehicle Incidents

Vehicle accidents or transportation incidents involving company:

- Personnel
- Information
- Equipment
- Inventory

must be reported through appropriate management channels.

Safety and emergency response take priority.

Security incidents arising from the event must also be reported under [[POL-009 Incident Response Policy]].

---

# 67. After-Hours Access

After-hours facility access must be limited to personnel with legitimate business need.

After-hours access does not independently require special authorization when normal access rights appropriately include such access.

Unusual or suspicious after-hours activity may be investigated.

---

# 68. Contractors

Contractors requiring physical access must:

- Have a legitimate business purpose.
- Have an appropriate sponsor.
- Remain within authorized areas.
- Follow applicable visitor or contractor procedures.
- Comply with Restricted-area requirements.

Physical access does not automatically authorize system access.

---

# 69. Infinity Contractors

Infinity contractor personnel performing privileged production or administrative work must use the designated controlled contractor workspace at headquarters.

Expected contractor personnel names should be provided at least one business day before scheduled access when practical.

Emergency exceptions must be documented.

Applicable requirements are established in:

- [[POL-003 Access Control Policy]]
- [[POL-008 Third-Party Security Policy]]
- [[PROC-020 Contractor Development Access Procedure]]

---

# 70. Penetration Testers and Security Specialists

Outside security specialists requiring physical access must:

- Have documented authorization.
- Have an appropriate company contact.
- Remain within authorized scope.
- Follow applicable Restricted-area controls.
- Have confidentiality protection before sensitive access.

Physical presence does not expand authorized technical testing scope.

---

# 71. Physical Security Events

Physical-security events must be reported when they may involve:

- Unauthorized entry
- Lost access credentials
- Restricted-area access
- Equipment theft
- Information theft
- Inventory theft
- Suspicious visitors
- Tampering
- Environmental damage

Security events must be evaluated according to [[POL-009 Incident Response Policy]] when appropriate.

---

# 72. Physical Evidence

Potential physical evidence from significant incidents must be preserved when practical and appropriate.

Evidence may include:

- Access records
- Visitor logs
- Keys
- Access cards
- Documents
- Equipment
- Landlord surveillance records
- Shipping records
- Receiving records

Evidence handling must follow [[PROC-011 Evidence Handling Procedure]] when formal preservation is required.

---

# 73. Physical Security Documentation

Physical-security documentation may include:

- Access authorizations
- Key records
- Card-access records
- Visitor logs
- Restricted-area lists
- Incident records
- Inspection records
- Landlord communications

Documentation depth must remain proportionate to risk.

---

# 74. Emergency Access

Emergency personnel must be permitted access necessary to protect life and safety.

Emergency access must not be delayed solely because normal visitor or Restricted-area procedures cannot be completed.

Company personnel should support emergency responders as directed and safe.

---

# 75. Evacuation

Facilities must maintain or follow applicable emergency evacuation procedures.

Personnel must:

- Follow emergency instructions.
- Use designated exits where appropriate.
- Avoid reentering unsafe facilities.
- Support personnel accountability where required.

Evacuation procedures are life-safety controls and take precedence over normal security restrictions.

---

# 76. Personnel Accountability During Emergencies

During significant facility emergencies, management must account for affected personnel where practical.

Personnel accountability supports:

- Life safety
- Emergency response
- Continuity decisions

It must not unnecessarily delay evacuation.

---

# 77. Facility Loss

Loss of a facility must be managed according to [[POL-010 Business Continuity & Disaster Recovery Policy]].

Physical-security planning must support recovery through:

- Off-site backups
- Cloud-hosted systems
- Alternate communications
- Alternate work arrangements
- Geographic distribution
- Recovery documentation

No individual facility should be assumed to be immune from loss.

---

# 78. Physical Security Review

Physical-security controls must be periodically reviewed.

Review should consider:

- Facility changes
- Access
- Restricted areas
- Keys
- Access cards
- Visitor controls
- Inventory protection
- Technology spaces
- Environmental conditions
- Significant incidents
- Open findings

Reviews must reflect actual facility conditions.

---

# 79. Facility Inspection

Facilities should receive periodic physical-security inspection appropriate to risk.

Inspection may include:

- Doors
- Locks
- Restricted areas
- Network equipment protection
- Inventory cages
- Sensitive records
- Visitor processes
- Environmental hazards
- Unauthorized network devices

Identified deficiencies must be documented where material.

---

# 80. Assessment

Physical and environmental controls must be periodically assessed.

Assessment may include:

- Inspection
- Observation
- Interviews
- Sampling
- Independent Performance
- Access-record review
- Visitor-record review
- Physical walkthrough
- Review of incidents

Assessment must evaluate actual control operation rather than written procedures alone.

---

# 81. Evidence

Physical and environmental security evidence may include:

- Access lists
- Access-card records
- Key records
- Visitor logs
- Restricted-area authorizations
- Facility inspection records
- Incident records
- Inventory reconciliation
- Landlord communications
- Environmental maintenance records
- Corrective-action records

Evidence must accurately represent actual activity.

---

# 82. Evidence Integrity

Personnel must not:

- Manufacture visitor records.
- Backdate access approvals.
- Conceal lost credentials.
- Record inspections that did not occur.
- Falsify physical-access reviews.
- Conceal material physical-security deficiencies.

Known deficiencies must be documented and addressed.

---

# 83. Findings

Material physical or environmental security deficiencies must be documented.

Examples include:

- Uncontrolled Restricted-area access
- Unreturned access credentials
- Broken locks
- Missing visitor records
- Unauthorized physical access
- Unsecured sensitive records
- Unsecured higher-value inventory
- Unprotected significant network equipment
- Repeated environmental hazards
- Material unresolved landlord security issues

Material corrective actions must be tracked in [[REG-004 Finding & Corrective Action Register]].

---

# 84. Corrective Action

Corrective action must identify, as appropriate:

- Finding
- Affected facility
- Affected control
- Associated risk
- Responsible party
- Required remediation
- Due date
- Expected evidence
- Validation requirement

Responsibility must reflect whether corrective action belongs to:

- Dunder Mifflin
- Landlord
- Third party
- Multiple parties

Dunder Mifflin remains responsible for managing its risk even when another party performs the corrective work.

---

# 85. Landlord-Controlled Deficiencies

When a physical or environmental deficiency is controlled by the landlord, Dunder Mifflin must:

- Report the issue.
- Document material risk.
- Request appropriate corrective action.
- Track significant unresolved issues.
- Consider compensating controls where practical.
- Escalate unacceptable risk.

Leasing a facility does not eliminate Dunder Mifflin's responsibility to understand the resulting risk.

---

# 86. Metrics

Physical and environmental security metrics may include:

- Restricted-area reviews completed
- Unreturned physical credentials
- Lost access cards
- Physical-security incidents
- Unauthorized-access events
- Visitor-control exceptions
- Open facility-security findings
- Overdue corrective actions
- Significant environmental incidents
- Warehouse inventory discrepancies requiring investigation

Metric definitions are maintained in [[REG-005 Security Metrics Catalog]].

---

# 87. Risk Relationships

This policy provides significant treatment support for risks including:

- R009 Sensitive Information Theft / Extortion
- R010 Unauthorized / Excessive Access
- R012 Malicious Insider
- R014 Lost or Stolen Device
- R021 Fire
- R022 Water Damage
- R023 Utility / Power / HVAC Failure
- R024 Unauthorized Physical Access
- R025 Vehicle / Transportation Incident
- R027 Backup / Recovery Failure
- R032 Inventory Theft
- R034 Loss of Headquarters
- R035 Regional Disaster

Risk treatment is documented in [[RTP-001 Enterprise Risk Treatment Plan]].

---

# 88. Exceptions

Exceptions to this policy require:

- Documented business justification
- Affected requirement
- Associated risk
- Defined duration
- Compensating controls where practical
- Responsible owner
- Appropriate approval

High or Critical residual risk resulting from an exception requires escalation according to [[POL-005 Risk Management Policy]].

Emergency action necessary to protect life and safety does not require advance exception approval.

---

# 89. Enforcement

Failure to comply with this policy may result in:

- Access restriction
- Physical-access removal
- Corrective action
- Management review
- Personnel action
- Contractual action
- Legal action where appropriate

Intentional bypass of physical-security controls must be escalated according to risk.

---

# 90. Policy Review

This policy must be reviewed:

- At least annually
- Following significant facility changes
- Following material physical-security incidents
- Following significant environmental incidents
- Following material assessment findings
- When physical-security requirements materially change

The COO coordinates review with the CIO and applicable facility or operational management.

Material revisions require CEO approval.

---

# 91. Traceability

Physical and environmental security must support the enterprise security lifecycle:

**Asset / Facility → Threat → Risk → Treatment → Physical or Environmental Requirement → Control → Procedure → Implementation → Evidence → Assessment → Finding → Corrective Action → Validation → Risk Reassessment**

Detailed relationships are maintained through [[REG-007 Cross-Reference Matrix]].
