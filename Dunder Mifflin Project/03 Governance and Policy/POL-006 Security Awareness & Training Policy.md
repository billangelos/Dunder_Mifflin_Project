# 1. Purpose

This policy establishes Dunder Mifflin's requirements for security awareness and training.

Security awareness and training must provide personnel with the knowledge necessary to:

- Understand their security responsibilities.
- Recognize common threats.
- Protect company information.
- Protect authentication credentials.
- Use company resources appropriately.
- Recognize suspicious activity.
- Report suspected security incidents.
- Respond appropriately to social-engineering attempts.
- Support Dunder Mifflin's security policies and controls.

Training must address both general workforce responsibilities and additional responsibilities associated with higher-risk roles.

---

# 2. Scope

This policy applies to:

- Employees
- Executives
- Temporary personnel
- Contractors
- Consultants
- Other individuals receiving authorized access to Dunder Mifflin systems or sensitive information

Training requirements may vary according to:

- Role
- Access
- Information sensitivity
- Privilege
- Business responsibility
- Risk

---

# 3. Objectives

The security-awareness and training program must:

1. Establish baseline security knowledge.
2. Reinforce individual security responsibilities.
3. Reduce susceptibility to phishing and social engineering.
4. Improve incident reporting.
5. Protect authentication credentials.
6. Improve information-handling behavior.
7. Support fraud prevention.
8. Provide role-specific security knowledge.
9. Measure personnel understanding.
10. Identify areas requiring additional training.
11. Maintain evidence of training completion.
12. Support continuous improvement.

---

# 4. Responsibilities

## 4.1 CIO

The CIO provides executive oversight of the security-awareness and training program.

---

## 4.2 IT & Information Security Manager

The IT & Information Security Manager coordinates the security-awareness program.

Responsibilities include:

- Developing or selecting training content
- Coordinating training delivery
- Maintaining program requirements
- Coordinating phishing simulations
- Reviewing training metrics
- Identifying improvement needs
- Reporting material issues to management

---

## 4.3 Human Resources

Human Resources supports:

- Identification of new personnel
- Training coordination
- Completion tracking
- Escalation of overdue training
- Personnel-related corrective action where appropriate

Human Resources and Information Technology must coordinate to ensure new personnel receive required security training.

---

## 4.4 Managers

Managers must:

- Support employee participation.
- Ensure personnel are available for required training.
- Address overdue training.
- Reinforce applicable security responsibilities.
- Identify roles requiring specialized training.

---

## 4.5 Personnel

Personnel must:

- Complete required training.
- Participate in required security exercises.
- Apply security requirements during normal work.
- Report suspected security incidents.
- Ask for guidance when security requirements are unclear.

Completion of training does not remove the individual's responsibility to follow security requirements.

---

# 5. Initial Security Awareness Training

New personnel requiring access to company systems or information must complete initial security-awareness training within five business days of receiving access.

Where practical, essential security instructions should be provided before or at the time access is issued.

Initial training must establish the user's fundamental security responsibilities.

---

# 6. Initial Training Content

Initial training must address, as applicable:

- Information-security responsibilities
- Acceptable use
- Authentication security
- Password protection
- Phishing
- Social engineering
- Malware
- Information classification
- Sensitive information handling
- Email security
- Internet use
- Mobile-device security
- Physical security
- Incident reporting
- Lost or stolen equipment
- Fraud awareness
- Remote-access responsibilities
- Policy violations

Training should explain how personnel can report suspected security events.

---

# 7. Annual Refresher Training

Personnel subject to this policy must complete security-awareness refresher training at least annually.

Annual training must reinforce important security responsibilities and address relevant changes in:

- Threats
- Company policies
- Business practices
- Technology
- Incident experience
- Assessment findings

Annual training should not merely repeat unchanged material when meaningful new lessons are available.

---

# 8. Annual Knowledge Assessment

Personnel must complete an annual security-awareness knowledge assessment.

The minimum passing score is:

**80 percent**

The assessment should evaluate whether personnel understand important security responsibilities rather than merely whether they attended training.

---

# 9. Assessment Failure

Personnel who do not achieve the minimum passing score must receive appropriate remediation.

Remediation may include:

- Review of missed material
- Additional training
- Manager coaching
- Reassessment

Failure of the initial assessment is not automatically a disciplinary matter.

Repeated failure or refusal to complete required remediation may require management or Human Resources review.

---

# 10. Training Completion

Required training is not complete solely because training material was assigned.

Completion requires the individual to:

- Complete required training content.
- Complete required assessment.
- Achieve the required passing score.

Applicable remediation must be completed when required.

---

# 11. Phishing Simulations

Dunder Mifflin must conduct phishing simulations monthly.

Simulations are intended to:

- Reinforce awareness.
- Measure susceptibility.
- Evaluate reporting behavior.
- Identify recurring weaknesses.
- Improve training.
- Support evaluation of phishing-related risk.

Simulations must be authorized and coordinated through the security-awareness program.

---

# 12. Phishing Simulation Design

Phishing simulations should reflect realistic business scenarios.

Examples may include:

- Password-expiration messages
- Unexpected attachments
- Delivery notifications
- Invoice messages
- Account-verification requests
- Internal-looking requests
- Financial requests
- Urgent management requests

Simulations must not unnecessarily expose sensitive information or create unreasonable operational disruption.

---

# 13. Phishing Simulation Measurement

The program should measure relevant results including:

- Messages delivered
- Links followed
- Attachments opened where applicable
- Credentials attempted where applicable
- Messages reported
- Reporting time
- Repeat susceptibility

Metrics should be used to identify patterns and improve the program.

---

# 14. Phishing Simulation Remediation

Personnel who interact with simulated phishing content may receive:

- Immediate educational feedback
- Targeted awareness material
- Additional training
- Manager coaching where appropriate

Repeated susceptibility may require additional remediation.

The primary purpose of simulation is risk reduction and learning rather than punishment.

---

# 15. Phishing Reporting

Personnel must be taught how to report suspected phishing.

Training must reinforce that:

- Suspicious messages should be reported promptly.
- Personnel should report even when uncertain.
- Reporting suspected phishing is preferable to ignoring a potentially malicious message.
- Suspected malicious content should not be unnecessarily forwarded to other personnel.

Reporting behavior should be included in program measurement.

---

# 16. Social Engineering

Training must address social-engineering techniques involving:

- Email
- Telephone calls
- In-person interaction
- Impersonation
- Urgency
- Authority claims
- Financial requests
- Requests for credentials
- Requests for sensitive information
- Requests for physical access

Personnel must understand that claimed authority does not eliminate the need for appropriate verification.

---

# 17. Credential Security

Training must reinforce that personnel must not:

- Share individually assigned passwords.
- Disclose authentication secrets.
- Approve unauthorized access.
- Provide credentials in response to suspicious requests.
- Permit another individual to use their account.

Suspected credential compromise must be reported promptly.

---

# 18. Business Email Compromise

Personnel involved in financial or sensitive business processes must receive awareness concerning Business Email Compromise (BEC).

Training should address:

- Executive impersonation
- Vendor impersonation
- Changed payment instructions
- Urgent transfer requests
- Payroll banking changes
- Unusual secrecy requests
- Requests to bypass normal approval
- Compromised business email accounts

Personnel must follow established verification and approval requirements rather than relying solely on the apparent sender of an email.

---

# 19. Financial Fraud Awareness

Personnel involved in:

- Accounting
- Accounts payable
- Payroll
- Vendor management
- Sales
- Refunds
- Credits
- Financial approvals

must receive training appropriate to relevant fraud risks.

Training should reinforce:

- Separation of duties
- Required approval
- Independent verification
- Reconciliation
- Suspicious-change reporting
- Individual accountability

---

# 20. Information Classification Training

Personnel must receive training appropriate to their information-handling responsibilities.

Training must address the classifications:

- Public
- Internal
- Confidential
- Restricted

Personnel must understand that classification determines information-protection requirements and is separate from business criticality.

Detailed requirements are established in:

- [[POL-004 Information Classification & Handling Policy]]
- [[STD-001 Information Classification Standard]]

---

# 21. Sensitive Information Handling

Personnel handling Confidential or Restricted information must understand requirements concerning:

- Access
- Storage
- Transmission
- External sharing
- Printing
- Physical protection
- Disposal
- Incident reporting

Training depth should correspond to the individual's responsibilities.

---

# 22. Acceptable Use Training

Personnel must receive awareness of acceptable-use requirements.

Training must address relevant requirements from [[POL-002 Acceptable Use Policy]], including:

- Authorized use
- Prohibited activity
- Software installation
- Internet use
- Email use
- Credential protection
- Security-control bypass
- Unauthorized security testing

---

# 23. Physical Security Awareness

Personnel must receive awareness concerning physical-security responsibilities.

Training should address:

- Access cards
- Keys
- Visitor control
- Restricted areas
- Unauthorized entry
- Sensitive documents
- Lost credentials
- Reporting suspicious physical activity

Personnel must understand that safety takes priority over protection of company property.

---

# 24. Mobile and Laptop Security

Personnel issued laptops or mobile devices must receive training concerning:

- Physical protection
- Device locking
- Loss reporting
- Remote use
- Sensitive information
- Untrusted networks
- Required security controls

Users must understand that company security requirements continue to apply outside company facilities.

---

# 25. Remote-Work Security

Personnel authorized to access company resources remotely must receive appropriate awareness concerning:

- Approved remote-access methods
- Protection of company equipment
- Untrusted networks
- Unauthorized viewing
- Device security
- Sensitive information
- Incident reporting

Remote access does not eliminate normal information-handling requirements.

---

# 26. Malware Awareness

Training must address common malware risks including:

- Malicious attachments
- Malicious links
- Unexpected software
- Removable media
- Suspicious security warnings
- Attempts to disable security controls

Personnel must know how to report suspected malware.

---

# 27. Incident Reporting Awareness

Personnel must understand what types of events should be reported.

Examples include:

- Suspected phishing
- Credential compromise
- Malware
- Unauthorized access
- Lost or stolen devices
- Sensitive information exposure
- Physical-security incidents
- Suspicious system behavior
- Suspected fraud

Personnel should report suspicious activity even when they are uncertain whether an actual incident has occurred.

---

# 28. Role-Based Training

Personnel with specialized responsibilities must receive additional security training appropriate to their roles.

Role-based training may apply to:

- System administrators
- Network administrators
- Security personnel
- Help desk personnel
- Application developers
- Infinity contractors
- Managers
- Human Resources
- Finance
- Payroll
- Warehouse personnel
- Personnel responsible for business continuity or recovery

---

# 29. Privileged User Training

Personnel receiving privileged access must receive training appropriate to administrative responsibilities.

Training should address:

- Separate privileged credentials
- Least privilege
- Administrative workstation requirements
- Change control
- Logging
- Credential protection
- Emergency access
- Incident reporting
- Prohibition on unauthorized access

Privileged access must not be granted solely because training has been completed.

Required authorization remains necessary.

---

# 30. IT and Security Personnel Training

IT and security personnel must maintain knowledge appropriate to assigned responsibilities.

Relevant subjects may include:

- Windows administration
- Network security
- Access control
- Vulnerability management
- Security monitoring
- Malware response
- Incident response
- Backup and recovery
- Secure configuration
- Evidence handling

Training may include:

- Formal courses
- Vendor training
- Professional certification
- Internal instruction
- Practical exercises
- Approved self-study

---

# 31. Secure Development Training

Personnel involved in application development or maintenance must receive security training appropriate to their responsibilities.

Training may address:

- Secure coding
- Authentication
- Authorization
- Input validation
- Error handling
- Change control
- Environment separation
- Threat modeling
- Code review
- Security testing

Infinity-related requirements are further established in [[POL-012 Application & Secure Development Policy]] and [[STD-009 Infinity Security Standard]].

---

# 32. Manager Training

Managers must understand their responsibilities concerning:

- Access authorization
- Transfers
- Terminations
- Information handling
- Incident escalation
- Security exceptions
- Risk ownership
- Employee training
- Corrective action

Managers must not assume that security responsibility belongs exclusively to Information Technology.

---

# 33. Human Resources Training

Human Resources personnel must receive training appropriate to:

- Sensitive personnel information
- Employee status
- Onboarding
- Transfers
- Terminations
- Security incident coordination
- Personnel investigations
- Training records

Human Resources must understand its role as the authoritative business source for employee status.

---

# 34. Finance and Payroll Training

Finance and payroll personnel must receive training appropriate to:

- Financial fraud
- Business Email Compromise
- Vendor payment changes
- Payroll banking changes
- Approval requirements
- Separation of duties
- Reconciliation
- Suspicious transaction reporting

Training should emphasize independent verification of suspicious or significant payment-information changes using a trusted contact method.

---

# 35. Warehouse Training

Warehouse personnel must receive security awareness appropriate to:

- Shared workstations
- Individual accountability
- Significant WMS transactions
- Inventory security
- Restricted areas
- High-value inventory cages
- Visitor and vendor access
- Suspicious activity
- Manual continuity procedures

Shared Windows access does not remove individual responsibility for significant business transactions.

---

# 36. Contractor Training

Contractors must receive security instructions appropriate to their access.

Training or orientation may address:

- Acceptable use
- Confidentiality
- Access restrictions
- Physical access
- Information handling
- Incident reporting
- System-specific responsibilities

Contractors do not automatically require the same training curriculum as employees when a more focused security briefing appropriately addresses their access and responsibilities.

---

# 37. Third-Party Specialists

Outside specialists such as:

- Penetration testers
- Incident-response specialists
- Technology consultants
- Application specialists

must receive security instructions appropriate to their engagement and access.

Dunder Mifflin may consider relevant professional qualifications and contractual requirements when determining necessary internal training.

---

# 38. Business Continuity Training

Personnel assigned business-continuity or recovery responsibilities must receive training appropriate to those responsibilities.

Training should address:

- Roles
- Escalation
- Communication
- Manual procedures
- Recovery priorities
- Safety
- Documentation
- Exercises

Training must support the requirements of [[POL-010 Business Continuity & Disaster Recovery Policy]].

---

# 39. Manual Processing Training

Personnel expected to perform manual continuity procedures must receive instruction concerning:

**Collect → Review → Enter → Reconcile → Verify**

Training must address:

- Record creation
- Attribution
- Review
- Reentry into restored systems
- Reconciliation
- Duplicate prevention
- Missing transactions
- Information protection

---

# 40. Incident Response Training

Personnel assigned incident-response responsibilities must receive training appropriate to their roles.

Training may include:

- Incident classification
- Escalation
- Containment
- Communication
- Evidence handling
- Recovery
- Documentation
- Playbook use

Incident-response exercises must be coordinated with [[PLN-001 Incident Response Plan]].

---

# 41. Training Following Incidents

Significant security incidents should be reviewed to determine whether:

- Existing training was insufficient.
- Personnel misunderstood requirements.
- A new threat should be incorporated into training.
- Role-specific training requires improvement.
- Existing awareness material requires revision.

Training must not be treated as the automatic corrective action for every incident.

Technical, procedural, or management controls may be more appropriate depending on the cause.

---

# 42. Training Following Findings

Assessment findings should be reviewed for training implications.

When a deficiency results from:

- Lack of knowledge
- Misunderstood responsibility
- Inadequate instruction

training may form part of corrective action.

When a deficiency results from poor technical design, inadequate staffing, missing controls, or other non-training causes, management must address the actual cause.

---

# 43. Training Records

Training records must be maintained.

Records should identify:

- Individual
- Training assigned
- Training completed
- Completion date
- Assessment score where applicable
- Remediation where applicable
- Role-specific training where required

Records must be sufficient to support management review and assessment.

---

# 44. Training Evidence

Evidence may include:

- Training completion records
- Attendance records
- Assessment results
- Phishing simulation results
- Remediation records
- Role-based training records
- Exercise records

Evidence must represent actual activity.

Training records must not be created or altered to represent training that did not occur.

---

# 45. Overdue Training

Overdue required training must be tracked.

Escalation may include:

1. Reminder to the individual
2. Notification to the manager
3. Notification to Human Resources
4. Additional management action when required

Access restriction may be considered when overdue training creates material risk and management determines restriction is appropriate.

---

# 46. Repeated Training Noncompliance

Repeated refusal or failure to complete required security training may result in:

- Manager review
- Human Resources review
- Access restriction
- Corrective action
- Personnel action where appropriate

Response must consider circumstances and risk.

---

# 47. Training Metrics

The security-awareness program must maintain useful metrics.

Metrics may include:

- Initial training completion rate
- Annual training completion rate
- Annual assessment pass rate
- Remediation completion rate
- Phishing interaction rate
- Phishing reporting rate
- Repeat phishing susceptibility
- Role-based training completion
- Overdue training
- Security incidents involving human behavior

Metric definitions are maintained in [[REG-005 Security Metrics Catalog]].

---

# 48. Key Performance Indicators

Key Performance Indicators (KPIs) may include:

- Percentage completing annual training on time
- Percentage passing annual assessment
- Percentage completing required remediation
- Percentage completing required role-based training

KPIs measure program performance.

---

# 49. Key Risk Indicators

Key Risk Indicators (KRIs) may include:

- Phishing susceptibility
- Repeat phishing susceptibility
- Low phishing reporting rate
- Repeated credential-handling incidents
- Overdue high-risk role training
- Security incidents involving preventable user behavior

KRIs help management understand whether human-related security risk may be increasing.

---

# 50. Metric Interpretation

Training metrics must be interpreted carefully.

For example:

- Training completion does not prove secure behavior.
- A high assessment score does not prove resistance to phishing.
- A single phishing failure does not prove general incompetence.
- Increased incident reporting may reflect improved awareness rather than worsening security.

Management should consider multiple indicators before reaching conclusions.

---

# 51. Program Effectiveness

The awareness program must be evaluated using more than attendance records.

Effectiveness should consider:

- Knowledge-assessment results
- Phishing simulation results
- Reporting behavior
- Incident trends
- Findings
- Repeat behavior
- Manager feedback
- Role-specific performance

Training should be revised when evidence indicates that important security behaviors are not improving.

---

# 52. Phishing Trend Analysis

Phishing results should be evaluated over time.

Analysis should consider:

- Overall susceptibility
- Reporting rate
- Repeat susceptibility
- Common failure patterns
- Department or role patterns where meaningful
- Simulation difficulty
- Changes in threat techniques

Management must avoid drawing conclusions from a single simulation without appropriate context.

---

# 53. Privacy and Fairness

Security-awareness measurement must be used for legitimate security and management purposes.

The program must not be designed as indiscriminate employee surveillance.

Individual results must be limited to personnel with legitimate business need.

Reporting to broader management should use aggregated information where individual identification is unnecessary.

---

# 54. Simulation Safety

Security simulations must not:

- Intentionally damage systems.
- Collect real passwords unnecessarily.
- Create unnecessary business disruption.
- Expose actual sensitive information.
- Create unreasonable safety concerns.
- Impersonate emergency situations in a manner likely to cause harmful panic.

Simulation techniques must remain proportionate to the training objective.

---

# 55. Phishing Credential Simulation

When a simulation evaluates credential-entry behavior, the simulation must be designed so that actual company passwords are not unnecessarily collected or retained.

The objective is to measure behavior, not obtain personnel authentication secrets.

---

# 56. Awareness Communications

Security awareness may also be reinforced through:

- Email notices
- Posters
- Brief reminders
- Manager communications
- Security bulletins
- Lessons from incidents
- Targeted warnings

Supplemental communications do not replace required formal training.

---

# 57. Threat-Based Awareness

The IT & Information Security Manager may issue targeted awareness communications when a specific threat requires additional attention.

Examples include:

- Active phishing campaigns
- Malware outbreaks
- Fraud attempts
- Significant third-party incidents
- Newly observed social-engineering techniques

Communications should provide personnel with practical actions.

---

# 58. Risk Relationships

This policy provides significant treatment support for risks including:

- R001 Phishing / Credential Compromise
- R002 Business Email Compromise
- R003 Ransomware
- R004 Malware
- R005 Password / Authentication Weakness
- R009 Sensitive Information Theft / Extortion
- R010 Unauthorized / Excessive Access
- R012 Malicious Insider
- R014 Lost or Stolen Device
- R029 Vendor Fraud
- R030 Sales Fraud
- R031 Payroll Fraud
- R033 Manual Outage Processing Error

Risk treatment is documented in [[RTP-001 Enterprise Risk Treatment Plan]].

---

# 59. Assessment

Security-awareness controls must be periodically assessed.

Assessment may include:

- Inspection of training records
- Sampling personnel records
- Review of assessment results
- Review of phishing simulations
- Observation
- Interviews
- Independent Performance
- Review of remediation records
- Comparison with incident trends

Assessment should determine whether required activities actually occurred and whether the program produces useful security outcomes.

---

# 60. Findings

Material awareness and training deficiencies must be documented.

Examples include:

- Required training not completed
- Missing training records
- Repeated overdue training
- Required assessments not performed
- Required remediation not completed
- Monthly phishing simulations not conducted
- High-risk personnel lacking required role-based training
- Training content materially inconsistent with policy

Material corrective actions must be tracked in [[REG-004 Finding & Corrective Action Register]].

---

# 61. Exceptions

Exceptions to this policy require:

- Documented business justification
- Affected requirement
- Associated risk
- Defined duration
- Compensating measures where practical
- Responsible owner
- Appropriate approval

An exception must not be used to conceal training noncompliance.

---

# 62. Enforcement

Failure to comply with required security training may result in:

- Additional training
- Management review
- Access restriction
- Corrective action
- Personnel action where appropriate
- Contractual action for applicable third parties

Response must consider severity, repetition, role, and risk.

---

# 63. Policy Review

This policy must be reviewed:

- At least annually
- Following significant security incidents
- Following significant assessment findings
- When major threat patterns change
- When security responsibilities materially change
- When training requirements materially change

The CIO coordinates review.

Material revisions require appropriate approval.

---
