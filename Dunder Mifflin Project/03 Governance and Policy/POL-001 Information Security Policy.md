# 1. Purpose

This policy establishes Dunder Mifflin's overarching requirements for protecting company information, systems, applications, technology, and supporting business operations.

The policy provides management direction for the Enterprise Information Security & Risk Management Program and establishes the security principles under which supporting policies, standards, plans, procedures, and controls operate.

Dunder Mifflin must protect information and technology in a manner appropriate to:

- Business requirements
- Information sensitivity
- Business criticality
- Enterprise risk
- Legal obligations
- Contractual obligations
- Customer expectations
- Operational requirements

Information security exists to support Dunder Mifflin's business objectives while maintaining risk within levels approved by management.

---

# 2. Scope

This policy applies to:

- All Dunder Mifflin employees
- Executives
- Temporary personnel
- Contractors
- Consultants
- Third parties with authorized access to company information or systems
- Company-owned information
- Company-managed information
- Company systems
- Applications
- Endpoints
- Networks
- Cloud-hosted resources
- Mobile devices
- Physical records
- Facilities supporting company operations

Third-party requirements apply according to the information, systems, facilities, or services involved.

---

# 3. Policy Objectives

Dunder Mifflin's information-security program must:

1. Protect the confidentiality of sensitive information.
2. Protect the integrity of information and business transactions.
3. Maintain appropriate availability of business processes and supporting systems.
4. Protect personnel and physical resources.
5. Manage security risk.
6. Establish individual accountability.
7. Limit access according to business need.
8. Protect systems against reasonably foreseeable threats.
9. Detect and respond to security incidents.
10. Maintain business continuity and recovery capability.
11. Manage security dependencies involving third parties.
12. Validate the effectiveness of important security controls.
13. Support continuous improvement.

---

# 4. Security Principles

## 4.1 Confidentiality

Information must be protected against unauthorized access, use, and disclosure according to its classification and business requirements.

---

## 4.2 Integrity

Information, systems, and business transactions must be protected against unauthorized or inappropriate modification.

Controls should support the accuracy, completeness, and reliability of important business information.

---

## 4.3 Availability

Systems and information must be available according to business requirements.

Availability requirements must be informed by business criticality and the Business Impact Analysis (BIA).

---

## 4.4 Safety

Human life and safety take priority over:

- Systems
- Operations
- Facilities
- Equipment
- Inventory
- Revenue
- Other company assets

Personnel must not place themselves at unreasonable physical risk to protect company property or maintain operations.

---

## 4.5 Least Privilege

Access must be limited to the permissions reasonably required to perform authorized responsibilities.

Employment position or executive seniority does not independently justify unrestricted access.

---

## 4.6 Need to Know

Access to sensitive information must be limited to individuals with an authorized business need.

---

## 4.7 Individual Accountability

Significant user, administrative, financial, and security activity should be attributable to an individual where practical.

Shared access must be limited to justified operational circumstances.

---

## 4.8 Separation of Duties

Responsibilities must be separated where practical when combining them would create unreasonable opportunity for:

- Fraud
- Unauthorized activity
- Concealment
- Unreviewed administrative changes
- Inappropriate approval

Separation of duties must remain proportionate to Dunder Mifflin's size and staffing.

---

## 4.9 Defense in Depth

Dunder Mifflin should not depend unnecessarily on a single security control when layered protection is practical and justified by risk.

Controls may include combinations of:

- Administrative controls
- Technical controls
- Physical controls
- Preventive controls
- Detective controls
- Corrective controls
- Recovery controls

---

## 4.10 Risk-Based Security

Security controls must be proportionate to business risk.

Not every system, process, or information type requires identical protection.

Control decisions should consider:

- Business criticality
- Information classification
- Threats
- Vulnerabilities
- Business impact
- Existing controls
- Residual risk
- Cost
- Operational requirements

- Security accountability
- Policy authority

# 6. Roles and Responsibilities

## 6.1 CEO

The CEO provides executive authority and approves major enterprise security policies and significant risk decisions.

---

## 6.2 CIO

The CIO provides executive leadership for information security, cybersecurity, technology security, and related security programs.

---

## 6.3 COO

The COO provides executive oversight for operational, facility, warehouse, inventory, transportation, and supplier-related security.

---

## 6.4 CFO

The CFO provides executive oversight for financial security, accounting, payroll, payment processes, and financial fraud controls.

---

## 6.5 Human Resources

Human Resources supports personnel security and serves as the authoritative business source for employee status.

---

## 6.6 Legal and Compliance

Legal and Compliance advises management concerning applicable legal, contractual, confidentiality, investigation, and compliance requirements.

---

## 6.7 Business, System, and Information Owners

Owners must:

- Identify requirements.
- Support risk assessment.
- Approve access where appropriate.
- Support classification.
- Support continuity planning.
- Participate in treatment decisions.
- Support security assessment.

---

## 6.8 IT & Information Security Manager

The IT & Information Security Manager coordinates operational implementation of the security program and escalates material security issues to appropriate management.

---

## 6.9 All Personnel

All personnel must:

- Follow security requirements.
- Protect company information.
- Protect authentication credentials.
- Use company resources appropriately.
- Report suspected security incidents.
- Report lost or stolen company devices.
- Follow physical-security requirements.
- Complete required security training.
- Cooperate with authorized investigations.

# 7. Information Classification

Dunder Mifflin information must be classified according to [[STD-001 Information Classification Standard]].

The approved classifications are:

- Public
- Internal
- Confidential
- Restricted

Protection requirements must correspond to information sensitivity.

Information owners are responsible for determining appropriate classification.

---

# 8. Information Handling

Information must be:

- Accessed only for authorized purposes.
- Stored appropriately.
- Transmitted appropriately.
- Shared only with authorized recipients.
- Protected according to classification.
- Retained according to applicable business, legal, and contractual requirements.
- Securely disposed of when no longer required.

Detailed requirements are established in [[POL-004 Information Classification & Handling Policy]] and [[STD-001 Information Classification Standard]].

---

# 9. Access Control

Access to Dunder Mifflin information, systems, applications, and facilities must be based on legitimate business need.

Access controls must support:

- Least privilege
- Need to know
- Individual accountability
- Appropriate authorization
- Prompt termination
- Appropriate transfer handling
- Privileged-access restrictions
- Periodic access review

Detailed requirements are established in [[POL-003 Access Control Policy]].

---

# 10. Authentication

Users must authenticate using approved credentials and authentication methods.

Authentication controls must be appropriate to the sensitivity and risk of the resource.

Where supported and required, stronger authentication may include smart-card multi-factor authentication using a card and PIN or other approved secret.

Authentication credentials must not be shared except for specifically authorized emergency or operational circumstances governed by appropriate controls.

---

# 11. Privileged Access

Privileged access must receive additional protection.

Requirements include:

- Separate privileged credentials
- Restricted administrative access
- Limited standing Domain Administrator membership
- Dedicated administrative workstations where required
- Periodic privileged-access review
- Appropriate logging
- Emergency-access protection

External remote privileged administration is prohibited unless specifically authorized through an approved exception.

Detailed requirements are established in [[PROC-002 Privileged Access Procedure]].

---

# 12. Acceptable Use

Company technology and information must be used in a manner consistent with:

- Business requirements
- Security requirements
- Legal obligations
- Professional conduct
- Protection of company resources

Detailed requirements are established in [[POL-002 Acceptable Use Policy]].

---

# 13. Endpoint Security

Company endpoints must be managed according to security requirements appropriate to their function and risk.

Requirements include, where applicable:

- Approved operating systems
- Managed antivirus
- Security updates
- Standard-user operation
- Screen locking
- Full-disk encryption for company laptops
- Central management
- Device inventory
- Approved software
- Security configuration

Detailed requirements are established in [[STD-003 Endpoint Security Standard]].

---

# 14. Server Security

Servers must be configured, administered, patched, and monitored according to their function and risk.

Server administration must be restricted to authorized personnel.

Detailed requirements are established in [[STD-004 Server Security Standard]].

---

# 15. Network Security

Networks must be designed and managed to protect company systems and information.

Requirements include:

- Firewall protection
- Restriction of unsolicited inbound access
- Protection of administrative interfaces
- Secure remote connectivity
- Appropriate network configuration
- Change control
- Configuration backup
- Network discovery
- Detection of unauthorized wireless infrastructure

Dunder Mifflin does not maintain a general corporate wireless network.

The headquarters lobby Internet-only guest wireless access point must remain separated from internal company resources.

Detailed requirements are established in [[STD-002 Network Security Standard]].

---

# 16. Remote Access

Remote access must be:

- Authorized
- Business justified
- Appropriately authenticated
- Restricted to required resources
- Protected when using untrusted networks

External or untrusted company laptops must use approved protected connectivity when accessing nonpublic company cloud resources.

Public Infinity services may be accessed directly through their intended public interfaces.

Remote access does not automatically authorize privileged administration.

---

# 17. Mobile Devices

Company-issued mobile devices must be managed according to their capabilities and business use.

Controls should include, where supported:

- Inventory
- Enrollment
- Configuration management
- Passcode requirements
- Automatic locking
- Location capability
- Remote lock
- Remote wipe

Company mobile devices may support continuity communications and cellular connectivity where appropriate.

---

# 18. Personally Owned Devices

General Bring Your Own Device (BYOD) access to company systems is not authorized.

Exceptions require documented business need, security review, and appropriate approval.

Personal devices may be used for emergency safety or personnel contact when directed by management, but such use does not establish general authorization to process company information on personal devices.

---

# 19. Malware Protection

Company systems must use appropriate protections against malicious software.

Controls include:

- Centrally managed antivirus
- Security updates
- Standard-user operation
- Secure configuration
- User awareness
- Monitoring
- Incident response

Malware incidents must be handled according to [[PROC-009 Malware Response Procedure]] and applicable incident-response requirements.

---

# 20. Vulnerability Management

Dunder Mifflin must identify and address material vulnerabilities in systems and applications.

The vulnerability-management process must include:

- Vulnerability identification
- Risk evaluation
- Remediation
- Exception management
- Retesting where appropriate
- Reporting

Detailed requirements are established in [[PRO-004 Vulnerability Management Program]].

---

# 21. Patch Management

Security updates must be evaluated, tested where appropriate, scheduled, deployed, monitored, and verified.

Patch implementation must balance:

- Security urgency
- System stability
- Business impact
- Testing requirements
- Recovery capability

Patch activity must integrate with change and configuration management.

---

# 22. Change and Configuration Management

Material technology changes must be controlled.

Change management must address, where appropriate:

- Request
- Review
- Approval
- Testing
- Implementation
- Rollback
- Verification
- Documentation

Systems must maintain appropriate security configuration.

Detailed requirements are established in [[POL-007 Change & Configuration Management Policy]] and [[STD-006 Secure Configuration Standard]].

---

# 23. Logging and Monitoring

Security-relevant activity must be logged and reviewed according to risk and operational requirements.

Monitoring should support detection of:

- Authentication anomalies
- Malware
- Unauthorized access
- Administrative activity
- Significant application activity
- Security incidents
- Control failures

Dunder Mifflin does not require continuous 24-hour monitoring of every system.

Monitoring must be proportionate to risk.

Detailed requirements are established in [[PRO-005 Security Operations Program]] and [[PROC-008 Security Monitoring Procedure]].

---

# 24. Security Incident Reporting

Personnel must promptly report suspected or known security incidents.

Reportable events may include:

- Suspected phishing
- Credential compromise
- Malware
- Unauthorized access
- Lost or stolen devices
- Sensitive information exposure
- Suspicious system activity
- Significant third-party incidents
- Physical-security incidents

Personnel should report suspected incidents even when they are uncertain whether an actual compromise occurred.

---

# 25. Incident Response

Dunder Mifflin must maintain an incident-response capability.

Incident response must support:

- Identification
- Reporting
- Triage
- Classification
- Escalation
- Containment
- Investigation
- Evidence handling
- Recovery
- Communication
- Lessons learned

Detailed requirements are established in:

- [[POL-009 Incident Response Policy]]
- [[PLN-001 Incident Response Plan]]
- [[STD-008 Incident Classification & Escalation Standard]]

---

# 26. Evidence Handling

Potential evidence associated with security incidents or investigations must be protected from inappropriate alteration, destruction, or disclosure.

Evidence handling should maintain:

- Identification
- Custody
- Integrity
- Relevant timestamps
- Appropriate documentation

Cryptographic hashes such as SHA-256 should be used where practical for digital evidence requiring integrity verification.

Detailed requirements are established in [[PROC-011 Evidence Handling Procedure]].

---

# 27. Backup

Important company information and systems must be backed up according to business and recovery requirements.

Backup controls must consider:

- Information classification
- Business criticality
- RPO
- Backup frequency
- Encryption
- Off-site separation
- Retention
- Administrative protection
- Recovery requirements

The existence of a backup does not demonstrate that recovery requirements can be met.

---

# 28. Recovery Testing

Dunder Mifflin must validate recovery capability.

Requirements include:

- Quarterly representative restoration testing
- Annual meaningful recovery testing for Critical functions
- Validation of recovery documentation
- Evaluation against applicable RTO and RPO requirements
- Documentation of deficiencies
- Corrective action where required

Detailed requirements are established in [[PROC-012 Backup & Recovery Procedure]] and [[TMP-003 Recovery Test Template]].

---

# 29. Business Continuity and Disaster Recovery

Dunder Mifflin must maintain Business Continuity/Disaster Recovery (BC/DR) capabilities based on business requirements.

Recovery priorities must be informed by [[RPT-002 Business Impact Analysis]].

General priority is:

**Safety → Critical → High → Moderate → Low**

Actual recovery sequencing may account for technical and business dependencies.

Detailed requirements are established in:

- [[POL-010 Business Continuity & Disaster Recovery Policy]]
- [[PLN-002 Business Continuity Plan]]
- [[PLN-003 Disaster Recovery Plan]]
- [[PLN-004 Cloud Exit & Emergency Migration Plan]]

---

# 30. Manual Continuity

Selected business functions must maintain manual continuity procedures where practical.

Manual processing must support the lifecycle:

**Collect → Review → Enter → Reconcile → Verify**

Manual operation is temporary and does not eliminate the requirement to restore normal systems.

Detailed requirements are established in [[PROC-013 Manual Processing Procedure]].

# 32. Confidentiality Agreements

Nonemployees and outside parties must be subject to an enforceable confidentiality obligation before receiving access to sensitive Dunder Mifflin information when the nature of the access requires such protection.

This may include:

- Contractors
- Consultants
- Technology providers
- Penetration testers
- Infinity contractors
- Temporary specialists
- Outside support personnel

The requirement is based on access and information sensitivity rather than vendor title.

Ordinary visitors, delivery personnel, vendor sales representatives, or building-maintenance personnel do not automatically require a confidentiality agreement when they are not provided sensitive access.

If the scope of their access changes, appropriate confidentiality protection must be established before sensitive access is granted.

---

# 33. Physical Security

Dunder Mifflin facilities must use physical controls appropriate to business operations and risk.

Controls may include:

- Receptionist-controlled access
- Employee access credentials
- Visitor controls
- Restricted areas
- Keys
- Access cards
- Security cages
- Escort requirements
- Facility inspections

Detailed requirements are established in [[POL-011 Physical & Environmental Security Policy]].

---

# 34. Environmental Protection

Facilities and technology must receive reasonable protection from environmental hazards including:

- Fire
- Water
- Power failure
- HVAC failure

Environmental controls must prioritize safety and business requirements.

Dunder Mifflin does not require backup generators unless business requirements and cost analysis justify them.

---

# 35. Personnel Security

Personnel-security controls must support:

- Appropriate onboarding
- Access authorization
- Role changes
- Termination
- Company property recovery
- Confidentiality
- Security awareness
- Investigation where appropriate

Detailed requirements are established in [[PRO-006 Physical & Personnel Security Program]] and [[PROC-017 Personnel Security Procedure]].

# 36. Security Awareness and Training

Personnel must receive security awareness appropriate to their responsibilities.

Requirements include:

- Initial security awareness within five business days
- Annual refresher training
- Annual knowledge assessment
- Minimum passing score of 80 percent
- Monthly phishing simulations
- Additional role-based training where appropriate

Detailed requirements are established in [[POL-006 Security Awareness & Training Policy]].

---

# 37. Application Security

Applications must receive security controls appropriate to their business function, information, exposure, and risk.

Application-security requirements may include:

- Access control
- Secure configuration
- Change management
- Environment separation
- Code review
- Security testing
- Threat modeling
- Vulnerability remediation
- Logging
- Recovery

Detailed requirements are established in [[POL-012 Application & Secure Development Policy]].

# 38. Infinity Security

Infinity requires additional security attention because it:

- Is Internet-facing.
- Provides customer ordering.
- Processes business information.
- Depends on a third-party support contractor.
- Represents Dunder Mifflin intellectual property.

Infinity must receive:

- Logical web/application/database separation
- Controlled administrative access
- Development/test/production separation
- Secure change controls
- Threat modeling
- Code review
- Security testing
- Annual security assessment
- Annual application penetration testing
- Finding remediation and retesting
- Recovery capability
- Annual transferability review

Detailed requirements are established in [[STD-009 Infinity Security Standard]].

---

# 39. Secure Development

Software developed for Dunder Mifflin must follow security practices appropriate to the application and risk.

Dunder Mifflin must maintain ownership or appropriate control of:

- Source code
- Repositories
- Documentation
- Company data
- Backups
- Intellectual property
- Contracted work product

Detailed requirements are established in [[PRO-007 Secure Software Development Lifecycle Program]].

# 40. Security Assessment

Security controls must be assessed according to risk and business importance.

Assessment may include:

- Inspection
- Observation
- Interview
- Sampling
- Independent Performance
- Technical Testing
- Exercises
- Reconciliation

Assessment scope and methods must be sufficient to support the conclusion being reached.

---

# 41. Penetration Testing

Penetration testing must be:

- Authorized
- Scoped
- Controlled
- Conducted by appropriately qualified personnel
- Documented
- Followed by remediation and retesting where required

Infinity must receive an annual application penetration test.

The broader enterprise must receive penetration testing at least biennially.

Detailed requirements are established in [[STD-007 Penetration Testing Standard]].

---

# 42. Security Assurance

Dunder Mifflin must maintain an assurance process to determine whether important controls are appropriately designed and operating as expected.

Assurance priorities must consider:

- Inherent risk
- Residual risk
- Business criticality
- Control dependency
- Significant change
- Previous findings
- Incident history

Detailed requirements are established in [[PRO-008 Security Assurance & Continuous Improvement Program]].

---

# 43. Evidence

Control operation and assessment conclusions must be supported by evidence appropriate to the control.

Evidence may include:

- Configuration records
- Logs
- Tickets
- Access records
- Approvals
- Training records
- Test results
- Assessment reports
- Contracts
- Physical inspection
- Observation
- Reconciliation
- Exercise results

The existence of a policy does not independently prove that the required control operates effectively.

---

# 44. Evidence Integrity

Security evidence must accurately represent actual activity.

Personnel must not:

- Manufacture evidence
- Backdate evidence
- Alter evidence to conceal control failure
- Create records representing activity that did not occur
- Represent planned activity as completed activity

Control deficiencies must be reported and addressed.

---

# 45. Security Findings

Security assessments may produce findings concerning control design or operation.

Material deficiencies must be:

- Documented
- Assigned
- Remediated
- Retested where appropriate
- Considered during risk reassessment

Material corrective actions are tracked in [[REG-004 Finding & Corrective Action Register]].

# 48. Risk Management

Security risk must be managed according to [[POL-005 Risk Management Policy]].

Risk management must distinguish among:

- Inherent risk
- Current residual risk
- Target residual risk
- Validated residual risk

Planned controls do not reduce current residual risk.

---

# 49. Risk Treatment

Risk treatment may include:

- Mitigation
- Transfer
- Avoidance
- Acceptance

Treatment activities are maintained in [[RTP-001 Enterprise Risk Treatment Plan]].

Implementation does not automatically establish effectiveness.

---

# 50. Risk Acceptance

Risk acceptance must follow approved authority.

| Residual Risk | Acceptance Authority |
|---|---|
| Low | Risk Owner |
| Moderate | Risk Owner + Appropriate Executive |
| High | CEO following ISRC review |
| Critical | CEO following ISRC review and exceptional justification |

High risk acceptance must be reassessed within 90 days.

Critical risk acceptance must be reassessed within 30 days.

---

# 51. Security Exceptions

Temporary deviation from an approved security requirement requires a documented exception when the deviation is material.

Exceptions must identify:

- Requirement affected
- Business justification
- Associated risk
- Duration
- Compensating controls where practical
- Responsible owner
- Approval
- Review or expiration date

Exceptions creating High or Critical residual risk require escalation consistent with risk-acceptance authority.

---

# 52. Security Documentation

Security documentation must be:

- Accurate
- Appropriately protected
- Available to authorized personnel
- Maintained when material changes occur
- Reviewed according to document requirements

Critical recovery documentation must not exist exclusively on systems that may themselves require recovery.

---

# 53. Change Impact on Recovery

A significant technology or configuration change is not complete until applicable recovery documentation and recovery requirements have been reviewed and updated as necessary.

Changes must not silently invalidate recovery capability.

---

# 54. Security Control Inventory

Security controls must be documented in [[REG-003 Enterprise Security Control Inventory]].

The control inventory should support traceability among:

- Requirements
- Risks
- Controls
- Owners
- Implementation
- Evidence
- Assessment
- Findings

---

# 55. Continuous Monitoring

Dunder Mifflin must maintain ongoing awareness of important security controls and risks.

Continuous monitoring may include:

- Automated alerts
- Periodic review
- Vulnerability assessment
- Access review
- Restoration testing
- Exercises
- Metrics
- Incident analysis
- Management review

Continuous monitoring does not require continuous 24-hour observation of every control.

Monitoring must be appropriate to risk.

---

# 56. Enforcement

Violations of this policy may result in:

- Access restriction
- Corrective action
- Management review
- Personnel action
- Contractual action
- Legal action where appropriate

Enforcement must consider severity, intent, business impact, repetition, and applicable legal or personnel requirements.

---

# 57. Exceptions to This Policy

Exceptions must follow the exception-governance requirements established in [[PRO-009 Security Governance Framework]].

An exception must not be used to conceal control failure or silently accept risk.

---

# 58. Policy Review

This policy must be reviewed:

- At least annually
- Following material business change
- Following material technology change
- Following significant security incidents
- Following significant assessment findings
- Following material legal or contractual change
- When the enterprise risk environment materially changes

The CIO coordinates review.

Material revisions require CEO approval.
