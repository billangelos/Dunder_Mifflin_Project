# 1. Purpose

This program establishes Dunder Mifflin's enterprise approach to physical and personnel security.

The program protects:

- Employees and visitors
- Company information
- Information systems
- Facilities
- Warehouse inventory
- Business records
- Equipment
- Recovery resources
- Other company assets

Physical and personnel security must support business operations without creating unnecessary barriers to normal work.

---

# 2. Security Priority

Human life and safety take priority over:

- Systems
- Operations
- Facilities
- Equipment
- Inventory
- Revenue
- Other company assets

No security requirement in this program requires an employee to remain in or enter an unsafe environment.

---

# 3. Scope

This program applies to:

- Scranton Headquarters
- Scranton Branch
- Syracuse Branch
- Utica Branch
- Rochester Branch
- Nashua Branch
- Akron Branch
- Employees
- Contractors
- Temporary personnel
- Visitors
- Vendors
- Delivery personnel
- Service personnel
- Company-controlled work areas
- Company information and equipment located within those areas

---

# 4. Facility Model

Dunder Mifflin operates seven leased facilities:

1. Scranton Headquarters
2. Scranton Branch
3. Syracuse Branch
4. Utica Branch
5. Rochester Branch
6. Nashua Branch
7. Akron Branch

Dunder Mifflin does not own these buildings.

Physical security responsibilities are divided between Dunder Mifflin and the applicable landlord or property manager.

---

# 5. Shared Responsibility

Landlords may control:

- Building exterior
- Common entrances
- Parking areas
- Common hallways
- Building utilities
- Common-area surveillance
- Building fire systems
- Other shared infrastructure

Dunder Mifflin remains responsible for security controls within company-controlled areas.

---

# 6. Landlord Controls

Landlord-provided controls must not automatically be treated as Dunder Mifflin controls.

Where company security depends on a landlord-controlled protection, the dependency should be identified.

---

# 7. CCTV Baseline

Landlord-controlled Closed-Circuit Television (CCTV) may exist in building common areas.

Dunder Mifflin does not maintain an enterprise-wide internal CCTV surveillance system.

---

# 8. Surveillance Principle

This program does not establish indiscriminate employee surveillance as a personnel-security control.

Security monitoring should remain proportionate to:

- Business need
- Security risk
- Privacy
- Legal requirements
- Facility conditions

---

# 9. Physical Security Objectives

Physical security objectives include:

- Protecting people.
- Preventing unauthorized access.
- Protecting Restricted areas.
- Protecting high-value inventory.
- Protecting information systems.
- Protecting sensitive records.
- Supporting accountability.
- Detecting physical-security weaknesses.
- Supporting continuity and recovery.

---

# 10. Personnel Security Objectives

Personnel security objectives include:

- Establishing appropriate access based on business need.
- Coordinating access with employment status.
- Protecting sensitive information.
- Maintaining individual accountability.
- Managing contractors and temporary personnel.
- Removing access when no longer required.
- Supporting investigations.
- Reducing dependence on individual personnel.

---

# 11. Security Zones

Dunder Mifflin uses a practical physical zoning model.

Areas may be categorized as:

- Public
- Controlled
- Restricted

---

# 12. Public Areas

Public areas are locations where authorized members of the public may normally enter without receiving employee-level facility access.

Examples may include:

- Reception areas
- Designated customer-facing areas
- Other specifically authorized public spaces

---

# 13. Public Access Limitation

Public access to one portion of a facility does not authorize access to:

- Employee work areas
- Warehouses
- Information Technology areas
- Restricted records
- Secured inventory
- Other Controlled or Restricted areas

---

# 14. Controlled Areas

Controlled areas are normal company work areas not intended for unrestricted public access.

Examples include:

- General offices
- Employee workspaces
- Warehouse operating areas
- Internal corridors
- Other employee work areas

---

# 15. Controlled Area Access

Controlled areas should be limited to:

- Employees
- Authorized contractors
- Authorized temporary personnel
- Escorted visitors
- Other specifically authorized individuals

---

# 16. Restricted Areas

Restricted areas contain assets or activities requiring stronger physical access restrictions.

Examples may include:

- Significant Information Technology equipment
- Sensitive records
- High-value inventory cages
- Other specifically designated areas

---

# 17. Restricted Area Principle

Restricted access must be based on legitimate business need.

General facility access does not automatically provide Restricted-area access.

---

# 18. Significant IT Areas

Areas containing significant Information Technology infrastructure should be designated Restricted where appropriate.

Access should be limited to personnel with a legitimate operational or support need.

---

# 19. Restricted Area Controls

Restricted areas may use controls such as:

- Keys
- Locked doors
- Access cards
- Locked cages
- Authorized-personnel lists
- Escort requirements

based on the area and risk.

---

# 20. Physical Access Lifecycle

Physical access follows:

**Request → Authorize → Issue → Use → Review → Modify → Revoke**

---

# 21. Access Request

Physical access should be requested based on:

- Job responsibilities
- Facility assignment
- Required work areas
- Business need

---

# 22. Access Authorization

Physical access must be approved by appropriate management or the responsible area owner.

---

# 23. Least Physical Access

Personnel should receive only the physical access reasonably required for their responsibilities.

---

# 24. Access Credentials

Physical access credentials may include:

- Access cards
- Keys
- Other approved facility credentials

Credentials remain company-controlled assets where applicable.

---

# 25. Individual Accountability

Physical access credentials assigned to an individual must not be intentionally shared unless an approved operational process specifically requires otherwise.

---

# 26. Lost Access Credential

A lost or stolen:

- Access card
- Key
- Other facility credential

must be reported promptly.

---

# 27. Lost Credential Response

Response should consider:

- Facility
- Area accessible
- Credential type
- Restricted access
- Whether the credential can be disabled
- Whether locks or other protections require change

---

# 28. Rear Employee Access

Where rear or employee entrances use access cards, access must be limited to authorized personnel.

---

# 29. Reception

Public-facing entrances should use reception or another reasonable method to control visitor entry into nonpublic work areas.

---

# 30. Visitor Management

Visitors entering Controlled or Restricted areas must be managed according to [[PROC-015 Visitor & Vendor Access Procedure]].

---

# 31. Visitor Identification

Visitors should be identified sufficiently to establish:

- Who they are
- Who they are visiting
- Business purpose
- Appropriate sponsor

---

# 32. Visitor Logging

Visitor records should be maintained where required by facility procedure.

The record may include:

- Visitor name
- Company
- Host
- Arrival time
- Departure time
- Purpose

---

# 33. Visitor Escort

Visitors must be escorted where their authorized access does not permit independent movement.

---

# 34. Visitor Access Scope

Visitor access must be limited to locations reasonably necessary for the authorized visit.

---

# 35. Visitor Responsibility

The employee sponsoring or receiving a visitor is responsible for complying with applicable visitor requirements.

---

# 36. Vendors

Vendor personnel do not automatically receive employee-equivalent facility access.

Access must be based on:

- Business purpose
- Required area
- Required duration
- Information exposure
- Equipment exposure

---

# 37. Service Personnel

Service personnel requiring access to Restricted areas must receive specific authorization.

Examples may include personnel servicing:

- Information Technology equipment
- Electrical systems
- HVAC systems
- Security systems
- Other sensitive infrastructure

---

# 38. Delivery Personnel

Ordinary delivery personnel should not receive unnecessary access to:

- Office workspaces
- Sensitive records
- Restricted IT areas
- High-value inventory cages

---

# 39. Warehouse Delivery Access

Warehouse receiving and shipping activity should be organized so delivery personnel receive only the access necessary to complete the delivery or pickup.

---

# 40. Confidentiality Requirement

Before a nonemployee or outside party receives access to sensitive company information, an appropriate Non-Disclosure Agreement (NDA) or equivalent enforceable confidentiality obligation must be in place.

---

# 41. Confidentiality Scope

The confidentiality requirement is based on:

- Access
- Information sensitivity
- Business relationship

rather than vendor title alone.

---

# 42. Confidentiality Examples

The requirement may apply to:

- Consultants
- Information Technology service providers
- Infinity contractors
- Penetration testers
- Temporary specialists
- Service personnel with sensitive access
- Other outside parties receiving sensitive information

---

# 43. Confidentiality Limitation

An NDA does not establish that:

- Physical access is appropriate.
- Technical access is appropriate.
- The third party is competent.
- Security controls are effective.
- Vendor monitoring is sufficient.
- Testing is adequate.

Confidentiality is one control within a larger security process.

---

# 44. Ordinary Visitors

Ordinary visitors without sensitive information access do not automatically require an NDA.

Their access must still comply with visitor controls.

---

# 45. Restricted Vendor Access

Vendor access to Restricted areas requires:

- Legitimate business need
- Authorization
- Appropriate supervision or escort where required
- Confidentiality protection when sensitive information may be exposed

---

# 46. Restricted Area Procedure

Detailed Restricted-area access requirements are established by [[PROC-016 Restricted Area Access Procedure]].

---

# 47. Warehouse Security

Each branch maintains warehouse inventory requiring physical protection.

Warehouse security must support:

- Inventory accountability
- Receiving
- Storage
- Picking
- Shipping
- Theft prevention

---

# 48. High-Value Inventory Security

Each warehouse maintains a locked security cage or enclosure for higher-value or theft-attractive inventory.

---

# 49. Security Cage Ownership

Warehouse security cages are operationally managed by Operations.

---

# 50. Security Cage Access

Access to a high-value inventory cage must be limited to personnel with a legitimate operational need.

---

# 51. Cage Access Review

Access to high-value inventory cages should be included in periodic physical-access review.

---

# 52. Cage Security Does Not Replace Inventory Controls

The locked cage is one layer of protection.

Inventory security also depends on:

- Receiving records
- Shipping records
- Warehouse Management System records
- Individual accountability for significant transactions
- Inventory counts
- Reconciliation
- Investigation of discrepancies

---

# 53. Inventory Movement

Material inventory movement should remain traceable through applicable business records.

This includes:

- Receiving
- Transfer
- Pick
- Adjustment
- Return
- Shipping

---

# 54. Inventory Theft Risk

Physical controls should make material inventory theft more difficult without:

- Bypassing physical protections
- Creating inventory discrepancies
- Creating receiving discrepancies
- Creating shipping discrepancies
- Creating transaction-record discrepancies

---

# 55. R032 Inventory Theft

R032 has:

- Inherent Risk: **12 High**
- Current Residual Risk: **6 Moderate**
- Target Residual Risk: **3 Low**

---

# 56. R032 Treatment

R032 treatment includes:

- Controlled warehouse access
- Locked high-value inventory cages
- Restricted cage access
- Individual accountability for significant WMS transactions
- Inventory counts
- Reconciliation
- Receiving records
- Shipping records
- Investigation of discrepancies
- Personnel controls

---

# 57. Office Security

Office areas should protect:

- Company records
- Workstations
- Portable equipment
- Sensitive conversations
- Other company assets

from unnecessary public or visitor access.

---

# 58. Unattended Sensitive Information

Employees should not leave Confidential or Restricted information unnecessarily exposed in public or uncontrolled areas.

---

# 59. Sensitive Paper Records

Sensitive paper records should be stored appropriately when unattended.

Appropriate protection may include:

- Locked office
- Locked cabinet
- Restricted records area
- Other controlled storage

---

# 60. Secure Disposal

Sensitive paper information must be securely disposed of when no longer required.

Shredding or an approved secure-destruction process should be used where appropriate.

---

# 61. Information Technology Equipment

Information Technology equipment must receive physical protection appropriate to:

- Business criticality
- Information sensitivity
- Equipment function
- Location

---

# 62. Network Equipment

Important:

- Firewalls
- Routers
- Switches
- Servers
- Other network infrastructure

should be located in Controlled or Restricted areas where practical.

---

# 63. Information Technology Workspace

Information Technology administrative workspaces should not be treated as public visitor areas.

---

# 64. Contractor IT Workspace

Privileged Infinity contractor production or administrative work is performed from a designated controlled workspace at Scranton Headquarters when required.

---

# 65. Contractor Remote Access

Routine external remote privileged administration by the Infinity contractor is not authorized.

The contractor does not receive general VPN access for privileged production administration.

---

# 66. Contractor Physical Access

Infinity contractor physical access must be:

- Sponsored
- Authorized
- Limited to required locations
- Limited to required duration
- Coordinated under applicable contractor and visitor procedures

---

# 67. Contractor Personnel Notice

Expected Infinity contractor personnel should be identified at least one business day before scheduled arrival where practical.

Emergency exceptions must be documented.

---

# 68. Contractor Access Relationship

Physical contractor access must coordinate with:

- [[PROC-004 Contractor Access Procedure]]
- [[PROC-015 Visitor & Vendor Access Procedure]]
- [[PROC-016 Restricted Area Access Procedure]]
- [[PROC-020 Contractor Development Access Procedure]]

where applicable.

---

# 69. Personnel Security Lifecycle

Personnel security follows:

**Hire / Engage → Authorize → Work → Review → Transfer → Terminate**

---

# 70. Personnel Security Procedure

Detailed personnel-security activities are established by [[PROC-017 Personnel Security Procedure]].

---

# 71. Employment Status

Human Resources is the authoritative source for employee status.

---

# 72. Personnel Coordination

Human Resources, management, Information Technology, and facility personnel must coordinate when employment status affects:

- Logical access
- Physical access
- Equipment
- Keys
- Access cards
- Smart cards where implemented
- Company records

---

# 73. Joiner

New personnel receive physical access based on:

- Role
- Facility
- Required work area
- Management authorization

---

# 74. Mover

When an employee changes role or location, existing physical access must be reviewed.

Access no longer required should be revoked.

New access must be separately authorized.

---

# 75. Transfer Principle

A transfer does not automatically justify retaining access from the prior role.

---

# 76. Leaver

When employment or engagement ends, company access and assets must be addressed promptly.

---

# 77. Termination Notification

Human Resources or responsible management must promptly notify appropriate personnel of termination or separation requiring access removal.

---

# 78. Physical Access Revocation

Termination processing should include applicable:

- Access-card disablement
- Key recovery
- Restricted-area access removal
- Contractor credential removal
- Other facility-access revocation

---

# 79. Logical and Physical Coordination

Physical-access termination must coordinate with logical-access termination under [[PROC-001 Joiner-Mover-Leaver Procedure]].

---

# 80. Company Property Recovery

Termination processing should recover applicable company property, including:

- Laptop
- Phone
- Access card
- Keys
- Smart card where implemented
- Backup media
- Documents
- Other company equipment

---

# 81. Unreturned Property

Unreturned company property must be documented and escalated according to:

- Asset value
- Information sensitivity
- Access capability
- Business risk

---

# 82. Unreturned Key

An unreturned key requires evaluation of whether:

- The key provides significant access.
- The area is Restricted.
- Locks should be changed.
- Compensating protection is required.

---

# 83. Unreturned Access Card

An unreturned electronic access card should be disabled promptly.

---

# 84. Smart Cards

Where smart-card authentication is implemented, termination processing must include recovery or invalidation of the smart card as appropriate.

---

# 85. Contractor Termination

Contractor access must be revoked when:

- Engagement ends.
- Authorized duration expires.
- Sponsorship ends.
- Business need ends.
- Security requires immediate removal.

---

# 86. Temporary Personnel

Temporary personnel must receive only the access required for their temporary responsibilities.

Access must not continue beyond the authorized need.

---

# 87. Personnel Screening

Personnel screening must be appropriate to:

- Job responsibilities
- Access
- Applicable law
- Business risk

The company should not perform unnecessary screening unrelated to legitimate business requirements.

---

# 88. Personnel Security Responsibility

Managers are responsible for identifying access and security requirements associated with personnel under their supervision.

---

# 89. Security Awareness

Employees must receive security-awareness training under [[POL-006 Security Awareness & Training Policy]].

---

# 90. Physical Security Awareness

Awareness should include applicable topics such as:

- Visitor handling
- Access credential protection
- Suspicious activity reporting
- Sensitive document protection
- Lost equipment
- Tailgating
- Emergency response
- Restricted-area responsibilities

---

# 91. Tailgating

Employees should not knowingly allow unauthorized individuals to enter Controlled or Restricted areas by following an authorized person through an access-controlled entrance.

---

# 92. Suspicious Person

An unknown or suspicious person in a Controlled or Restricted area should be:

- Challenged when safe and appropriate
- Reported to management
- Reported to facility security or law enforcement when necessary

Employees must not create unnecessary personal danger.

---

# 93. Suspicious Activity

Suspicious physical activity may include:

- Attempted unauthorized entry
- Credential misuse
- Forced door
- Tampered lock
- Unexplained presence in a Restricted area
- Theft
- Deliberate damage
- Suspicious photography of sensitive areas
- Attempted access to Information Technology equipment

---

# 94. Security Event Escalation

Physical-security events with information-security implications must be escalated under [[PROC-010 Security Event Escalation Procedure]].

---

# 95. Incident Response

A physical event that becomes a security incident must coordinate with [[PLN-001 Incident Response Plan]].

---

# 96. Evidence

Potential evidence from a physical-security incident may include:

- Access records
- Visitor logs
- Witness statements
- Photographs
- Damaged equipment
- Landlord records
- Available common-area CCTV
- Recovered property
- Relevant system logs

---

# 97. Evidence Handling

Security incident evidence must be handled according to [[PROC-011 Evidence Handling Procedure]].

---

# 98. Landlord CCTV Evidence

When relevant CCTV is controlled by the landlord, Dunder Mifflin may request available footage or records.

Availability must not be assumed.

---

# 99. Physical Access Reviews

Physical access should be reviewed at least annually.

---

# 100. Review Scope

The annual physical-access review should include as applicable:

- Facility access
- Restricted-area access
- Significant IT-area access
- Warehouse security-cage access
- Contractor access
- Other sensitive physical access

---

# 101. Access Review Objective

The review determines whether each person still has:

- A legitimate business need
- Appropriate scope
- Appropriate location access

---

# 102. Review Result

Access identified as unnecessary must be:

- Removed
- Corrected
- Investigated where appropriate

---

# 103. Physical Access Evidence

Access-review evidence should identify:

- Area reviewed
- Personnel reviewed
- Reviewer
- Review date
- Exceptions
- Required corrective action

---

# 104. Key Inventory

Important keys providing access to Restricted areas should be reasonably accounted for.

---

# 105. Key Review

Key assignments should be reviewed when:

- Personnel leave
- Personnel transfer
- Access requirements change
- A key is lost
- A Restricted area changes

---

# 106. Environmental Security

Environmental protection supports:

- Human safety
- System availability
- Facility availability
- Business-process availability

These are related but distinct objectives.

---

# 107. Fire Protection

Fire protection must prioritize:

1. Human safety
2. Emergency response
3. Business continuity
4. Asset recovery

---

# 108. Fire Systems

Building fire-detection and suppression systems may be landlord-controlled.

Dunder Mifflin should coordinate with the landlord regarding applicable:

- Fire alarms
- Fire extinguishers
- Suppression
- Inspections
- Evacuation requirements

---

# 109. Fire Response

Employees must follow emergency evacuation requirements.

No employee should delay evacuation to:

- Shut down a computer
- Retrieve backup media
- Recover documents
- Protect inventory

---

# 112. Water Protection

Facilities should avoid placing important equipment or sensitive records in locations unnecessarily exposed to:

- Plumbing leaks
- Roof leaks
- Groundwater
- Other predictable water hazards

where practical.

---

# 113. Water Incident

Water leaks affecting:

- Information Technology equipment
- Sensitive records
- Warehouse inventory
- Business operations

must be reported promptly.

# 115. R022 Treatment

---

# 117. Utility Protection

Facilities depend on:

- Electrical power
- Heating

- Air conditioning
- Building utilities

---

---

# 119. UPS Purpose

UPS protection may support:

- Short interruption tolerance
- Orderly shutdown
- Temporary continued operation

---

# 120. Generator Baseline

Dunder Mifflin does not maintain facility generators as part of the normal baseline.

---

# 121. Generator Evaluation

Generator capability should be considered only where:

- BIA requirements
- Facility conditions
- Outage history
- Business need
- Cost

justify the investment.

---

# 122. R023 Utility / Power / HVAC Failure

R023 has:

- Inherent Risk: **16 High**
- Current Residual Risk: **9 Moderate**
- Target Residual Risk: **4 Low**

---

# 123. R023 Treatment

Treatment includes:

- UPS protection

- Cellular capability where applicable

---

# 124. HVAC

Environmental conditions affecting significant Information Technology equipment must be addressed promptly.

Equipment should not be operated in conditions likely to cause damage or create unsafe working conditions.

---

# 125. Utility Failure

During extended utility failure, facility operations should follow [[PLN-002 Business Continuity Plan]].

---

# 126. Facility Availability

Facility availability refers to the ability to safely occupy and use a business location.

---

# 127. System Availability

System availability refers to the ability to access and use required information systems.

---

# 128. Business-Process Availability

Business-process availability refers to the ability to perform the business activity.

---

# 129. Availability Distinction

Loss of a facility does not necessarily mean loss of cloud-hosted systems.

Loss of a system does not necessarily mean the facility is unavailable.

Loss of either may affect business-process availability.

---

- Management
- Other important processes

---

# 154. Exclusive Capability

Important business capability should not depend unnecessarily on one person possessing:

- Credentials
- Documentation
- Configuration knowledge
- Source code
- Recovery information
- Vendor contacts

---

# 155. Emergency Access

Emergency access needed because normal personnel are unavailable must follow [[PROC-005 Emergency Access Procedure]] where applicable.

---

# 156. Physical Security During Continuity Events

Physical-security controls remain applicable during:

- Technology outages
- Facility disruption
- Cloud outage
- Manual processing
- Disaster Recovery
- Cyber incidents

---

# 157. Emergency Conditions

Emergency conditions may require temporary changes to normal physical controls.

Any material exception should be:

- Necessary
- Authorized where practical
- Documented
- Limited in duration
- Reviewed afterward

---

# 158. Evacuation

Facility evacuation requirements take priority over access-control procedures that would delay safe exit.

---

# 159. Emergency Responder Access

Emergency responders must not be obstructed by normal visitor procedures when responding to an emergency.

---

# 160. Reentry

Reentry after an evacuation or facility emergency must follow instructions from:

- Emergency responders
- Landlord
- Property management
- Appropriate company management

as applicable.

---

# 161. Recovery Site Security

Temporary or alternate work locations used during continuity must receive reasonable security appropriate to:

- Information
- Equipment
- Personnel
- Duration
- Business activity

---

# 162. Manual Processing Security

Manual continuity records must be protected according to [[PROC-013 Manual Processing Procedure]].

---

# 163. Backup Media Security

External backup media must be protected according to [[PROC-012 Backup & Recovery Procedure]].

---

# 164. Backup Media Physical Protection

Physical protection of backup media includes:

- Controlled custody
- Encryption
- Off-site separation
- Accountability
- Secure storage
- Secure disposal

---

# 165. Off-Site Backup Provider

Dunder Mifflin uses an off-site Disaster Recovery storage provider for encrypted external backup drives.

The provider normally holds two recent weekly generations for each of seven facilities.

---

# 166. Backup Media Count

Dunder Mifflin maintains:

- 3 encrypted external backup drives per facility
- 7 facilities
- 21 total drives
- Normally 14 drives held off-site

---

# 167. Third-Party Physical Security

Third parties with material physical access must be managed according to:

- [[POL-008 Third-Party Security Policy]]
- [[PROC-015 Visitor & Vendor Access Procedure]]
- Applicable contractual requirements

---

# 168. Third-Party Incidents

A physical-security event involving a third party may require response under [[PBK-005 Third-Party Incident Playbook]].

---

# 169. Physical Security Inspection

Facilities should receive periodic inspection appropriate to risk.

---

# 170. Inspection Scope

Inspection may include:

- Doors
- Locks
- Access-card operation
- Restricted areas
- Warehouse security cages
- Key controls
- Visitor procedures
- Sensitive record storage
- Significant IT areas
- Environmental concerns

---

# 171. Physical Security Deficiency

A physical-security deficiency may include:

- Broken lock
- Failed access-card reader
- Unsecured Restricted area
- Missing key
- Unauthorized access
- Visitor-control failure
- Unsecured high-value inventory
- Environmental hazard
- Repeated tailgating

---

# 172. Immediate Correction

A physical-security deficiency creating immediate material risk should be corrected or temporarily compensated for as soon as practical.

---

# 173. Compensating Physical Control

Temporary compensating controls may include:

- Additional employee presence
- Temporary lock
- Relocation of sensitive assets
- Restricted use of an area
- Increased manual access control
- Other reasonable protection

---

# 174. Finding

A finding is a documented conclusion supported by evidence concerning whether a requirement, control, process, or expected outcome was satisfied.

---

# 175. Finding Examples

Physical and personnel findings may include:

- Unauthorized Restricted-area access
- Unreturned Restricted-area key
- Access retained after termination
- Missing visitor record
- Unescorted visitor in a Restricted area
- Unsecured security cage
- Personnel dependency without sufficient documentation
- Repeated physical-access review failure

---

# 176. Corrective Action

Material deficiencies must identify:

- Finding
- Required corrective action
- Accountable owner
- Due date
- Validation method
- Retest where appropriate

---

# 177. Finding Register

Material deficiencies and corrective actions are tracked in [[REG-004 Finding & Corrective Action Register]].

---

# 178. Corrective Action Validation

A corrective action is not complete merely because the assigned activity has been performed.

Validation must determine whether the identified weakness was corrected.

---

# 179. Physical Security Testing

Physical-security controls may be assessed using:

- Inspection
- Observation
- Interviews
- Sampling
- Record review
- Independent Performance

---

# 180. Independent Performance

An assessor may independently perform an authorized physical-security procedure.

For example:

1. Select a sample of personnel.
2. Identify required physical access.
3. Compare assigned access with business need.
4. Verify Restricted-area authorization.
5. Identify unnecessary access.
6. Document the result.

---

# 181. Termination Testing

An assessor may select a sample of terminated personnel and determine whether applicable:

- Logical access was disabled.
- Access cards were disabled.
- Keys were recovered.
- Restricted access was removed.
- Company equipment was recovered.
- Exceptions were documented.

---

# 182. Visitor-Control Testing

An assessor may sample visitor records and determine whether:

- Visitor identity was recorded.
- A host was identified.
- Access was appropriate.
- Escort requirements were followed.
- Departure was recorded where required.

---

# 183. Restricted-Area Testing

An assessor may compare personnel with Restricted-area access against:

- Job responsibilities
- Management authorization
- Current employment status
- Current business need

---

# 184. Warehouse Cage Testing

An assessor may inspect warehouse security-cage controls and determine whether:

- The enclosure is secured.
- Access is appropriately restricted.
- Authorized access remains current.
- Inventory controls support accountability.

---

# 185. Personnel Dependency Testing

An assessor may determine whether a selected important function can be performed by a qualified alternate using available:

- Documentation
- Credentials
- Procedures
- Configuration information
- Vendor information

---

# 186. Evidence Integrity

Assessment evidence must reflect actual conditions.

Personnel must not:

- Create visitor records after the fact solely to satisfy assessment.
- Backdate access reviews.
- Invent key inventories.
- Conceal access exceptions.
- Record unperformed inspections as complete.
- Alter results to make controls appear effective.

---

# 187. Control Effectiveness

Physical and personnel controls may be assessed as:

- Effective
- Partially Effective
- Ineffective
- Not Implemented

---

# 188. Control Effectiveness Evidence

Effectiveness conclusions should consider:

- Access records
- Access reviews
- Visitor records
- Inspection results
- Termination testing
- Key accountability
- Restricted-area controls
- Warehouse controls
- Personnel dependency testing
- Findings
- Corrective-action results

---

# 189. Metrics

Physical and personnel security metrics may include:

- Physical-access reviews completed
- Unnecessary access identified
- Access cards not promptly disabled
- Unreturned keys
- Visitor-control exceptions
- Restricted-area exceptions
- Physical-security findings
- Overdue corrective actions
- Lost company equipment
- Warehouse security discrepancies
- Personnel-dependency findings

Metric definitions are maintained in [[REG-005 Security Metrics Catalog]].

---

# 190. Key Performance Indicators

Key Performance Indicators (KPIs) may include:

- Percentage of annual physical-access reviews completed
- Percentage of termination physical-access actions completed
- Percentage of material physical-security corrective actions validated
- Percentage of required Restricted-area reviews completed

---

# 191. Key Risk Indicators

Key Risk Indicators (KRIs) may include:

- Terminated personnel retaining facility access
- Missing Restricted-area key
- Unauthorized Restricted-area access
- Repeated visitor-control failure
- Unsecured high-value inventory
- Repeated warehouse discrepancy
- Critical function dependent on one individual
- Material physical-security corrective action overdue

---

# 192. Reporting

Physical and personnel security information should support:

**Operational Reporting**
- Access exceptions
- Visitor issues
- Lost credentials
- Physical deficiencies
- Immediate corrective activity

**Management Reporting**
- Access-review completion
- Termination performance
- Repeated physical deficiencies
- Personnel dependency
- Corrective actions

**Executive Reporting**
- Material physical-security risk
- Significant personnel-security risk
- Major unresolved deficiency
- Required enterprise risk decision

---

# 193. Continuous Monitoring

Continuous monitoring in this program means maintaining risk-appropriate awareness of physical and personnel security conditions.

It does not require continuous surveillance of every employee, room, or facility.

---

# 194. Continuous Monitoring Activities

Activities may include:

- Access review
- Visitor-log review
- Physical inspection
- Lost-credential reporting
- Termination review
- Security-event reporting
- Warehouse reconciliation
- Corrective-action tracking

---

# 195. Current and Proposed Controls

Program reporting must distinguish among:

- Current controls
- Partially implemented controls
- Planned controls
- Implemented controls awaiting validation
- Validated controls

---

# 196. No Premature Risk Credit

Current residual risk must not be reduced merely because:

- A new lock is planned.
- An access review is scheduled.
- A new procedure is written.
- Training is planned.
- A corrective action is assigned.
- A physical-security improvement is approved.

Risk reduction requires implementation and appropriate validation.

---

# 197. Target Residual Risk

Target residual risk represents expected risk after planned treatment.

It is a prediction.

---

# 198. Validated Post-Treatment Risk

After treatment is implemented and validated, applicable risks may be reassessed.

Actual post-treatment risk may be:

- Higher than target
- Equal to target
- Lower than target

---

# 199. Historical Integrity

The original May 2013 risk assessment must remain preserved.

Later validated assessments may establish a new current residual risk without rewriting the historical assessment.

---

# 200. Program Exceptions

Exceptions to this program must be:

- Documented
- Justified
- Risk evaluated
- Approved under appropriate authority
- Time-limited where practical
- Reviewed when conditions change

---

# 201. Program Review

This program must be reviewed when material changes affect:

- Facilities
- Physical-access systems
- Restricted areas
- Warehouse operations
- Personnel processes
- Information Technology infrastructure
- Business Continuity requirements
- Security risk

---

# 202. Traceability

This program supports the enterprise traceability model:

**Business Requirement → Asset / Process → Classification / BIA → Risk → Treatment → Policy / Standard → Control → Procedure → Implementation → Evidence → Test / Audit → Finding → Remediation → Retest → Risk Reassessment**

Physical or personnel security requirements do not become effective controls merely because they are documented.

Implementation and evidence are required before effectiveness can be established.
