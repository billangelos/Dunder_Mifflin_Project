# 1. Purpose

This procedure defines how Dunder Mifflin manages physical access by visitors, vendors, delivery personnel, service providers, consultants, contractors, and other nonemployees.

The procedure establishes requirements for:

- Identification
- Sponsorship
- Authorization
- Visitor logging
- Access limitation
- Escort
- Restricted-area access
- Sensitive information exposure
- Confidentiality
- Temporary credentials
- Departure
- Security-event reporting

---

# 2. Objectives

Visitor and vendor access must support legitimate business activity while protecting:

- Employees
- Company information
- Information systems
- Facilities
- Warehouse inventory
- Sensitive records
- Restricted areas
- Company equipment

---

# 3. Scope

This procedure applies to nonemployee physical access at:

1. Scranton Headquarters
2. Scranton Branch
3. Syracuse Branch
4. Utica Branch
5. Rochester Branch
6. Nashua Branch
7. Akron Branch

---

# 4. Covered Persons

This procedure may apply to:

- Business visitors
- Customers
- Vendors
- Consultants
- Contractors
- Temporary specialists
- Information Technology service providers
- Infinity contractor personnel
- Penetration testers
- Delivery personnel
- Maintenance personnel
- Building service personnel
- Other nonemployees

The controls applied depend on the person's purpose, required access, duration, and potential exposure.

# 6. Safety Priority

Human safety takes priority over normal visitor-control procedures.

This procedure must not delay:

- Emergency evacuation
- Emergency medical response
- Fire response
- Law-enforcement response
- Other necessary emergency activity

---

# 7. Visitor Access Principle

A legitimate reason to enter a Dunder Mifflin facility does not automatically authorize access to every area within that facility.

Visitor access must be limited to what is reasonably necessary for the approved purpose.

---

# 8. Access Zones

Visitor controls recognize three physical zones:

- Public
- Controlled
- Restricted

---

# 9. Public Areas

Authorized visitors may enter designated Public areas without employee-level facility credentials.

Examples may include:

- Reception
- Designated customer-facing areas

Public-area access does not authorize entry into Controlled or Restricted areas.

---

# 10. Controlled Areas

Controlled areas are company work areas not intended for unrestricted public access.

Visitors entering Controlled areas must have:

- Legitimate business purpose
- Appropriate host or sponsor
- Authorization appropriate to the visit
- Escort where required

---

# 11. Restricted Areas

Restricted areas require specific authorization.

Examples include:

- Significant Information Technology areas
- Sensitive records areas
- High-value inventory cages
- Other designated sensitive areas

Visitor or vendor access to a Restricted area must comply with [[PROC-016 Restricted Area Access Procedure]].

---

# 12. Visitor Lifecycle

Visitor access follows:

**Identify → Sponsor → Authorize → Register → Admit → Escort / Control → Monitor as Appropriate → Depart → Close Record**

---

# 13. Sponsor

A visitor entering a nonpublic company area should have an identified Dunder Mifflin sponsor or host.

---

# 14. Sponsor Responsibility

The sponsor is responsible for:

- Confirming the business purpose.
- Identifying required areas.
- Coordinating appropriate authorization.
- Meeting or receiving the visitor.
- Ensuring escort requirements are followed.
- Preventing unnecessary access.
- Reporting material security concerns.

---

# 15. Sponsor Availability

A visitor should not receive access to a nonpublic area merely because the expected sponsor is unavailable.

Another authorized employee may assume sponsor responsibility when appropriate.

---

# 16. Preauthorization

Visits involving:

- Restricted areas
- Significant Information Technology equipment
- Sensitive records
- Privileged technical work
- Significant contractor activity

should be authorized before arrival where practical.

---

# 17. Expected Visitor Information

Where advance coordination is appropriate, the sponsor should provide:

- Visitor name
- Organization
- Purpose
- Expected date
- Expected arrival
- Expected host
- Required area
- Special access requirements

---

# 18. Visitor Identification

Visitors entering nonpublic areas should be identified sufficiently to establish that they are the expected or authorized person.

---

# 19. Identification Method

Identification may be established through:

- Sponsor recognition
- Business identification
- Government-issued identification where appropriate
- Other reasonable verification

based on the circumstances and risk.

---

# 20. Identification Limitation

Identification establishes who the visitor is.

It does not by itself authorize access.

---

# 21. Reception

Public-facing entrances should direct visitors through:

- Reception
- Designated receiving personnel
- Another approved visitor-control process

before entry into nonpublic work areas.

---

# 22. Visitor Registration

Visitors entering Controlled or Restricted areas should be recorded where required by facility procedure.

---

# 23. Visitor Log

A visitor log may record:

- Visitor name
- Organization
- Host
- Business purpose
- Arrival time
- Departure time

Additional information should be collected only when reasonably necessary.

---

# 24. Visitor Log Protection

Visitor records may contain Internal or personal information and must not be unnecessarily exposed.

---

# 25. Visitor Record Accuracy

Visitor records must reflect actual visits.

Personnel must not:

- Create fictional visitor entries.
- Backdate visitor records to satisfy an assessment.
- Alter visitor times to conceal an exception.
- Record an escort that did not occur.

---

# 26. Temporary Visitor Credential

Where temporary visitor credentials are used, they should:

- Be identifiable as temporary where practical.
- Provide only required access.
- Be returned when the visit ends.
- Not be intentionally transferred to another visitor.

---

# 27. Temporary Credential Issuance

Before issuing a temporary credential, responsible personnel should confirm:

- Visitor identity
- Sponsor
- Business purpose
- Required access

---

# 28. Temporary Credential Scope

A temporary visitor credential must not provide broader access than reasonably necessary.

---

# 29. Temporary Credential Expiration

Temporary electronic access should expire or be disabled when the authorized visit or access period ends.

---

# 30. Lost Visitor Credential

A lost visitor credential must be reported promptly.

Where possible, an electronic credential should be disabled.

---

# 31. Escort

Visitors without independent authorization for a Controlled or Restricted area must be escorted where required.

---

# 32. Escort Responsibility

The escort should:

- Keep the visitor within authorized areas.
- Prevent unnecessary access to sensitive information or equipment.
- Ensure applicable Restricted-area controls are followed.
- Return the visitor to the appropriate exit or reception area when the visit ends.

---

# 33. Escort Transfer

Escort responsibility may be transferred to another authorized employee when:

- The new escort understands the visitor's purpose.
- The new escort is authorized for the area.
- The visitor remains appropriately controlled.

---

# 34. Unescorted Access

Unescorted nonemployee access may be permitted only when specifically authorized based on:

- Business need
- Access scope
- Duration
- Area
- Risk

---

# 35. Unescorted Access Does Not Equal Employee Access

Authorization for a nonemployee to move independently within one area does not grant general employee-equivalent facility access.

---

# 36. Restricted-Area Escort

Visitors and vendors entering Restricted areas should be escorted unless specific independent access has been authorized.

---

# 37. Access Limitation

Visitors must not enter areas unrelated to their authorized business purpose merely because:

- A door is open.
- Another employee enters.
- The visitor is familiar with the facility.
- The visitor has previously received access.
- A temporary credential technically permits entry.

---

# 38. Sensitive Information Exposure

Hosts and escorts should take reasonable steps to prevent unnecessary visitor exposure to:

- Confidential information
- Restricted information
- Passwords
- Authentication information
- Customer records
- Employee records
- Financial information
- Sensitive system information
- Other protected company information

---

# 39. Sensitive Paper Information

Sensitive paper records should not be left unnecessarily exposed to visitors.

---

# 40. Computer Screens

Employees should take reasonable precautions when sensitive information displayed on a workstation may be visible to a visitor.

---

# 41. Sensitive Conversations

Sensitive discussions should not be conducted unnecessarily in front of visitors who are not authorized to receive the information.

---

# 42. Confidentiality Requirement

Before a nonemployee or outside party receives access to sensitive company information, an appropriate Non-Disclosure Agreement (NDA) or equivalent enforceable confidentiality obligation must be in place.

---

# 43. Confidentiality Is Access-Based

The confidentiality requirement depends on:

- Information access
- Information sensitivity
- Nature of the work

rather than the person's vendor title alone.

---

# 44. Parties Commonly Requiring Confidentiality Protection

Depending on access, this may include:

- Consultants
- Information Technology service providers
- Infinity contractors
- Penetration testers
- Temporary specialists
- Service personnel with sensitive access
- Other outside support personnel

---

# 45. Ordinary Visitors

An ordinary visitor who does not receive sensitive information access does not automatically require an NDA.

---

# 46. Delivery Personnel

Ordinary delivery personnel do not automatically require an NDA solely because they deliver or collect goods.

---

# 47. Maintenance Personnel

Ordinary building maintenance personnel do not automatically require an NDA unless their work provides access to sensitive information.

---

# 48. Changing Access Scope

If a visitor's work changes and sensitive information access becomes necessary, the confidentiality requirement must be satisfied before that access is provided.

---

# 49. NDA Limitation

An NDA does not establish that:

- Facility access is appropriate.
- Restricted-area access is appropriate.
- Technical access is appropriate.
- The third party is competent.
- Security controls are effective.
- Vendor oversight is sufficient.
- Testing is adequate.

---

# 50. Vendor Access

Vendor personnel must receive only the access required for the authorized service.

---

# 51. Vendor Sponsor

A vendor requiring access to a nonpublic company area must have an appropriate company contact or sponsor.

---

# 52. Vendor Access Duration

Vendor access should be limited to the time reasonably necessary to perform the approved work.

---

# 53. Vendor Access Scope

Vendor access should identify:

- Facility
- Work area
- Required equipment
- Required duration
- Sensitive information exposure
- Restricted-area requirement

where applicable.

---

# 54. Vendor Personnel Change

Authorization for one vendor employee does not automatically authorize another employee from the same vendor.

---

# 55. Service Personnel

Service personnel working on:

- Information Technology equipment
- Network equipment
- Electrical systems
- HVAC
- Security systems
- Other sensitive infrastructure

must receive access appropriate to the work being performed.

---

# 56. Restricted Service Work

Service work in a Restricted area requires specific authorization.

---

# 57. Service Personnel Escort

Service personnel should be escorted in Restricted areas unless specifically authorized for independent access.

---

# 58. Service Completion

When service work is complete, the responsible employee should confirm as appropriate that:

- The visitor has left the work area.
- Temporary credentials are returned.
- Restricted areas are secured.
- Company equipment remains accounted for.
- Material security concerns are reported.

---

# 59. Delivery Personnel

Delivery personnel should normally remain within:

- Receiving
- Shipping
- Loading
- Other designated delivery areas

required for the transaction.

---

# 60. Delivery Access Limitation

Delivery personnel should not receive unnecessary access to:

- Office workspaces
- Sensitive records
- Significant Information Technology areas
- High-value inventory cages

---

# 61. Warehouse Deliveries

Warehouse receiving and shipping personnel are responsible for controlling delivery access within warehouse operations.

---

# 62. Delivery Documentation

Normal business records should support accountability for:

- Goods received
- Goods shipped
- Returns
- Other material inventory movement

---

# 63. High-Value Inventory Cage

Delivery personnel must not receive access to a locked high-value inventory cage unless specifically required and authorized.

---

# 64. Inventory Security

Visitor and vendor controls support inventory security but do not replace:

- Warehouse Management System records
- Receiving records
- Shipping records
- Inventory counts
- Reconciliation
- Investigation of discrepancies

---

# 65. Consultants

Consultants requiring recurring access must still receive access based on:

- Business need
- Sponsor
- Required area
- Required duration
- Information sensitivity

---

# 66. Contractor Access

Contractors requiring logical or recurring physical access must also comply with [[PROC-004 Contractor Access Procedure]].

---

# 67. Infinity Contractor

Infinity contractor personnel may require physical access to Scranton Headquarters for authorized application-support activity.

---

# 68. Infinity Contractor Workspace

Privileged Infinity contractor production or administrative work is performed from a designated controlled workspace at Scranton Headquarters when required.

---

# 69. Infinity Contractor Notice

Expected Infinity contractor personnel should be identified at least one business day before scheduled arrival where practical.

---

# 70. Infinity Emergency Exception

An emergency may require contractor personnel who could not reasonably be identified one business day in advance.

The exception must be documented.

---

# 71. Infinity Contractor Verification

Upon arrival, contractor personnel should be matched with the expected:

- Individual
- Contractor organization
- Sponsor
- Business purpose

before access is provided.

---

# 72. Infinity Contractor Physical Scope

Infinity contractor access must be limited to locations required for the authorized work.

---

# 73. Infinity Contractor Technical Access

Physical entry into Scranton Headquarters does not itself authorize logical or privileged access.

Technical access remains governed by:

- [[PROC-004 Contractor Access Procedure]]
- [[PROC-020 Contractor Development Access Procedure]]
- [[POL-003 Access Control Policy]]

---

# 74. No General Contractor VPN

The Infinity contractor does not receive general VPN access for privileged production administration.

Routine external remote privileged administration is not authorized.

---

# 75. Penetration Testers

External penetration testers may require:

- Facility access
- Network access
- Test-system access
- Sensitive technical information

depending on the authorized assessment.

---

# 76. Penetration Tester Authorization

Physical access for a penetration tester must not be interpreted as authorization to test systems outside the approved assessment scope.

---

# 77. Penetration Tester Confidentiality

Appropriate confidentiality obligations must be established before penetration testers receive sensitive company information or system access.

---

# 78. Penetration Testing Coordination

Penetration testing must comply with [[STD-007 Penetration Testing Standard]].

---

# 79. Temporary Specialists

Temporary specialists requiring access to sensitive information or Restricted areas must receive controls appropriate to the access rather than being treated as ordinary visitors.

---

# 80. Building Personnel

Landlord or property-management personnel may require access for:

- Inspection
- Repair
- Emergency response
- Building maintenance

---

# 81. Building Personnel Access

Building personnel access to company-controlled Restricted areas should be:

- Limited to legitimate building need.
- Coordinated with company personnel where practical.
- Escorted where appropriate.

---

# 82. Emergency Building Access

An urgent building emergency may require immediate landlord or emergency-service access.

Normal advance visitor procedures must not obstruct necessary emergency action.

---

# 83. Post-Emergency Review

After material emergency access to a Restricted area, responsible management should determine whether:

- Sensitive assets were affected.
- Information was exposed.
- Equipment was altered.
- Additional security review is required.

---

# 84. Law Enforcement

Law-enforcement access must be handled in accordance with:

- Safety requirements
- Applicable law
- Legal guidance
- Business requirements

as circumstances permit.

---

# 85. Legal Requests

Requests involving:

- Search
- Seizure
- Legal process
- Sensitive company records

should be referred to Counsel when circumstances permit.

---

# 86. Emergency Responders

Emergency responders must receive necessary access during a genuine emergency.

Normal visitor registration must not delay life-safety response.

---

# 87. Photography

Visitors should not photograph sensitive:

- Information
- Systems
- Restricted areas
- Records
- Security controls

without authorization.

---

# 88. Recording

Audio or video recording in nonpublic areas should require appropriate authorization when sensitive business activity may be captured.

---

# 89. Personal Electronic Devices

Visitor possession of personal electronic devices does not automatically authorize:

- Connection to company systems
- Connection to company networks
- Photography
- Recording
- Use within Restricted areas

---

# 90. Guest Wireless

Scranton Headquarters maintains one ceiling-mounted wireless access point in the lobby for Internet-only guest wireless access.

---

# 91. Guest Wireless Limitation

Guest wireless access does not provide authorization to:

- Internal company systems
- Administrative systems
- Restricted information
- Company network resources

---

# 92. Other Facilities

No corporate wireless network is maintained at the other Dunder Mifflin facilities.

---

# 93. Visitor Network Access

Visitors must not connect unauthorized devices to:

- Internal wired network ports
- Company workstations
- Network equipment
- Other internal technology resources

---

# 94. Visitor Use of Company Equipment

Visitor use of company equipment must be specifically authorized.

---

# 95. Restricted IT Areas

Visitors requiring access to significant Information Technology areas must comply with [[PROC-016 Restricted Area Access Procedure]].

---

# 96. Sensitive Records Areas

Visitor access to areas containing sensitive employee, customer, financial, or legal records must be limited to authorized business need.

---

# 97. Reception Responsibility

Reception or designated receiving personnel should:

1. Determine whom the visitor is meeting.
2. Contact the host where required.
3. Complete applicable visitor registration.
4. Prevent unauthorized movement into nonpublic areas.
5. Issue temporary credentials where applicable.
6. Direct the visitor to the host or waiting area.

---

# 98. Host Receipt

The host should meet or receive the visitor before the visitor proceeds into nonpublic areas unless another approved process is in place.

---

# 99. Visitor Movement

The visitor should remain within areas appropriate to the approved purpose.

---

# 100. Unattended Visitor

A visitor requiring escort should not be intentionally left unattended in a Controlled or Restricted area.

---

# 101. Host Temporarily Unavailable

If the host must leave temporarily, the visitor should:

- Remain with another authorized escort.
- Return to an appropriate waiting area.
- Leave the Controlled or Restricted area.

as appropriate.

---

# 102. Unexpected Visitor

An unexpected visitor should not receive nonpublic access solely because the person claims to have an appointment or business relationship.

---

# 103. Unexpected Vendor

For an unexpected vendor or service technician:

1. Identify the individual.
2. Identify the organization.
3. Confirm the business purpose.
4. Contact the responsible employee or manager.
5. Authorize access before entry into nonpublic areas.

---

# 104. Unverified Visitor

If the visitor's identity, sponsor, or purpose cannot reasonably be verified, nonpublic access should not be provided.

---

# 105. Refused Access

A person refused access should not be allowed to bypass the decision through another entrance or employee.

Suspicious attempts should be reported.

---

# 106. Tailgating

Visitors must not enter Controlled or Restricted areas by following an authorized employee through an access-controlled entrance without authorization.

---

# 107. Employee Responsibility

Employees should not knowingly permit an unknown or unauthorized visitor to tailgate into a nonpublic area.

---

# 108. Suspicious Visitor Behavior

Suspicious behavior may include:

- Attempting unauthorized doors
- Avoiding reception
- Misrepresenting identity
- Attempting to use another person's credential
- Entering unrelated work areas
- Photographing sensitive areas without authorization
- Attempting to access Information Technology equipment
- Refusing reasonable visitor procedures

---

# 109. Safety During Challenge

Employees should not create unnecessary personal danger when responding to suspicious visitor behavior.

---

# 110. Security Escalation

Suspicious visitor or vendor activity should be reported to:

- Management
- Facility responsibility
- Information Technology / Information Security where relevant
- Law enforcement where necessary

---

# 111. Information Security Event

A visitor-related event must be escalated under [[PROC-010 Security Event Escalation Procedure]] when it may involve:

- Information compromise
- System access
- Credential compromise
- Device theft
- Unauthorized technical activity
- Other information-security impact

---

# 112. Incident Response

A material visitor or vendor security incident may require activation of [[PLN-001 Incident Response Plan]].

---

# 113. Third-Party Incident

A security incident involving a vendor, contractor, or other third party may require [[PBK-005 Third-Party Incident Playbook]].

---

# 114. Evidence

Potential visitor or vendor incident evidence may include:

- Visitor log
- Temporary credential record
- Access-control logs
- Sponsor statement
- Witness statements
- Available landlord CCTV
- Service records
- Delivery records
- Physical evidence
- Related system logs

---

# 115. Evidence Handling

Incident evidence must be handled according to [[PROC-011 Evidence Handling Procedure]].

---

# 116. Landlord CCTV

Relevant landlord-controlled CCTV may be requested when appropriate.

Availability, retention, and useful coverage must not be assumed.

---

# 117. Departure

When a visitor's authorized activity is complete, the visitor should leave the nonpublic area.

---

# 118. Visitor Checkout

Where visitor logging is used, departure should be recorded.

---

# 119. Temporary Credential Return

Temporary visitor credentials should be returned at the end of the visit.

---

# 120. Missing Temporary Credential

A missing temporary credential should be:

1. Reported.
2. Identified.
3. Disabled where possible.
4. Evaluated for access risk.
5. Documented where material.

---

# 121. Vendor Work Completion

When significant vendor work is completed, the responsible employee should confirm as appropriate:

- Work is complete.
- Temporary physical access is no longer required.
- Temporary credentials are returned or disabled.
- Restricted areas are secured.
- Temporary logical access is addressed separately.
- Material changes are documented.

---

# 122. Logical Access Removal

End of a physical visit does not automatically demonstrate that temporary logical access has been removed.

Logical access must be managed under applicable Identity and Access Management procedures.

---

# 123. Physical and Logical Access Coordination

Where a vendor receives both physical and logical access, both must be independently addressed when the work ends.

---

# 124. Recurring Vendors

Recurring vendor relationships do not eliminate visitor or access-control requirements.

Controls may be streamlined when justified, but access must remain:

- Authorized
- Appropriately scoped
- Current
- Reviewable

---

# 125. Long-Term Contractors

Long-term contractors with recurring facility access must be managed through the contractor access process rather than relying solely on ordinary visitor procedures.

---

# 126. Third-Party Security Classification

Third parties are categorized under [[POL-008 Third-Party Security Policy]] as:

- Critical
- Important
- Routine

where applicable.

---

# 127. Third-Party Classification and Physical Access

Third-party classification may affect oversight, but physical access must still be based on the actual access required.

A Critical provider does not automatically receive broader physical access.

---

# 128. Third-Party Due Diligence

Visitor processing does not replace required third-party due diligence.

---

# 129. Physical Access Does Not Establish Trust

Successful visitor registration establishes that the visitor completed the required entry process.

It does not establish that:

- The vendor is secure.
- The vendor's systems are secure.
- Technical activity is authorized.
- Contractual requirements are satisfied.
- The vendor's personnel are trustworthy beyond the required business determination.

---

# 130. Visitor Records

Visitor records should be retained according to applicable business, legal, and security requirements.

---

# 131. Visitor Record Access

Visitor records should be available only to personnel with legitimate:

- Operational
- Security
- Management
- Legal

need.

---

# 132. Visitor Record Review

Visitor records may be reviewed for:

- Security investigations
- Physical-access assessments
- Repeated visitor-control exceptions
- Other legitimate business purposes

---

# 133. Vendor Access Records

Material vendor-access records should provide sufficient evidence to determine:

- Who received access
- Why access was required
- When access occurred
- Who sponsored the access
- Whether Restricted access was involved

where applicable.

---

# 134. Visitor Access Review

Visitor processes should be periodically reviewed for compliance with this procedure.

---

# 135. Review Scope

Review may include:

- Visitor logs
- Temporary credentials
- Escort practices
- Vendor access
- Restricted-area visits
- Missing credentials
- Security incidents
- Repeated exceptions

---

# 136. Visitor-Control Finding

Potential findings include:

- Missing visitor record
- Missing sponsor
- Unescorted visitor where escort was required
- Unauthorized Restricted-area access
- Temporary credential not returned
- Sensitive information exposed without required confidentiality protection
- Vendor access extending beyond business need
- Repeated tailgating

---

# 137. Finding

A finding is a documented conclusion supported by evidence concerning whether a requirement, control, process, or expected outcome was satisfied.

---

# 138. Corrective Action

Material visitor or vendor deficiencies must identify:

- Finding
- Corrective action
- Accountable owner
- Due date
- Validation method
- Retest where appropriate

---

# 139. Finding Register

Material findings and corrective actions are tracked in [[REG-004 Finding & Corrective Action Register]].

---

# 140. Corrective Action Validation

Corrective action must be validated.

Examples include:

- Confirming temporary access was disabled.
- Confirming an access process was corrected.
- Confirming Restricted access was removed.
- Confirming missing credentials were addressed.
- Confirming required confidentiality protection was established.

---

# 141. Assessment

Visitor and vendor controls are subject to assurance under [[PRO-008 Security Assurance & Continuous Improvement Program]].

---

# 142. Assessment Methods

Assessment may use:

- Inspection
- Observation
- Interviews
- Sampling
- Record review
- Independent Performance

---

# 143. Independent Performance

An assessor may independently perform the visitor-control review procedure.

For example:

1. Select a sample of visitor records.
2. Identify the visitor.
3. Confirm the host or sponsor.
4. Confirm business purpose.
5. Determine whether the visitor entered a Controlled or Restricted area.
6. Verify applicable authorization.
7. Verify escort where required.
8. Verify departure or credential return where required.
9. Document the result.

---

# 144. Vendor Sample

For a vendor sample, the assessor may additionally determine whether:

- Vendor access was appropriate.
- Restricted-area access was specifically authorized.
- Sensitive information access had appropriate confidentiality protection.
- Temporary access ended when no longer required.

---

# 145. Restricted-Area Sample

An assessor may select visitor records involving Restricted areas and determine whether:

- Specific authorization existed.
- Escort requirements were followed.
- Access remained within approved scope.

---

# 146. Confidentiality Sample

An assessor may select nonemployee access involving sensitive information and determine whether an appropriate NDA or equivalent enforceable confidentiality obligation existed before access.

---

# 147. Evidence Integrity

Assessment evidence must reflect actual conditions.

Personnel must not:

- Invent visitor records.
- Backdate visitor entries.
- Add a sponsor after the fact solely to satisfy assessment.
- Record an escort that did not occur.
- Conceal lost credentials.
- Alter findings to make controls appear effective.

---

# 148. Control Effectiveness

Visitor and vendor controls may be assessed as:

- Effective
- Partially Effective
- Ineffective
- Not Implemented

---

# 149. Effectiveness Evidence

Control effectiveness may consider:

- Visitor records
- Sponsor records
- Escort compliance
- Temporary credential handling
- Restricted-area authorization
- Confidentiality evidence
- Security events
- Findings
- Corrective-action validation

---

# 150. Metrics

Visitor and vendor metrics may include:

- Visitors processed
- Visitor records missing required information
- Escort exceptions
- Missing temporary credentials
- Unauthorized Restricted-area attempts
- Vendor-access exceptions
- Confidentiality exceptions
- Repeated visitor-control findings
- Overdue corrective actions

Metric definitions are maintained in [[REG-005 Security Metrics Catalog]].

---

# 151. Key Performance Indicators

Key Performance Indicators (KPIs) may include:

- Percentage of sampled visitor records meeting requirements
- Percentage of Restricted-area visits with required authorization
- Percentage of identified temporary access removed when no longer required
- Percentage of material corrective actions validated

---

# 152. Key Risk Indicators

Key Risk Indicators (KRIs) may include:

- Unescorted visitor in a Restricted area
- Unauthorized vendor access
- Missing temporary credential
- Sensitive information exposed without required confidentiality protection
- Repeated visitor-control failure
- Overdue material visitor-access finding

---

# 154. R024 Treatment Relationship

This procedure supports R024 treatment through:

- Reception

- Visitor logging
- Sponsorship

- Access limitation
- Temporary credential control
- Departure control

---

# 155. R018 Third-Party Technology Failure / Compromise

R018 has:

- Inherent Risk: **12 High**
- Current Residual Risk: **8 Moderate**
- Target Residual Risk: **6 Moderate**

Visitor and vendor controls support physical aspects of third-party risk but do not replace the broader third-party security program.

---

# 156. R019 Infinity Contractor

R019 has:

- Inherent Risk: **12 High**
- Current Residual Risk: **8 Moderate**
- Target Residual Risk: **4 Low**

# 157. R019 Treatment Relationship

This procedure supports R019 through:

- Sponsor requirements
- Contractor identification
- Advance personnel notice where practical
- Controlled Headquarters workspace
- Physical access limitation
- Restricted-area controls
- Confidentiality requirements
- Coordination with contractor logical-access controls

# 158. R032 Inventory Theft

R032 has:

- Inherent Risk: **12 High**
- Current Residual Risk: **6 Moderate**
- Target Residual Risk: **3 Low**

Delivery and vendor access controls support R032 by limiting unnecessary warehouse and high-value inventory access.

---

# 159. R012 Malicious Insider

R012 primarily addresses malicious action by legitimately authorized personnel.

Visitor controls should not be used to obscure the distinction between unauthorized physical access and malicious use of legitimate access.

---

# 160. Current and Proposed Controls

Reporting must distinguish among:

- Current controls
- Partially implemented controls
- Planned controls
- Implemented controls awaiting validation
- Validated controls

---

# 161. No Premature Risk Credit

Current residual risk must not be reduced merely because:

- A visitor procedure exists.
- A visitor log is created.
- A new credential system is planned.
- An escort rule is documented.
- A corrective action is assigned.

Risk reduction requires implementation and appropriate validation.

---

# 162. Target Residual Risk

Target residual risk represents expected risk after planned treatment.

It is a prediction rather than proof of future control effectiveness.

---

# 163. Validated Post-Treatment Risk

After controls are implemented and validated, applicable risks may be reassessed.

Actual validated risk may be:

- Higher than target
- Equal to target
- Lower than target

---

# 164. Historical Integrity

The original May 2013 risk assessment must remain preserved.

Later validated assessments may establish a new current residual risk without rewriting the historical assessment.

---

# 165. Exceptions

Exceptions to this procedure must be:

- Documented
- Justified
- Risk evaluated
- Approved under appropriate authority
- Time-limited where practical
- Reviewed when conditions change

---

# 166. Emergency Exceptions

An emergency may make normal visitor registration or advance authorization impractical.

Life safety and emergency response take priority.

Material deviations should be documented after the event where practical.

---

# 167. Procedure Review

This procedure must be reviewed when material changes affect:

- Visitor processing
- Vendor access
- Facility access
- Restricted areas
- Contractor activity
- Reception
- Third-party security requirements
- Physical-security risk

---

# 168. Traceability

This procedure supports the enterprise traceability model:

**Business Requirement → Asset / Process → Classification / BIA → Risk → Treatment → Policy / Standard → Control → Procedure → Implementation → Evidence → Test / Audit → Finding → Remediation → Retest → Risk Reassessment**

Visitor security is not demonstrated merely by having a visitor log.

Effective visitor control requires that the visitor be:

**Identified → Sponsored → Authorized → Appropriately Limited → Escorted Where Required → Accounted for at Departure**
