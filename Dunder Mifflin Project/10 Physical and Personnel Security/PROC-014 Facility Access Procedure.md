# 1. Purpose

This procedure defines how Dunder Mifflin requests, authorizes, issues, uses, reviews, modifies, and revokes physical access to company-controlled areas.

The procedure establishes consistent physical-access practices while supporting:

- Employee safety
- Business operations
- Individual accountability
- Protection of information
- Protection of equipment
- Protection of inventory
- Restricted-area security
- Personnel lifecycle management

---

# 2. Scope

This procedure applies to physical access at:

1. Scranton Headquarters
2. Scranton Branch
3. Syracuse Branch
4. Utica Branch
5. Rochester Branch
6. Nashua Branch
7. Akron Branch

It applies to:

- Employees
- Contractors
- Temporary personnel
- Visitors where applicable
- Vendors where applicable
- Other authorized personnel

---

# 3. Authority

This procedure operates under:

- [[POL-001 Information Security Policy]]
- [[POL-003 Access Control Policy]]
- [[POL-011 Physical & Environmental Security Policy]]
- [[PRO-006 Physical & Personnel Security Program]]

---

# 4. Safety Priority

Human safety takes priority over physical access control.

Physical-security controls must not prevent or unnecessarily delay:

- Emergency evacuation
- Emergency medical response
- Fire response
- Law-enforcement response
- Other required emergency activity

---

# 5. Facility Model

Dunder Mifflin operates from leased facilities.

Physical access is a shared responsibility between:

- Dunder Mifflin
- Landlords
- Property managers
- Building management

depending on the facility and area.

---

# 6. Company-Controlled Access

Dunder Mifflin controls access to company work areas to the extent provided by the leased facility.

Landlord-controlled common areas remain dependent on building-management controls.

---

# 7. Access Zones

Physical access is organized into:

- Public
- Controlled
- Restricted

areas.

---

# 8. Public Areas

Public areas may be entered by authorized members of the public without employee-level facility credentials.

Examples may include:

- Reception
- Designated customer-facing areas

Public-area access does not authorize access to Controlled or Restricted areas.

---

# 9. Controlled Areas

Controlled areas are company workspaces not intended for unrestricted public access.

Examples include:

- General offices
- Employee work areas
- Warehouse operating areas
- Internal company corridors

---

# 10. Restricted Areas

Restricted areas require specific authorization based on business need.

Examples include:

- Significant Information Technology areas
- Sensitive records areas
- High-value inventory cages
- Other designated sensitive areas

---

# 11. Facility Access Lifecycle

Physical access follows:

**Request → Authorize → Issue → Use → Review → Modify → Revoke**

---

# 12. Access Principle

Physical access must be based on legitimate business need.

Employment by Dunder Mifflin does not automatically authorize access to every:

- Facility
- Warehouse
- Restricted area
- Office
- Secured enclosure

---

# 13. Access Request

A request for physical access should identify:

- Person requiring access
- Employment or engagement status
- Facility
- Required area
- Business reason
- Expected duration where temporary
- Requesting manager or sponsor

---

# 14. Employee Access Request

The employee's manager is responsible for identifying the physical access required for the employee's job.

---

# 15. Contractor Access Request

Contractor physical access must have an identified company sponsor.

The request should identify:

- Contractor
- Company
- Business purpose
- Required location
- Required duration
- Sensitive areas involved

---

# 16. Temporary Personnel

Temporary personnel must receive only the access required for the authorized assignment.

Temporary status does not justify unrestricted facility access.

---

# 17. Access Authorization

Physical access must be approved by appropriate:

- Management
- Facility responsibility
- Area owner

based on the requested access.

---

# 18. Restricted Access Authorization

Restricted-area access requires specific approval from the responsible area owner or designated management authority.

Detailed requirements are established by [[PROC-016 Restricted Area Access Procedure]].

---

# 19. Least Physical Access

Personnel must receive only the physical access reasonably required to perform assigned duties.

---

# 20. Access Duration

Access may be:

- Ongoing
- Temporary
- Time-limited
- Event-specific

depending on business need.

---

# 21. Temporary Access

Temporary physical access should have an identifiable end condition.

Examples include:

- End date
- Project completion
- Service visit completion
- Contractor engagement expiration

---

# 22. Access Credential Types

Physical access credentials may include:

- Electronic access card
- Physical key
- Other approved facility credential

---

# 23. Credential Assignment

Where a credential is assigned to an individual, the assignment should identify:

- Individual
- Credential
- Facility
- Authorized access
- Issue date

where applicable.

---

# 24. Credential Ownership

Company-issued access credentials remain company-controlled assets.

---

# 25. Credential Protection

Personnel must reasonably protect assigned access credentials from:

- Loss
- Theft
- Unauthorized use
- Intentional sharing

---

# 26. Credential Sharing

Individually assigned access credentials must not be intentionally shared unless a specifically approved operational process requires otherwise.

---

# 27. Access Card Issuance

Before issuing an access card, responsible personnel should confirm:

- Individual identity
- Employment or engagement status
- Required facility
- Authorized access scope

---

# 28. Access Card Activation

Electronic access cards should be activated only for approved access.

---

# 29. Access Card Record

Where applicable, the access-card record should identify:

- Card holder
- Card identifier
- Authorized facility or areas
- Activation status

---

# 30. Key Issuance

Keys providing access to Controlled or Restricted areas must be issued only to authorized personnel.

---

# 31. Key Record

Important key assignments should identify:

- Person
- Key or key group
- Area accessible
- Issue date

where practical.

---

# 32. Master or Sensitive Keys

Keys providing broad or Restricted-area access require stronger accountability than ordinary low-risk keys.

---

# 33. Rear Employee Entrance

Where a rear employee entrance uses electronic card access, only authorized personnel should use that entrance for independent employee access.

---

# 34. Public Entrance

Public-facing entrances should direct visitors through:

- Reception
- Designated receiving personnel
- Another approved visitor-control process

before entering nonpublic areas.

---

# 35. Visitor Access

Visitor access is governed by [[PROC-015 Visitor & Vendor Access Procedure]].

---

# 36. Visitor Credentials

Temporary visitor credentials, where used, must not provide broader access than necessary.

---

# 37. Visitor Escort

A visitor without independent authorization for a Controlled or Restricted area must be escorted where required.

---

# 38. Vendor Access

Vendors do not automatically receive employee-equivalent facility access.

Vendor access must be based on:

- Business purpose
- Required area
- Required duration
- Sponsor
- Information or equipment exposure

---

# 39. Delivery Access

Delivery personnel should receive only the physical access required for:

- Delivery
- Pickup
- Receiving
- Shipping

activity.

---

# 40. Warehouse Access

Warehouse access must be limited to personnel with a legitimate:

- Operational
- Management
- Support
- Delivery
- Service

need.

---

# 41. Warehouse Security Cage

Access to locked high-value inventory cages or enclosures must be specifically limited to authorized personnel.

---

# 42. Cage Access

General warehouse access does not automatically authorize security-cage access.

---

# 43. Significant IT Areas

Areas containing significant Information Technology infrastructure should be Restricted where appropriate.

---

# 44. IT Area Access

Access to significant IT areas should be limited to personnel requiring:

- Administration
- Maintenance
- Support
- Authorized service

---

# 45. Non-IT Personnel

Non-IT personnel requiring temporary access to a Restricted IT area should be:

- Specifically authorized
- Escorted where appropriate
- Limited to the required work

---

# 46. Service Personnel

Service personnel working in a Restricted area must have:

- Business justification
- Authorization
- Appropriate supervision or escort where required

---

# 47. Confidentiality

If physical access exposes a nonemployee to sensitive information, an appropriate Non-Disclosure Agreement (NDA) or equivalent enforceable confidentiality obligation must be in place before that access.

---

# 48. Confidentiality Limitation

An NDA does not itself authorize facility or Restricted-area access.

Physical access requires separate authorization.

---

# 49. Access Use

Personnel must use physical access only for authorized business purposes.

---

# 50. Access Beyond Job Need

Personnel must not use an assigned credential to enter an area solely because the credential technically permits entry when there is no legitimate business reason.

---

# 51. Tailgating

Personnel must not knowingly permit unauthorized individuals to follow them into Controlled or Restricted areas.

---

# 52. Door Propping

Access-controlled doors must not be intentionally propped open in a manner that defeats required access control except when:

- Operationally necessary
- Appropriately supervised
- Temporarily authorized

---

# 53. Emergency Door Conditions

Emergency or fire-exit requirements must not be altered in a manner that interferes with safe evacuation.

---

# 54. Suspicious Entry Attempt

Suspected unauthorized entry should be reported when safe and appropriate.

Examples include:

- Repeated access-card failure
- Attempted use of another person's credential
- Forced door
- Tailgating
- Unknown person in a nonpublic area
- Attempted entry into a Restricted area

---

# 55. Employee Challenge

Employees may question an unknown individual in a Controlled area when safe and appropriate.

Employees must not place themselves at unnecessary personal risk.

---

# 56. Escalation

Suspicious physical-access events with security implications must be escalated under [[PROC-010 Security Event Escalation Procedure]].

---

# 57. Physical Security Incident

A significant unauthorized physical-access event may require activation of [[PLN-001 Incident Response Plan]].

---

# 58. Access Records

Physical-access records should be maintained where supported by the applicable access-control mechanism.

---

# 59. Electronic Access Records

Electronic access systems may provide records such as:

- Credential identifier
- Door
- Date
- Time
- Successful access
- Failed access

---

# 60. Access Record Limitations

An electronic access record demonstrates use of a credential.

It does not automatically prove which individual physically passed through the door.

---

# 61. Access Log Review

Access logs may be reviewed when:

- Investigating a security event
- Investigating lost credentials
- Validating access-control operation
- Performing an authorized assessment

Routine review should remain proportionate to risk.

---

# 62. No Indiscriminate Surveillance

Physical-access records must not be interpreted as authorization for unnecessary continuous employee surveillance.

---

# 63. Lost Access Card

A lost or stolen access card must be reported promptly.

---

# 64. Lost Access Card Response

Responsible personnel should:

1. Identify the card.
2. Determine its access scope.
3. Disable the card promptly where possible.
4. Issue replacement access if authorized.
5. Document material circumstances.
6. Escalate suspicious loss where appropriate.

---

# 65. Found Access Card

A found access card should be returned to appropriate company personnel.

It should not be used to determine where it provides access.

---

# 66. Lost Key

A lost or stolen key must be reported promptly.

---

# 67. Lost Key Assessment

The responsible manager should determine:

- Which areas the key accesses
- Whether the area is Restricted
- Whether the key can be uniquely identified
- Whether unauthorized possession creates material risk
- Whether lock replacement or rekeying is required
- Whether temporary compensating controls are required

---

# 68. Restricted-Area Key Loss

Loss of a key providing significant Restricted-area access requires prompt security evaluation.

---

# 69. Compensating Controls

Temporary compensating controls for lost credentials may include:

- Increased employee presence
- Temporary lock
- Restricted area use
- Manual access control
- Relocation of sensitive assets

until permanent correction is completed.

---

# 70. Damaged Credential

A damaged or malfunctioning access credential should be replaced through an authorized process.

---

# 71. Access Failure

Failure of an electronic access-control system must not automatically result in unrestricted facility access.

---

# 72. Access System Outage

During an access-control system outage, temporary procedures may include:

- Manual employee verification
- Reception control
- Manager authorization
- Manual access logs
- Temporary key control

based on facility conditions.

---

# 73. Emergency Access During Outage

Emergency responders must receive necessary access during an emergency without being delayed by normal administrative procedures.

---

# 74. Temporary Manual Access Record

When electronic access controls are unavailable, significant manual access may be recorded with:

- Name
- Date
- Time
- Area
- Authorization

where practical.

---

# 75. Access Modification

Physical access must be reviewed when:

- Job responsibilities change
- Facility assignment changes
- Work location changes
- Restricted-area need changes
- Contractor scope changes

---

# 76. Employee Transfer

A transfer does not automatically preserve previous physical access.

---

# 77. Transfer Review

For an employee transfer:

1. Identify existing physical access.
2. Determine which access remains necessary.
3. Remove unnecessary access.
4. Authorize new access separately.
5. Update applicable records.

---

# 78. Location Transfer

When an employee moves to another facility, access to the prior facility should be removed unless continuing access has a documented business need.

---

# 79. Restricted Access Transfer

Restricted-area access from a previous role must not continue solely because the employee once required it.

---

# 80. Joiner Coordination

New employee physical access must coordinate with the personnel lifecycle established by [[PROC-001 Joiner-Mover-Leaver Procedure]].

---

# 81. New Employee Access

Before providing facility access, responsible personnel should confirm:

- Employment status
- Assigned facility
- Manager
- Required access scope
- Required Restricted-area authorization

---

# 82. Termination

When employment ends, physical access must be revoked promptly.

---

# 83. Termination Notification

Human Resources or responsible management must notify appropriate personnel when physical-access revocation is required.

---

# 84. Termination Actions

Applicable actions include:

- Disable electronic access card.
- Recover physical keys.
- Remove Restricted-area authorization.
- Recover company access credentials.
- Recover company equipment.
- Document exceptions.

---

# 85. Termination Timing

Physical-access revocation should be coordinated with the effective termination time and circumstances.

---

# 86. Immediate Termination

When immediate access removal is required, physical-access credentials should be disabled or recovered as part of the coordinated termination process.

---

# 87. Involuntary Termination

For an involuntary termination presenting elevated security concerns, Human Resources, management, Information Technology, and facility personnel should coordinate:

- Access removal
- Equipment recovery
- Logical-access disablement
- Safe departure

as appropriate.

---

# 88. Termination Safety

Termination security procedures must consider employee and workplace safety.

Law enforcement or other appropriate assistance should be used when a credible safety threat exists.

---

# 89. Unreturned Access Card

An unreturned electronic access card should be disabled promptly.

---

# 90. Unreturned Key

An unreturned key requires evaluation of whether:

- Continued possession creates material access risk.
- Rekeying is required.
- Temporary compensating controls are necessary.

---

# 91. Unreturned Company Property

Unreturned physical-security credentials or equipment must be documented and escalated based on risk.

---

# 92. Contractor Access Expiration

Contractor physical access must end when:

- Authorized duration expires
- Engagement ends
- Sponsor withdraws authorization
- Business need ends
- Security requires removal

---

# 93. Contractor Renewal

Continued contractor access requires confirmation that:

- Sponsorship remains valid.
- Business need remains.
- Access scope remains appropriate.
- Required confidentiality obligations remain in place.

---

# 94. Contractor Access Coordination

Contractor access must coordinate with [[PROC-004 Contractor Access Procedure]].

---

# 95. Temporary Personnel Expiration

Temporary personnel access must be removed when the authorized assignment ends.

---

# 96. Visitor Departure

Visitor access credentials, where issued, should be recovered when the visit ends.

---

# 97. Access Review

Physical access must be reviewed at least annually.

---

# 98. Annual Review Scope

The review should include as applicable:

- Facility access
- Electronic access cards
- Important keys
- Restricted-area access
- Significant IT-area access
- Warehouse security-cage access
- Contractor access

---

# 99. Review Preparation

The responsible reviewer should obtain:

- Current personnel information
- Current access assignments
- Current Restricted-area authorization
- Contractor status where applicable

---

# 100. Review Questions

For each access assignment, determine:

1. Is the person still employed or engaged?
2. Does the person still require the facility?
3. Does the person still require the area?
4. Does the access match current responsibilities?
5. Is Restricted access still justified?
6. Is temporary access still within its authorized period?

---

# 101. Review Decision

Each access assignment should result in:

- Retain
- Modify
- Remove
- Investigate

---

# 102. Unnecessary Access

Access determined to be unnecessary must be removed.

---

# 103. Unknown Access

Access that cannot be justified should be treated as an exception requiring investigation rather than automatically retained.

---

# 104. Review Evidence

Annual access-review evidence should identify:

- Facility or area
- Personnel reviewed
- Reviewer
- Review date
- Decisions
- Exceptions
- Corrective actions

---

# 105. Restricted-Area Review

Restricted-area access should receive specific review rather than being assumed valid because general facility access remains appropriate.

---

# 106. Warehouse Cage Review

Warehouse security-cage access should be reviewed against current operational responsibilities.

---

# 107. Significant IT Access Review

Physical access to significant IT areas should be reviewed against current Information Technology or support responsibilities.

---

# 108. Contractor Review

Contractor physical access should be reviewed against:

- Current contract or engagement
- Sponsor
- Required work
- Required duration
- Current access scope

---

# 109. Access Review Completion

An access review is not complete until identified unnecessary access has been:

- Removed
- Assigned for corrective action
- Formally addressed through an approved exception

---

# 110. Access Review Exception

An unresolved material access-review issue must be documented.

---

# 111. Physical Access Inventory

Where practical, Dunder Mifflin should maintain sufficient records to identify personnel with significant physical access.

---

# 112. Key Inventory

Important keys providing Restricted or broad facility access should be periodically accounted for.

---

# 113. Key Inventory Record

The key inventory should identify where practical:

- Key
- Assigned person
- Area
- Status

---

# 114. Spare Keys

Spare keys providing sensitive access should be:

- Controlled
- Stored securely
- Issued only when authorized

---

# 115. Emergency Keys

Emergency keys must be protected from unauthorized routine use.

---

# 116. Emergency Physical Access

Emergency physical access may be required because of:

- Fire
- Water
- Utility failure
- Equipment failure
- Safety event
- Business Continuity event
- Security incident

---

# 117. Emergency Access Principle

Emergency access should be limited to what is necessary to address the emergency.

---

# 118. Emergency Documentation

Material emergency physical-access exceptions should be documented after the event when immediate documentation is impractical.

---

# 119. Emergency Review

After a significant emergency-access event, management should determine whether:

- Access was appropriate.
- Temporary credentials remain active.
- Keys were returned.
- Restricted areas remain secure.
- Corrective action is required.

---

# 120. Facility Evacuation

During evacuation:

- Personnel must follow emergency instructions.
- Doors and access controls must not be manipulated in a manner that impedes safe exit.
- Asset protection is secondary to life safety.

---

# 121. Facility Reentry

Personnel must not reenter a facility after an emergency until authorized by appropriate:

- Emergency responders
- Landlord
- Property management
- Company management

as applicable.

---

# 122. Facility Closure

When a facility is temporarily closed, management should consider:

- Access restrictions
- Sensitive records
- Company equipment
- Warehouse inventory
- Backup media
- Required personnel access

---

# 123. Extended Facility Closure

An extended facility closure may require activation of [[PLN-002 Business Continuity Plan]].

---

# 124. Loss of Headquarters

Loss of Scranton Headquarters does not automatically mean loss of Dunder Mifflin's cloud-hosted systems.

Physical continuity should coordinate with distributed operations and remote capability.

---

# 125. Regional Disaster

A regional disaster may simultaneously affect:

- Facility access
- Personnel
- Transportation
- Utilities
- Wired communications
- Cellular communications

Physical-access decisions must remain subordinate to personnel safety.

---

# 126. Fire

During fire or suspected fire:

- Follow evacuation requirements.
- Do not delay evacuation to protect assets.
- Do not reenter without authorization.

---

# 127. Water Event

Water intrusion affecting facility access should be reported promptly.

Personnel must avoid areas presenting electrical, structural, or other safety hazards.

---

# 128. Utility Failure

A utility failure may require temporary closure or restricted facility access.

Management should coordinate with the landlord or property manager as appropriate.

---

# 129. Physical Access and Incident Response

A physical-access event may become an information-security incident when it involves:

- Unauthorized access to sensitive information
- Unauthorized access to Information Technology systems
- Theft of equipment
- Theft of backup media
- Deliberate security-control bypass
- Evidence of malicious activity

---

# 130. Evidence Preservation

Physical-access evidence related to an incident must be handled under [[PROC-011 Evidence Handling Procedure]].

---

# 131. Physical Evidence

Physical evidence may include:

- Access card
- Key
- Damaged lock
- Damaged door
- Recovered equipment
- Paper record
- Other physical item

---

# 132. Electronic Evidence

Electronic physical-access evidence may include:

- Access-control logs
- Building records
- Available landlord CCTV
- Related system logs

---

# 133. Landlord Evidence

When evidence is controlled by a landlord, property manager, or other third party, Dunder Mifflin should request relevant records where appropriate.

Availability must not be assumed.

---

# 141. Access Metrics

Physical-access metrics may include:

- Annual access reviews completed
- Access assignments reviewed
- Unnecessary access identified
- Unnecessary access removed
- Lost access cards
- Lost keys
- Unreturned credentials
- Terminated personnel with access exceptions
- Restricted-area exceptions
- Overdue corrective actions

Metric definitions are maintained in [[REG-005 Security Metrics Catalog]].

---

# 142. Key Performance Indicators

Key Performance Indicators (KPIs) may include:

- Percentage of required annual physical-access reviews completed
- Percentage of identified unnecessary access removed
- Percentage of termination access actions completed
- Percentage of Restricted-area reviews completed

---

# 143. Key Risk Indicators

Key Risk Indicators (KRIs) may include:

- Terminated personnel retaining active access
- Missing Restricted-area key
- Unauthorized Restricted-area access
- Repeated lost access credential
- Unresolved annual access-review exception
- Overdue material physical-access corrective action

---

# 144. Finding

A finding is a documented conclusion supported by evidence concerning whether a requirement, control, process, or expected outcome was satisfied.

---

# 145. Physical Access Findings

Potential findings include:

- Unauthorized access
- Unnecessary access
- Active access after termination
- Unreturned Restricted-area key
- Missing access authorization
- Expired contractor access remaining active
- Access review not completed
- Restricted-area access without current business need

---

# 146. Corrective Action

Material deficiencies must identify:

- Finding
- Required action
- Accountable owner
- Due date
- Validation method
- Retest requirement where appropriate

---

# 147. Corrective Action Register

Material deficiencies and corrective actions are tracked in [[REG-004 Finding & Corrective Action Register]].

---

# 148. Validation

Corrective action must be validated.

Examples include:

- Confirming access was disabled
- Confirming a key was recovered
- Confirming locks were changed
- Confirming authorization was corrected
- Confirming temporary access expired

---

# 149. Assessment

Facility-access controls are subject to assurance under [[PRO-008 Security Assurance & Continuous Improvement Program]].

---

# 150. Assessment Methods

Assessment may use:

- Inspection
- Observation
- Interviews
- Sampling
- Record review
- Independent Performance

---

# 151. Independent Performance

An assessor may independently perform an authorized facility-access review.

For example:

1. Select a sample of personnel.
2. Identify assigned facility access.
3. Confirm current employment or engagement.
4. Confirm current business need.
5. Confirm Restricted-area authorization where applicable.
6. Identify unnecessary access.
7. Document the result.

---

# 152. Termination Sample

An assessor may select terminated personnel and determine whether applicable:

- Electronic access was disabled.
- Keys were recovered.
- Restricted access was removed.
- Exceptions were documented.

---

# 153. Contractor Sample

An assessor may select contractor access and determine whether:

- Sponsor remains valid.
- Engagement remains active.
- Access scope remains necessary.
- Access duration remains authorized.

---

# 154. Restricted-Area Sample

An assessor may select personnel with Restricted-area access and compare that access with:

- Job responsibilities
- Current authorization
- Business need

---

# 155. Evidence Integrity

Assessment evidence must reflect actual conditions.

Personnel must not:

- Backdate access approvals.
- Backdate access reviews.
- Invent credential records.
- Conceal lost credentials.
- Record access removal that did not occur.
- Alter findings to make controls appear effective.

---

# 156. Control Effectiveness

Facility-access controls may be assessed as:

- Effective
- Partially Effective
- Ineffective
- Not Implemented

---

# 157. Control Effectiveness Evidence

Effectiveness conclusions should consider:

- Access authorization
- Credential records
- Access reviews
- Termination records
- Restricted-area reviews
- Lost-credential response
- Findings
- Corrective-action validation

---

# 158. Current and Proposed Controls

Physical-access reporting must distinguish among:

- Current controls
- Partially implemented controls
- Planned controls
- Implemented controls awaiting validation
- Validated controls

---

# 159. No Premature Risk Credit

Current residual risk must not be reduced merely because:

- An access review is planned.
- A new lock is ordered.
- A new card reader is approved.
- A procedure is documented.
- A corrective action is assigned.

Risk reduction requires implementation and appropriate validation.

---

# 160. Target Residual Risk

Target residual risk represents expected risk after planned treatment.

It is a prediction.

---

# 161. Validated Post-Treatment Risk

After physical-access improvements are implemented and validated, applicable risks may be reassessed.

Actual validated risk may be:

- Higher than target
- Equal to target
- Lower than target

---

# 162. Historical Integrity

The original May 2013 risk assessment must remain preserved.

Later validated assessments may establish a new current residual risk without rewriting the historical assessment.

---

# 163. Procedure Exceptions

Exceptions to this procedure must be:

- Documented
- Justified
- Risk evaluated
- Approved under appropriate authority
- Time-limited where practical
- Reviewed when conditions change

---

# 164. Procedure Review

This procedure must be reviewed when material changes affect:

- Facility access
- Access-control technology
- Restricted areas
- Warehouse security
- Personnel lifecycle
- Contractor access
- Facility layout

---

# 165. Traceability

This procedure supports the enterprise traceability model:

**Business Requirement → Asset / Process → Classification / BIA → Risk → Treatment → Policy / Standard → Control → Procedure → Implementation → Evidence → Test / Audit → Finding → Remediation → Retest → Risk Reassessment**

Facility access is not effective merely because an access credential exists.

The credential must be:

**Authorized → Appropriately Scoped → Used as Intended → Reviewed → Revoked When No Longer Required**

# 167. Review

This procedure must be reviewed:

- At least annually
- Following material facility change
- Following material access-control change
- Following significant physical-access incident
- Following material personnel-lifecycle change
- Following significant assessment findings

The COO coordinates review.

Material revisions require CEO approval.
