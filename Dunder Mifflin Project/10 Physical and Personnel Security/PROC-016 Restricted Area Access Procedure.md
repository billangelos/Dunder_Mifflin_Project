# 1. Purpose

This procedure establishes requirements for controlling physical access to Dunder Mifflin Restricted areas.

Restricted areas require stronger physical protection because they contain:

- Significant Information Technology equipment
- Sensitive information
- Sensitive business records
- High-value or theft-attractive inventory
- Other assets requiring protection beyond normal Controlled-area access

---

# 2. Objectives

Restricted-area controls are intended to:

- Limit access to legitimate business need.
- Protect sensitive information.
- Protect significant Information Technology equipment.
- Protect high-value inventory.
- Maintain individual accountability.
- Control visitor and vendor access.
- Remove unnecessary access.
- Support investigation of security events.
- Provide evidence for security assessment.

---

# 3. Scope

This procedure applies to Restricted areas within:

1. Scranton Headquarters
2. Scranton Branch
3. Syracuse Branch
4. Utica Branch
5. Rochester Branch
6. Nashua Branch
7. Akron Branch

# 5. Safety Priority

Human life and safety take priority over Restricted-area security.

Restricted-area controls must not interfere with:

- Emergency evacuation
- Emergency medical response
- Fire response
- Law-enforcement response
- Other required emergency activity

---

# 6. Restricted Area Definition

A Restricted area is a company-controlled physical location requiring specific access authorization beyond ordinary facility access.

---

# 7. Restricted Area Designation

An area may be designated Restricted based on:

- Information sensitivity
- System importance
- Asset value
- Theft risk
- Business impact
- Regulatory or contractual requirement
- Security risk

---

# 8. Restricted Area Examples

Restricted areas may include:

- Significant Information Technology spaces
- Sensitive records storage
- High-value inventory cages or enclosures
- Other specifically designated sensitive locations

---

# 9. General Facility Access Is Insufficient

Authorization to enter a Dunder Mifflin facility does not automatically authorize entry into a Restricted area.

---

# 10. Controlled Area Access Is Insufficient

Authorization to work within a Controlled area does not automatically authorize Restricted-area access.

---

# 11. Restricted Access Lifecycle

Restricted-area access follows:

**Request → Justify → Authorize → Issue → Use → Review → Modify → Revoke**

---

# 12. Business Need

Restricted-area access must be based on a current and legitimate business need.

---

# 13. Least Physical Access

Personnel should receive access only to the Restricted areas required for their responsibilities.

---

# 14. Access Request

A Restricted-area access request should identify:

- Person
- Employment or engagement status
- Restricted area
- Business justification
- Required duration
- Requesting manager or sponsor

---

# 15. Access Authorization

Restricted-area access must be approved by the responsible area owner or designated management authority.

---

# 16. Authorization Before Access

Except for legitimate emergency conditions, authorization must occur before Restricted-area access is provided.

---

# 17. Area Owner

Each Restricted area should have an identifiable business or operational owner responsible for determining appropriate access.

---

# 18. Area Owner Responsibilities

The area owner is responsible for:

- Approving access.
- Reviewing access.
- Identifying unnecessary access.
- Coordinating removal.
- Identifying material security concerns.
- Supporting assessment.

---

# 19. Restricted Area Inventory

Dunder Mifflin should maintain sufficient records to identify designated Restricted areas and their responsible owners.

---

# 20. Access Method

Restricted areas may be protected using:

- Physical key
- Electronic access card
- Locked enclosure
- Other appropriate physical access mechanism

---

# 21. Credential Scope

A credential providing Restricted-area access must not provide unnecessarily broad access.

---

# 22. Individual Credentials

Where technically and operationally practical, Restricted-area access credentials should be individually assigned.

---

# 23. Credential Sharing

Individually assigned Restricted-area credentials must not be intentionally shared.

---

# 24. Key Access

Keys providing Restricted-area access must be limited to authorized personnel.

---

# 25. Restricted Key Record

Important Restricted-area key assignments should identify where practical:

- Person
- Key or key group
- Restricted area
- Issue date
- Status

---

# 26. Master Keys

A key providing access to multiple Restricted areas requires stronger control than a key providing ordinary low-risk access.

---

# 27. Spare Restricted Keys

Spare Restricted-area keys must be:

- Controlled
- Stored securely
- Issued only when authorized

---

# 28. Emergency Keys

Emergency keys must be protected against unauthorized routine use.

---

# 29. Electronic Access

Where electronic access cards protect Restricted areas, access should be assigned only to personnel with current authorization.

---

# 30. Access Record

Where supported by the access mechanism, Restricted-area access records should provide sufficient information to identify:

- Authorized person
- Area
- Credential
- Access status

---

# 31. Electronic Access Logs

Electronic access-control systems may record:

- Credential
- Door
- Date
- Time
- Successful access
- Failed access

---

# 32. Log Limitation

An electronic access log demonstrates use of a credential.

It does not automatically prove which person physically entered the area.

---

# 33. Restricted IT Areas

Areas containing significant Information Technology equipment should be designated Restricted where appropriate.

---

# 34. Restricted IT Assets

Restricted IT areas may contain:

- Firewalls
- Routers
- Switches
- Servers
- Telecommunications equipment
- Backup equipment
- Other significant infrastructure

---

# 35. IT Area Authorization

Physical access to a Restricted IT area should be limited to personnel requiring:

- Administration
- Maintenance
- Support
- Authorized service
- Security assessment

---

# 36. IT Personnel

Information Technology employment does not automatically justify access to every Restricted IT area.

Access should reflect assigned responsibilities.

---

# 37. Non-IT Personnel

Non-IT personnel requiring Restricted IT-area access must have:

- Specific business need
- Appropriate authorization
- Escort where required

---

# 38. Service Personnel

Service personnel entering Restricted IT areas must receive specific authorization.

---

# 39. Service Personnel Escort

Service personnel should be escorted in Restricted IT areas unless independent access has been specifically authorized.

---

# 40. Service Scope

Physical access for service personnel must remain limited to the equipment and area required for the authorized work.

---

# 41. Technical Access Is Separate

Physical access to an IT area does not automatically authorize:

- System login
- Administrative access
- Network access
- Application access
- Database access
- Configuration change

Logical access remains subject to applicable Identity and Access Management requirements.

---

# 42. Privileged Technical Work

Privileged technical work performed from a Restricted or Controlled location remains subject to:

- [[POL-003 Access Control Policy]]
- [[PRO-003 Identity and Access Management Program]]
- [[PROC-002 Privileged Access Procedure]]

---

# 43. Infinity Contractor

The Infinity contractor may require authorized physical access to Scranton Headquarters for application-support activity.

---

# 44. Infinity Privileged Work

Privileged Infinity contractor production or administrative work is performed from a designated controlled workspace at Scranton Headquarters when required.

---

# 45. Infinity Contractor Access

Physical access for Infinity contractor personnel must be:

- Sponsored
- Authorized
- Limited to required locations
- Limited to required duration
- Coordinated with contractor-access requirements

---

# 46. Contractor Personnel Notice

Expected Infinity contractor personnel should be identified at least one business day before scheduled arrival where practical.

---

# 47. Emergency Contractor Exception

Emergency support may require contractor personnel who could not reasonably be identified one business day in advance.

The exception must be documented.

---

# 48. Contractor Technical Authorization

Physical presence at Headquarters does not authorize privileged technical access.

Contractor technical access must comply with:

- [[PROC-004 Contractor Access Procedure]]
- [[PROC-020 Contractor Development Access Procedure]]

---

# 49. Sensitive Records Areas

Areas used to store significant volumes of Confidential or Restricted records may be designated Restricted.

---

# 50. Records-Area Access

Access to sensitive records areas must be based on business need.

---

# 51. Sensitive Records

Sensitive records may include:

- Personnel records
- Payroll records
- Financial records
- Legal records
- Customer information
- Security documentation
- Other protected business records

---

# 52. Records Protection

Sensitive records within Restricted areas should be protected from:

- Unauthorized viewing
- Removal
- Alteration
- Destruction

---

# 53. Record Removal

Removal of sensitive records from a Restricted area must be for an authorized business purpose.

---

# 54. Information Classification

Information within a Restricted area remains subject to [[POL-004 Information Classification & Handling Policy]] and [[STD-001 Information Classification Standard]].

Physical location does not change information classification.

---

# 55. Warehouse Security Cages

Each Dunder Mifflin warehouse maintains a locked security cage or enclosure for higher-value or theft-attractive inventory.

---

# 56. Cage Classification

The high-value inventory cage is treated as a Restricted area.

---

# 57. Cage Ownership

Warehouse security cages are operationally managed by Operations.

---

# 58. Cage Access

Cage access must be limited to personnel with a legitimate operational or management need.

---

# 59. General Warehouse Access

General authorization to work in the warehouse does not automatically provide authorization to enter the high-value inventory cage.

---

# 60. Cage Keys or Credentials

Keys or credentials providing cage access must be limited to authorized personnel.

---

# 61. Inventory Accountability

Physical cage security operates with:

- Warehouse Management System records
- Receiving records
- Shipping records
- Inventory counts
- Individual accountability for significant transactions
- Reconciliation
- Investigation of discrepancies

---

# 62. Inventory Movement

Material inventory movement into or out of the cage must be supported by applicable business records.

---

# 63. Physical Control Limitation

A locked cage does not by itself establish complete inventory security.

Inventory accountability also depends on accurate operational records and reconciliation.

---

# 64. R032 Inventory Theft

R032 has:

- Inherent Risk: **12 High**
- Current Residual Risk: **6 Moderate**
- Target Residual Risk: **3 Low**

---

# 65. R032 Treatment Relationship

Restricted cage controls support R032 through:

- Limited access
- Locked storage
- Individual accountability
- Inventory records
- Receiving and shipping records
- Counts
- Reconciliation
- Investigation of discrepancies

---

# 66. Visitor Restricted Access

Visitors do not receive Restricted-area access solely because they are authorized to enter the facility.

---

# 67. Visitor Authorization

Visitor entry into a Restricted area requires:

- Legitimate business purpose
- Specific authorization
- Appropriate sponsor
- Escort where required

---

# 68. Visitor Escort

Visitors should be escorted in Restricted areas unless independent access has been specifically authorized.

---

# 69. Visitor Scope

Visitors must remain within the Restricted area and scope required for the approved activity.

---

# 70. Vendor Restricted Access

Vendors requiring Restricted-area access must have:

- Identified business purpose
- Appropriate authorization
- Required access scope
- Appropriate supervision or escort
- Required confidentiality protection where sensitive information may be exposed

---

# 71. Confidentiality Requirement

Before a nonemployee receives access to sensitive company information within a Restricted area, an appropriate Non-Disclosure Agreement (NDA) or equivalent enforceable confidentiality obligation must be in place.

---

# 72. Confidentiality Limitation

An NDA does not itself authorize Restricted-area access.

---

# 73. Visitor Procedure

Visitor and vendor access must comply with [[PROC-015 Visitor & Vendor Access Procedure]].

---

# 74. Penetration Testers

External penetration testers requiring Restricted-area access must receive specific physical authorization separate from the technical assessment authorization.

---

# 75. Penetration Test Scope

Physical presence does not expand the authorized penetration-testing scope.

Testing must comply with [[STD-007 Penetration Testing Standard]].

---

# 76. Temporary Access

Temporary Restricted-area access should have an identifiable expiration or end condition.

---

# 77. Temporary Access Examples

Temporary Restricted access may be appropriate for:

- Maintenance
- Installation
- Repair
- Audit
- Security assessment
- Contractor work
- Inventory activity
- Emergency support

---

# 78. Temporary Access Expiration

Temporary Restricted access must be removed when:

- Work is complete.
- Authorized duration expires.
- Business need ends.
- Sponsor withdraws authorization.
- Security requires removal.

---

# 79. Recurring Access

Recurring access must not remain active solely because it has existed historically.

The business need must remain current.

---

# 80. Personnel Transfer

Restricted-area access must be reviewed when an employee:

- Changes role
- Changes facility
- Changes department
- Changes responsibilities

---

# 81. Transfer Principle

Restricted access associated with a prior role must not automatically transfer to the new role.

---

# 82. Transfer Process

For a transfer:

1. Identify existing Restricted-area access.
2. Determine current business need.
3. Remove unnecessary access.
4. Authorize required new access separately.
5. Update applicable records.

---

# 83. Termination

Restricted-area access must be revoked promptly when employment or engagement ends.

---

# 84. Termination Coordination

Termination must coordinate with:

- Human Resources
- Management
- Information Technology where applicable
- Facility responsibility
- Area owner

---

# 85. Termination Actions

Applicable Restricted-area termination actions include:

- Disable electronic access.
- Recover keys.
- Recover temporary credentials.
- Remove authorized-personnel listing.
- Recover company property.
- Document unresolved exceptions.

---

# 86. Immediate Termination

Where immediate access removal is required, Restricted-area access should be revoked as part of the coordinated termination process.

---

# 87. Unreturned Restricted Key

An unreturned Restricted-area key requires prompt risk evaluation.

---

# 88. Unreturned Key Evaluation

The evaluation should determine:

- Area accessible
- Assets exposed
- Whether the key is identifiable
- Whether unauthorized possession creates material risk
- Whether rekeying is required
- Whether temporary compensating controls are required

---

# 89. Unreturned Access Card

An unreturned electronic access card providing Restricted access should be disabled promptly.

---

# 90. Contractor Termination

Restricted-area access for a contractor must end when:

- Engagement ends.
- Authorized period expires.
- Sponsorship ends.
- Required work ends.
- Security requires removal.

---

# 91. Personnel Lifecycle Coordination

Restricted physical access must coordinate with:

- [[PROC-001 Joiner-Mover-Leaver Procedure]]
- [[PROC-017 Personnel Security Procedure]]

---

# 92. Annual Access Review

Restricted-area access must be reviewed at least annually.

---

# 93. Review Scope

The annual review should include:

- Restricted-area list
- Area owners
- Personnel with access
- Contractors with access
- Keys
- Electronic access where applicable
- Temporary access still active

---

# 94. Review Questions

For each person with Restricted access, determine:

1. Is the person still employed or engaged?
2. Does the person still require access?
3. Is the access appropriate to current responsibilities?
4. Is the scope appropriate?
5. Is temporary access still valid?
6. Is the authorization still current?

---

# 95. Review Decision

Each access assignment should result in:

- Retain
- Modify
- Remove
- Investigate

---

# 96. Unnecessary Access

Restricted access that is no longer necessary must be removed.

---

# 97. Unjustified Access

Restricted access that cannot be justified should not be automatically retained.

It must be investigated.

---

# 98. Review Evidence

Restricted-area review evidence should identify:

- Area
- Personnel reviewed
- Reviewer
- Review date
- Decision
- Exception
- Corrective action where required

---

# 99. Review Completion

A Restricted-area review is not complete merely because a list was examined.

Identified unnecessary access must be:

- Removed
- Assigned for corrective action
- Addressed through an approved exception

---

# 100. Access System Failure

Failure of an electronic Restricted-area access mechanism must not automatically result in unrestricted access.

---

# 101. Temporary Manual Control

During an access-control failure, temporary controls may include:

- Physical key
- Manual authorization
- Employee presence
- Temporary access log
- Restricted entry

depending on the area and risk.

---

# 102. Temporary Control Accountability

Temporary access during a control failure should remain attributable where practical.

---

# 103. Failed Lock

A failed lock protecting a Restricted area must be reported promptly.

---

# 104. Failed Lock Response

Response may include:

- Immediate repair
- Temporary lock
- Employee presence
- Relocation of sensitive assets
- Restriction of area use
- Other appropriate compensating control

---

# 105. Door Propping

A Restricted-area door must not be intentionally propped open in a manner that defeats required access control except when:

- Operationally necessary
- Temporarily authorized
- Appropriately supervised

---

# 106. Maintenance Condition

When a Restricted-area door must remain open for authorized maintenance or movement of equipment, responsible personnel should maintain reasonable control over entry.

---

# 107. Tailgating

Personnel must not knowingly allow an unauthorized individual to tailgate into a Restricted area.

---

# 108. Suspicious Access Attempt

Suspicious Restricted-area activity may include:

- Repeated unauthorized entry attempts
- Credential misuse
- Forced door
- Tampered lock
- Tailgating
- Unknown individual
- Unexplained presence
- Attempted removal of sensitive assets
- Unauthorized photography

---

# 109. Security Reporting

Suspicious Restricted-area activity must be reported when safe and appropriate.

---

# 110. Personal Safety

Employees must not physically confront an individual when doing so would create unnecessary personal danger.

---

# 111. Security Event Escalation

Restricted-area events with information-security implications must be escalated under [[PROC-010 Security Event Escalation Procedure]].

---

# 112. Incident Response

A material Restricted-area security event may require activation of [[PLN-001 Incident Response Plan]].

---

# 113. Evidence

Potential Restricted-area incident evidence may include:

- Access-control logs
- Key records
- Visitor records
- Witness statements
- Physical damage
- Photographs
- Available landlord CCTV
- System logs
- Inventory records
- Recovered property

---

# 114. Evidence Handling

Incident evidence must be handled according to [[PROC-011 Evidence Handling Procedure]].

---

# 115. Landlord-Controlled Evidence

Where relevant evidence is controlled by a landlord or property manager, Dunder Mifflin should request it where appropriate.

Availability must not be assumed.

---

# 116. Emergency Access

Emergency access to a Restricted area may be required for:

- Fire
- Medical emergency
- Water intrusion
- Utility failure
- Equipment failure
- Security incident
- Business Continuity
- Disaster Recovery

---

# 117. Emergency Access Priority

Life safety and emergency response take priority over normal Restricted-area authorization procedures.

---

# 118. Emergency Responder Access

Emergency responders must receive necessary access without being delayed by normal administrative access procedures.

---

# 119. Emergency Employee Access

Where an employee requires exceptional Restricted-area access during an emergency, access should be limited to what is necessary.

---

# 120. Emergency Exception Documentation

If normal authorization cannot reasonably occur before emergency access, the material exception should be documented afterward.

---

# 121. Post-Emergency Review

Following material emergency access, responsible management should determine:

- Who entered the area.
- Why access was required.
- Whether sensitive assets were affected.
- Whether equipment was altered.
- Whether temporary access remains active.
- Whether corrective action is required.

---

# 122. Evacuation

Restricted-area controls must not impede emergency evacuation.

---

# 123. Reentry

Personnel must not reenter a Restricted area following an evacuation until reentry is authorized by appropriate:

- Emergency responders
- Landlord
- Property management
- Company management

as applicable.

---

# 124. Fire

Personnel must not remain in or reenter a Restricted area during a fire solely to:

- Shut down systems
- Retrieve records
- Recover backup media
- Protect inventory
- Secure equipment

---

# 125. Water

Personnel must avoid entering a Restricted area affected by water when electrical, structural, or other safety hazards may exist.

---

# 126. Utility Failure

A utility failure may require:

- Restricted access
- Equipment shutdown
- Facility closure
- Business Continuity activation

depending on conditions.

---

# 127. Backup Media

Where backup media is stored in a Restricted area, access must be limited to personnel with legitimate backup, recovery, or custody responsibilities.

---

# 128. Backup Media Removal

Removal of backup media must follow [[PROC-012 Backup & Recovery Procedure]].

---

# 129. Backup Media Confidentiality

Encryption does not eliminate the need for physical protection of backup media.

---

# 130. Continuity Event

Restricted-area controls remain applicable during Business Continuity and Disaster Recovery events unless a necessary exception is authorized or documented.

---

# 131. Alternate Work Location

A temporary continuity location should receive physical protection appropriate to the:

- Information
- Equipment
- Personnel
- Business activity
- Duration

involved.

---

# 132. R024 Physical Access

R024 has:

- Inherent Risk: **16 High**
- Current Residual Risk: **8 Moderate**
- Target Residual Risk: **4 Low**

---

# 133. R024 Treatment Relationship

This procedure supports R024 treatment through:

- Restricted-area designation
- Business-need authorization
- Credential control
- Key control
- Visitor authorization
- Escort
- Annual access review
- Termination access removal
- Emergency-access review

---

# 134. R011 Delayed Termination

R011 has:

- Inherent Risk: **16 High**
- Current Residual Risk: **8 Moderate**
- Target Residual Risk: **4 Low**

Prompt Restricted-area access removal supports R011 treatment.

---

# 135. R012 Malicious Insider

R012 has:

- Inherent Risk: **15 High**
- Current Residual Risk: **10 High**
- Target Residual Risk: **5 Moderate**

---

# 136. R012 Treatment Relationship

Restricted-area least privilege reduces unnecessary physical opportunity.

It does not eliminate malicious action by personnel who legitimately require access.

---

# 137. R019 Infinity Contractor

R019 has:

- Inherent Risk: **12 High**
- Current Residual Risk: **8 Moderate**
- Target Residual Risk: **4 Low**

Controlled contractor physical access supports treatment of R019.

---

# 138. R032 Inventory Theft

Restricted warehouse-cage access is a significant physical control supporting treatment of R032.

---

# 139. R021 Fire

Restricted-area security must not interfere with life-safety response under R021 Fire.

---

# 140. R022 Water

Physical protection of sensitive equipment and records supports treatment of R022 Water.

---

# 141. R023 Utility / Power / HVAC Failure

Restricted IT-area controls must support safe equipment response during R023 Utility / Power / HVAC Failure without placing personnel at risk.

---

# 142. R026 Personnel Loss

Restricted-area access should not depend unnecessarily on a single individual possessing the only:

- Key
- Authorization knowledge
- Access information
- Recovery capability

required for essential business activity.

---

# 143. R034 Loss of Headquarters

Loss of Headquarters may make Headquarters Restricted areas unavailable.

Recovery planning must not depend on physical access to Headquarters remaining available.

---

# 144. R035 Regional Disaster

Regional disaster may make multiple Restricted areas unavailable.

Personnel safety and continuity requirements take priority over asset retrieval.

---

# 145. Restricted Area Inspection

Restricted areas should be periodically inspected as appropriate to risk.

---

# 146. Inspection Scope

Inspection may include:

- Door condition
- Lock condition
- Access mechanism
- Key control
- Unauthorized access
- Sensitive information exposure
- Environmental concern
- High-value inventory cage condition

---

# 147. Inspection Result

Inspection results should identify:

- Area
- Date
- Inspector
- Condition
- Exception
- Required corrective action

where appropriate.

---

# 148. Immediate Deficiency

A deficiency creating immediate material exposure should be corrected or compensated for as soon as practical.

---

# 149. Compensating Control

Temporary compensating controls may include:

- Employee presence
- Temporary lock
- Restricted use
- Relocation of sensitive assets
- Manual access control
- Other reasonable protection

---

# 150. Restricted Access Metrics

Metrics may include:

- Restricted-area access reviews completed
- Personnel with Restricted access
- Unnecessary Restricted access identified
- Unnecessary access removed
- Missing Restricted keys
- Lost Restricted credentials
- Unauthorized access attempts
- Visitor exceptions
- Warehouse cage exceptions
- Overdue corrective actions

Metric definitions are maintained in [[REG-005 Security Metrics Catalog]].

---

# 151. Key Performance Indicators

Key Performance Indicators (KPIs) may include:

- Percentage of required Restricted-area reviews completed
- Percentage of unnecessary Restricted access removed
- Percentage of material Restricted-area deficiencies corrected and validated
- Percentage of temporary Restricted access removed when no longer required

---

# 152. Key Risk Indicators

Key Risk Indicators (KRIs) may include:

- Terminated personnel retaining Restricted access
- Missing Restricted-area key
- Unauthorized Restricted-area entry
- Unsecured high-value inventory cage
- Repeated visitor escort failure
- Repeated Restricted-area access exception
- Overdue material corrective action

---

# 153. Finding

A finding is a documented conclusion supported by evidence concerning whether a requirement, control, process, or expected outcome was satisfied.

---

# 154. Restricted-Area Findings

Potential findings include:

- Access without current business need
- Access without approval
- Missing Restricted-area key
- Active access after termination
- Expired contractor access
- Unescorted visitor where escort was required
- Failed lock
- Unsecured security cage
- Incomplete annual review
- Emergency access not reviewed

---

# 155. Corrective Action

Material deficiencies must identify:

- Finding
- Required action
- Accountable owner
- Due date
- Validation method
- Retest requirement where appropriate

---

# 156. Finding Register

Material findings and corrective actions are tracked in [[REG-004 Finding & Corrective Action Register]].

---

# 157. Corrective Action Validation

A corrective action is not complete solely because work was performed.

Validation must determine whether the identified Restricted-area weakness was corrected.

---

# 158. Assessment

Restricted-area controls are subject to assurance under [[PRO-008 Security Assurance & Continuous Improvement Program]].

---

# 159. Assessment Methods

Assessment may use:

- Inspection
- Observation
- Interviews
- Sampling
- Record review
- Independent Performance

---

# 160. Independent Performance

An assessor may independently perform an authorized Restricted-area access review.

For example:

1. Select a Restricted area.
2. Obtain the current authorized-access population.
3. Select a sample.
4. Confirm current employment or engagement.
5. Confirm business need.
6. Confirm authorization.
7. Identify unnecessary or unexplained access.
8. Document the result.

---

# 161. Termination Testing

An assessor may select terminated personnel and determine whether applicable Restricted access was removed promptly.

Evidence may include:

- Disabled electronic credential
- Recovered key
- Updated authorization list
- Documented exception

---

# 162. Key-Control Testing

An assessor may select Restricted-area keys and determine whether:

- Assignment is documented.
- Assigned person remains authorized.
- Key is accounted for.
- Lost keys received appropriate response.

---

# 163. Visitor Testing

An assessor may sample Restricted-area visitor activity and determine whether:

- Business purpose existed.
- Specific authorization existed.
- Sponsor was identified.
- Escort requirements were followed.
- Sensitive access had required confidentiality protection.

---

# 164. Warehouse Cage Testing

An assessor may inspect a high-value inventory cage and determine whether:

- The cage is secured.
- Access is appropriately limited.
- Authorized access is current.
- Material inventory movement is supported by business records.
- Identified discrepancies receive investigation.

---

# 165. IT Area Testing

An assessor may inspect a Restricted IT area and determine whether:

- Physical access is limited.
- Access authorization is current.
- Visitors are appropriately controlled.
- Significant equipment is reasonably protected.

---

# 166. Independent Performance Limitation

Independent Performance must be authorized and controlled.

Assessment activity must not:

- Bypass physical security without authorization.
- Damage locks or doors.
- Impersonate personnel without approved scope.
- Create unnecessary safety risk.
- Disrupt business operations without authorization.

---

# 167. Evidence Integrity

Assessment evidence must reflect actual conditions.

Personnel must not:

- Backdate Restricted-area approvals.
- Backdate access reviews.
- Invent key records.
- Record a visitor escort that did not occur.
- Conceal missing credentials.
- Record an inspection that was not performed.
- Alter results to make controls appear effective.

---

# 168. Control Effectiveness

Restricted-area controls may be assessed as:

- Effective
- Partially Effective
- Ineffective
- Not Implemented

---

# 169. Effectiveness Evidence

Effectiveness conclusions should consider:

- Access authorization
- Access reviews
- Key records
- Electronic access records where available
- Visitor records
- Inspections
- Termination testing
- Warehouse controls
- Findings
- Corrective-action validation

---

# 170. Current and Proposed Controls

Restricted-area reporting must distinguish among:

- Current controls
- Partially implemented controls
- Planned controls
- Implemented controls awaiting validation
- Validated controls

---

# 171. No Premature Risk Credit

Current residual risk must not be reduced merely because:

- A Restricted area is designated.
- A new lock is planned.
- A key inventory is scheduled.
- An access review is scheduled.
- A procedure is written.
- A corrective action is assigned.

Risk reduction requires implementation and appropriate validation.

---

# 172. Target Residual Risk

Target residual risk represents expected risk after planned treatment.

It is a prediction.

---

# 173. Validated Post-Treatment Risk

After Restricted-area controls are implemented and validated, applicable risks may be reassessed.

Actual validated risk may be:

- Higher than target
- Equal to target
- Lower than target

---

# 174. Historical Integrity

The original May 2013 risk assessment must remain preserved.

Later validated assessments may establish a new current residual risk without rewriting the historical assessment.

---

# 175. Exceptions

Exceptions to this procedure must be:

- Documented
- Justified
- Risk evaluated
- Approved under appropriate authority
- Time-limited where practical
- Reviewed when conditions change

---

# 176. Exception Expiration

A temporary Restricted-area exception must not become permanent merely because its expiration was overlooked.

---

# 177. Exception Review

Before renewing an exception, responsible management should determine whether:

- The business need remains.
- The risk remains acceptable.
- A permanent corrective solution is available.
- Additional controls are required.

---

# 178. Procedure Review

This procedure must be reviewed when material changes affect:

- Restricted areas
- Facility layout
- Access-control technology
- Significant IT equipment
- Sensitive records storage
- Warehouse security cages
- Contractor access
- Physical-security risk

---

# 179. Traceability

This procedure supports the enterprise traceability model:

**Business Requirement → Asset / Process → Classification / BIA → Risk → Treatment → Policy / Standard → Control → Procedure → Implementation → Evidence → Test / Audit → Finding → Remediation → Retest → Risk Reassessment**

Restricted-area security is not demonstrated merely by placing a lock on a door or cage.

Effective Restricted-area control requires:

**Business Need → Authorization → Appropriate Access → Accountability → Review → Timely Revocation → Validation**
