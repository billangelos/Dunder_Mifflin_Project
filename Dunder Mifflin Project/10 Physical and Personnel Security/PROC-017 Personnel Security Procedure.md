# 1. Purpose

This procedure establishes Dunder Mifflin's personnel-security requirements throughout the employment and engagement lifecycle.

Personnel security is intended to reduce risk associated with:

- Inappropriate access
- Delayed access removal
- Unauthorized disclosure
- Malicious insider activity
- Loss of key personnel
- Excessive dependence on individual employees or contractors
- Inadequate transfer of security responsibilities

---

# 2. Objectives

Personnel-security activities must support:

- Appropriate personnel screening
- Confidentiality
- Security awareness
- Access based on business need
- Individual accountability
- Timely access changes
- Prompt termination processing
- Company property recovery
- Contractor and temporary personnel control
- Personnel-loss resilience
- Cross-training
- Business Continuity

---

# 3. Scope

This procedure applies as appropriate to:

- Employees
- Managers
- Executives
- Information Technology personnel
- Temporary personnel
- Contractors
- Consultants
- Other nonemployees receiving recurring company access

# 5. Human Resources Authority

Human Resources is the authoritative source for employee status.

Employee status includes:

- Hire
- Active employment
- Transfer
- Leave where relevant
- Separation
- Termination

---

# 6. Personnel Security Lifecycle

Personnel security follows:

**Hire / Engage → Authorize → Educate → Work → Review → Transfer → Separate / Terminate**

---

# 7. Shared Responsibility

Personnel security requires coordination among:

- Human Resources
- Management
- Information Technology
- Information Security
- Operations
- Facility responsibility
- Legal or Compliance where appropriate

---

# 8. Human Resources Responsibilities

Human Resources is responsible for:

- Maintaining authoritative employment status.
- Coordinating appropriate personnel processes.
- Communicating relevant employment changes.
- Supporting termination coordination.
- Maintaining applicable personnel records.
- Coordinating confidentiality requirements where applicable.

---

# 9. Manager Responsibilities

Managers are responsible for:

- Defining job responsibilities.
- Identifying required access.
- Confirming continuing business need.
- Identifying personnel dependencies.
- Supporting access reviews.
- Reporting role changes.
- Coordinating employee departures.
- Supporting cross-training and documentation.

---

# 10. Information Technology Responsibilities

Information Technology is responsible for:

- Provisioning authorized logical access.
- Modifying logical access.
- Disabling logical access.
- Managing company technology assets.
- Supporting technical termination actions.
- Maintaining applicable access records.

---

# 11. Physical Access Responsibility

Physical-access responsibilities include:

- Facility credential issuance
- Facility credential modification
- Facility credential revocation
- Key control
- Restricted-area access
- Company property recovery

as applicable.

---

# 12. Employee Responsibilities

Employees are responsible for:

- Following company security requirements.
- Protecting company information.
- Protecting assigned credentials.
- Protecting company equipment.
- Reporting security concerns.
- Completing required security training.
- Returning company property when required.

---

# 13. Personnel Screening

Personnel screening should be appropriate to:

- Job responsibilities
- Information access
- System access
- Financial responsibility
- Physical access
- Applicable law
- Business risk

---

# 14. Proportional Screening

Screening must be proportionate to legitimate business requirements.

This procedure does not require unnecessary investigation of employees unrelated to their job responsibilities.

---

# 15. Screening Considerations

Where appropriate and legally permissible, screening may consider:

- Identity verification
- Employment history
- Professional qualifications
- References
- Other job-relevant information

based on the position.

---

# 16. Sensitive Positions

Positions with significant access or authority may justify additional screening appropriate to the role.

Examples may include roles with:

- Privileged system access
- Significant financial authority
- Sensitive personnel information
- Restricted information access
- High-value inventory responsibility

---

# 17. Screening Limitation

Completion of screening does not establish that an employee:

- Cannot become malicious.
- Will never violate policy.
- Should receive unrestricted access.
- No longer requires oversight.
- No longer requires access review.

Screening is one component of personnel security.

---

# 18. Confidentiality

Employees and nonemployees must protect company information according to its sensitivity.

---

# 19. Employee Confidentiality

Employee confidentiality obligations may arise from:

- Employment requirements
- Company policy
- Job responsibilities
- Specific agreements
- Applicable law

---

# 20. Nonemployee Confidentiality

Before a nonemployee or outside party receives sensitive company information, an appropriate Non-Disclosure Agreement (NDA) or equivalent enforceable confidentiality obligation must be in place.

---

# 21. Access-Based Confidentiality

The nonemployee confidentiality requirement is based on:

- Information access
- Information sensitivity
- Nature of the work

rather than vendor title alone.

---

# 22. Confidentiality Examples

Depending on access, confidentiality protection may apply to:

- Consultants
- Information Technology service providers
- Infinity contractors
- Penetration testers
- Temporary specialists
- Service personnel
- Other outside support personnel

---

# 23. Confidentiality Limitation

A confidentiality agreement does not by itself establish that:

- Logical access is appropriate.
- Physical access is appropriate.
- Restricted-area access is appropriate.
- The individual is competent.
- Security controls are effective.
- Oversight is sufficient.

---

# 24. Security Responsibilities

Personnel must be informed of security responsibilities applicable to their work.

---

# 25. Security Awareness

Security-awareness requirements are established by [[POL-006 Security Awareness & Training Policy]].

---

# 26. Initial Awareness

New employees must complete required initial security awareness within the period established by the Security Awareness & Training Policy.

---

# 27. Ongoing Awareness

Personnel must complete required refresher training and other security-awareness activities.

---

# 28. Role-Specific Security

Personnel with specialized responsibilities may require additional security instruction.

Examples include:

- Administrators
- Managers
- Finance personnel
- Human Resources personnel
- Warehouse personnel
- Application support personnel

---

# 29. Acceptable Use

Personnel using company information systems must comply with [[POL-002 Acceptable Use Policy]].

---

# 30. Access Principle

Employment does not automatically authorize access to all company:

- Systems
- Applications
- Information
- Facilities
- Restricted areas
- Records

---

# 31. Least Privilege

Access must be based on legitimate business need and limited to what is required for assigned responsibilities.

---

# 32. Individual Accountability

Personnel must use individually attributable credentials where required.

Shared access must not be used when individual accountability is required.

---

# 33. Warehouse Workstations

Shared Windows workstation accounts may be used in warehouse operations where operationally justified.

Significant Warehouse Management System transactions must maintain individual accountability.

---

# 34. Privileged Access

Privileged access must be separately authorized and managed under:

- [[PROC-002 Privileged Access Procedure]]
- [[STD-005 Authentication & Account Security Standard]]

---

# 35. Separate Privileged Credentials

Information Technology personnel requiring privileged access must use separate privileged credentials from ordinary user credentials where required.

---

# 36. Physical Access

Employee physical access must comply with [[PROC-014 Facility Access Procedure]].

---

# 37. Restricted Areas

Restricted-area access must comply with [[PROC-016 Restricted Area Access Procedure]].

---

# 38. New Employee Process

For a new employee:

1. Human Resources establishes employment status.
2. Management identifies required responsibilities.
3. Management identifies required access.
4. Appropriate access is authorized.
5. Information Technology provisions logical access.
6. Appropriate physical access is issued.
7. Company equipment is assigned as required.
8. Required security awareness is completed.
9. Applicable confidentiality requirements are established.

---

# 39. Access Before Employment

Normal employee access should not be activated before the employee requires it for authorized work.

---

# 40. Manager Authorization

Managers must not request access solely for convenience when the access is unrelated to assigned responsibilities.

---

# 41. New Employee Access Scope

Initial access should reflect:

- Job role
- Department
- Assigned facility
- Required applications
- Required information
- Required physical areas

---

# 42. Administrative Access

Administrative privileges must not be provided solely because an employee is:

- Senior
- An executive
- Technically knowledgeable
- A manager

Privileged access must be based on operational need.

---

# 43. Standard User Baseline

Ordinary users, including executives, operate without local administrative rights under the established endpoint baseline.

---

# 44. Company Equipment

Company equipment should be assigned according to job need.

Examples include:

- Desktop
- Laptop
- Company phone
- Other authorized equipment

---

# 45. Mobile Personnel

Personnel requiring mobile work may receive:

- Company laptop
- Company phone
- Other approved mobile capability

according to business need.

---

# 46. Company Equipment Accountability

Assigned company equipment must be reasonably attributable to the responsible employee.

---

# 47. Equipment Protection

Personnel must take reasonable steps to protect company equipment from:

- Loss
- Theft
- Unauthorized use
- Unnecessary exposure

---

# 48. Lost Equipment

Lost or stolen company equipment must be reported promptly.

---

# 49. Lost Device Coordination

Lost device response must coordinate with technical controls such as:

- Authentication
- Full-disk encryption
- Screen locking
- Central management
- Mobile device management
- Remote lock or wipe where available

---

# 50. Role Change

A change in job responsibilities requires review of existing access.

---

# 51. Mover Process

When an employee transfers or changes role:

1. Human Resources or management identifies the change.
2. Existing access is identified.
3. Access associated with the prior role is reviewed.
4. Unnecessary access is revoked.
5. New access is separately authorized.
6. Physical access is adjusted.
7. Privileged access is reviewed.
8. Required equipment changes are completed.

---

# 52. Transfer Principle

A transfer begins with no assumption that previous business permissions remain necessary.

Required access must be reauthorized for the new responsibilities.

---

# 53. No Automatic Access Retention

Access from a previous role must not remain solely because removing it is inconvenient.

---

# 54. Transfer Timing

Access changes should be coordinated with the effective role change.

---

# 55. Privileged Role Change

A role change involving privileged access requires specific review of:

- Administrative accounts
- Group membership
- Domain privileges
- Application privileges
- Physical IT-area access
- Emergency access responsibilities

---

# 56. Physical Access Change

Facility and Restricted-area access must be reviewed when an employee:

- Changes facility
- Changes department
- Changes job
- Changes operational responsibilities

---

# 57. Temporary Assignment

Temporary assignment to additional duties may justify temporary access.

---

# 58. Temporary Access Expiration

Temporary access must have an identifiable end condition and should be removed when no longer required.

---

# 59. Access Reviews

Personnel access is subject to periodic review under:

- [[PROC-003 Access Review Procedure]]
- [[PROC-014 Facility Access Procedure]]
- [[PROC-016 Restricted Area Access Procedure]]

as applicable.

---

# 60. Ordinary Logical Access Review

Ordinary logical access is reviewed at least annually.

---

# 61. Privileged Access Review

Privileged logical access is reviewed at least quarterly.

---

# 62. Physical Access Review

Physical access is reviewed at least annually.

---

# 63. Access Review Participation

Managers and appropriate system or area owners must support access reviews by confirming whether access remains necessary.

---

# 64. Unnecessary Access

Access identified as unnecessary must be removed.

---

# 65. Unexplained Access

Access that cannot be justified must be investigated rather than automatically retained.

---

# 66. Dormant Accounts

Dormant logical accounts are managed according to [[STD-005 Authentication & Account Security Standard]].

---

# 67. Dormant Account Investigation

An ordinary account inactive for 30 days should be investigated.

---

# 68. Dormant Account Disablement

An ordinary account inactive for 60 days should be disabled unless a documented business reason supports continued availability.

---

# 69. Leave of Absence

Access during an extended leave should be evaluated according to:

- Business need
- Duration
- Role
- Risk

Access may be suspended where appropriate.

---

# 70. Return From Leave

Access suspended during leave must be appropriately reactivated before use.

---

# 71. Separation

Employee separation may include:

- Resignation
- Retirement
- Layoff
- Dismissal
- Other end of employment

---

# 72. Termination Principle

Access must not continue after the employee no longer has an authorized business need.

---

# 73. Termination Notification

Human Resources or responsible management must promptly notify personnel responsible for:

- Logical access
- Physical access
- Equipment
- Restricted areas
- Other relevant company assets

when employment ends.

---

# 74. Termination Coordination

Termination should coordinate:

- Human Resources
- Employee management
- Information Technology
- Physical access responsibility
- Other relevant business functions

---

# 75. Termination Checklist

A termination checklist should address applicable:

- User accounts
- Privileged accounts
- Email
- Remote access
- Application access
- Access cards
- Keys
- Smart cards where implemented
- Company laptop
- Company phone
- Documents
- Backup media
- Other company property

---

# 76. Logical Access Disablement

Logical access must be disabled promptly in coordination with the effective termination.

---

# 77. Physical Access Revocation

Physical access must be revoked promptly in coordination with the effective termination.

---

# 78. Access Card

Company access cards should be recovered where practical and electronically disabled where supported.

---

# 79. Keys

Company keys must be recovered where applicable.

---

# 80. Smart Cards

Where smart-card authentication is implemented, applicable smart cards must be recovered or invalidated.

---

# 81. Company Equipment Recovery

Applicable company equipment should be recovered during separation.

---

# 82. Information Return

Personnel must return company information and records in their possession where required.

---

# 83. Company Information After Separation

Former personnel remain subject to applicable confidentiality obligations after employment or engagement ends.

---

# 84. Unreturned Equipment

Unreturned company equipment must be documented and evaluated according to:

- Asset value
- Information sensitivity
- Access capability
- Business risk

---

# 85. Unreturned Laptop

An unreturned laptop requires coordination with Information Technology to evaluate:

- Account access
- Device management
- Full-disk encryption
- Remote lock or wipe where available
- Information exposure
- Recovery options

---

# 86. Unreturned Phone

An unreturned company phone requires evaluation of:

- Company information
- Company service
- Mobile device management
- Remote lock or wipe where available
- Account access

---

# 87. Unreturned Key

An unreturned key requires evaluation of:

- Area accessible
- Restricted-area exposure
- Need for rekeying
- Temporary compensating controls

---

# 88. Unreturned Access Card

An unreturned electronic access card should be disabled promptly.

---

# 89. Involuntary Termination

Involuntary termination presenting elevated security concerns requires coordinated planning appropriate to the circumstances.

---

# 90. Involuntary Termination Coordination

Coordination may include:

- Human Resources
- Management
- Information Technology
- Facility responsibility
- Legal
- Law enforcement where a credible safety threat exists

---

# 91. Immediate Access Removal

When circumstances require immediate removal, logical and physical access should be disabled at the appropriate termination time.

---

# 92. Termination Safety

Personnel safety must be considered when planning or conducting a termination.

Security procedures must not create unnecessary confrontation or danger.

---

# 93. Termination Evidence

Termination records should provide sufficient evidence to determine whether applicable security actions were completed.

---

# 94. Termination Exception

Any material termination action that cannot be completed must be:

- Documented
- Risk evaluated
- Assigned for follow-up
- Escalated where appropriate

---

# 95. Contractor Personnel

Contractors must receive access based on:

- Business need
- Sponsorship
- Required duration
- Required systems
- Required information
- Required physical areas

---

# 96. Contractor Sponsor

Each contractor requiring recurring company access must have an appropriate company sponsor.

---

# 97. Contractor Duration

Contractor access must be time-limited according to [[PROC-004 Contractor Access Procedure]].

---

# 98. Contractor Initial Authorization

Contractor access may be initially authorized for up to three months.

Continued access requires review and renewal.

---

# 99. Contractor Renewal

After initial authorization, contractor access may be renewed for periods not exceeding six months when business need continues.

---

# 100. Contractor Access Expiration

Contractor access must not continue indefinitely because an expiration date was overlooked.

---

# 101. Contractor Separation

When a contractor's engagement ends:

- Logical access must be removed.
- Physical access must be removed.
- Company credentials must be recovered or disabled.
- Company equipment must be recovered.
- Restricted-area access must be removed.

as applicable.

---

# 102. Contractor Confidentiality

Required confidentiality obligations must remain applicable according to their terms after contractor access ends.

---

# 103. Infinity Contractor

Infinity contractor personnel receive access according to:

- [[PROC-004 Contractor Access Procedure]]
- [[PROC-015 Visitor & Vendor Access Procedure]]
- [[PROC-020 Contractor Development Access Procedure]]

---

# 104. Infinity Ownership

Dunder Mifflin retains ownership of:

- Infinity application
- Source code
- Repository
- Documentation
- Data
- Backups
- Intellectual property
- Contract work product

---

# 105. Contractor Dependency

Infinity support must not create unnecessary dependence on one contractor or individual.

---

# 106. Infinity Transferability

Infinity documentation and operational knowledge must support transfer to another qualified party if necessary.

Transferability is reviewed under [[RPT-004 Infinity Transferability Review]].

---

# 107. Temporary Personnel

Temporary personnel must receive access appropriate to:

- Assignment
- Duration
- Business need
- Information sensitivity

---

# 108. Temporary Personnel Expiration

Temporary personnel access must be removed when the assignment ends.

---

# 109. Temporary Personnel Confidentiality

Temporary personnel receiving sensitive information must have appropriate confidentiality obligations.

---

# 110. Temporary Personnel Equipment

Company equipment issued to temporary personnel must be recovered when the assignment ends.

---

# 111. Malicious Insider Risk

Personnel controls support treatment of malicious-insider risk but cannot eliminate it.

---

# 112. R012 Malicious Insider

R012 has:

- Inherent Risk: **15 High**
- Current Residual Risk: **10 High**
- Target Residual Risk: **5 Moderate**

---

# 113. R012 Treatment

Personnel-security controls supporting R012 include:

- Least privilege
- Separation of Duties
- Individual accountability
- Separate privileged credentials
- Access reviews
- Personnel lifecycle controls
- Information classification
- Physical security
- Financial approval controls
- Security awareness
- Security-event reporting
- Incident Response

---

# 114. Authorized Access Distinction

Malicious-insider risk concerns misuse of legitimate or legitimately obtained access.

It must not be confused with ordinary unauthorized-access risk.

---

# 115. Privacy

Personnel-security controls must remain proportionate to legitimate business and security requirements.

---

# 116. No Indiscriminate Surveillance

This procedure does not authorize indiscriminate monitoring of employees.

---

# 117. Security Investigation

A legitimate security concern may require investigation using appropriate:

- Access records
- System logs
- Business records
- Witness information
- Physical evidence

within authorized and lawful scope.

---

# 118. Investigation Coordination

Personnel-related security investigations may require coordination among:

- Human Resources
- Management
- Information Security
- Information Technology
- Legal
- Operations

depending on the incident.

---

# 119. Incident Response

Personnel-related security incidents must be handled under [[PLN-001 Incident Response Plan]] where applicable.

---

# 120. Evidence Handling

Security evidence must be handled according to [[PROC-011 Evidence Handling Procedure]].

---

# 121. Reporting Concerns

Employees should promptly report suspected:

- Credential misuse
- Unauthorized access
- Information theft
- Fraud
- Deliberate security-control bypass
- Theft of company property
- Other significant security concerns

---

# 122. Good-Faith Reporting

Personnel should be able to report security concerns through appropriate management or security channels without being required to personally investigate the suspected activity.

---

# 123. Financial Personnel Controls

Personnel with financial responsibilities must follow applicable:

- Authorization
- Separation of Duties
- Reconciliation
- Change verification
- Accountability

requirements.

---

# 124. Vendor Fraud

Personnel controls support R029 Vendor Fraud through:

- Appropriate financial responsibility assignment
- Separation of Duties
- Vendor-change verification
- Accountability
- Reconciliation

---

# 125. Sales Fraud

Personnel controls support R030 Sales Fraud through:

- Individual accountability
- Approval of significant pricing, discount, credit, or refund activity
- Reconciliation
- Investigation of anomalies

---

# 126. Payroll Fraud

Personnel controls support R031 Payroll Fraud through:

- Human Resources authoritative employment records
- Payroll access restrictions
- Separation of Duties where practical
- Authorization of compensation changes
- Reconciliation
- Investigation of anomalies

---

# 127. Inventory Theft

Personnel controls support R032 Inventory Theft through:

- Authorized warehouse access
- Restricted cage access
- Individual accountability for significant transactions
- Inventory reconciliation
- Investigation of discrepancies

---

# 128. Personnel Loss

Dunder Mifflin must manage risk created by loss or unavailability of key personnel.

---

# 129. R026 Personnel Loss

R026 has:

- Inherent Risk: **16 High**
- Current Residual Risk: **9 Moderate**
- Target Residual Risk: **4 Low**

---

# 130. Personnel-Loss Causes

Personnel loss may result from:

- Resignation
- Termination
- Illness
- Injury
- Extended absence
- Retirement
- Unavailability during emergency
- Contractor departure

---

# 131. Key Personnel Dependency

Managers should identify important functions where successful operation depends excessively on one individual.

---

# 132. Dependency Examples

Personnel dependency may exist when only one person possesses:

- Required credentials
- Critical system knowledge
- Recovery knowledge
- Configuration information
- Vendor relationships
- Process knowledge
- Source-code knowledge
- Management authority required for an essential action

---

# 133. Dependency Treatment

Personnel dependencies should be reduced through:

- Documentation
- Cross-training
- Alternate personnel
- Delegated authority
- Controlled emergency access
- Company ownership of operational information

---

# 134. Documentation

Important operational knowledge must be documented sufficiently for a qualified replacement to perform essential duties.

---

# 135. Documentation Ownership

Important company documentation must remain under company control.

---

# 136. Credential Ownership

Essential company access must not depend on credentials known or controlled only by one employee or contractor.

---

# 137. Configuration Ownership

Important configuration information must remain accessible to authorized company personnel.

---

# 138. Source-Code Ownership

Dunder Mifflin must maintain control of company-owned source code and associated development records.

---

# 139. Vendor Information

Important vendor:

- Contacts
- Account information
- Support procedures
- Escalation information

should not be known only to one employee.

---

# 140. Cross-Training

Cross-training should be prioritized for functions where individual unavailability could materially affect operations.

---

# 141. Cross-Training Priorities

Cross-training may be particularly important for:

- Information Technology administration
- Security operations
- Backup and recovery
- Business Continuity
- Warehouse operations
- Finance
- Payroll
- Procurement
- Management responsibilities

---

# 142. Cross-Training Objective

Cross-training is intended to provide reasonable continuity.

It does not require every employee to be capable of performing every job.

---

# 143. Alternate Personnel

Important functions should identify qualified alternate personnel where practical.

---

# 144. Delegated Authority

Business Continuity planning should identify appropriate delegated authority when a required decision maker is unavailable.

---

# 145. Emergency Access

Emergency technical access required because normal personnel are unavailable must follow [[PROC-005 Emergency Access Procedure]].

---

# 146. Emergency Physical Access

Emergency physical access must follow applicable requirements in:

- [[PROC-014 Facility Access Procedure]]
- [[PROC-016 Restricted Area Access Procedure]]

---

# 147. Recovery Documentation

Recovery documentation should be sufficient for a qualified Information Technology professional unfamiliar with the environment to perform required recovery activities.

---

# 148. Business Continuity

Personnel-loss planning must coordinate with [[PLN-002 Business Continuity Plan]].

---

# 149. Disaster Recovery

Technical personnel dependencies affecting recovery must coordinate with [[PLN-003 Disaster Recovery Plan]].

---

# 150. Manual Continuity

Manual processing must not depend unnecessarily on a single employee knowing an undocumented process.

---

# 151. Manual Processing Documentation

Manual continuity responsibilities should be documented under [[PROC-013 Manual Processing Procedure]].

---

# 152. Succession

Management should identify reasonable succession or delegated decision authority for functions where prolonged leadership unavailability could materially affect operations.

---

# 153. No Exclusive Critical Capability

No employee or contractor should intentionally maintain exclusive control of a critical company capability when reasonable documentation or alternate access can eliminate that dependency.

---

# 154. Personnel Dependency Review

Managers should review significant personnel dependencies:

- Periodically
- Following major role changes
- Following key personnel departure
- Following Business Continuity exercises
- When a material dependency is discovered

---

# 155. Personnel Dependency Evidence

Evidence may include:

- Procedures
- Cross-training records
- Alternate assignments
- Recovery documentation
- Delegated authority
- Transferability reviews
- Exercise results

---

# 156. Security Awareness During Employment

Security awareness is an ongoing responsibility rather than a one-time hiring activity.

---

# 157. Awareness Topics

Personnel awareness may include:

- Phishing
- Credential protection
- Sensitive information handling
- Visitor security
- Physical security
- Lost equipment
- Security-event reporting
- Social engineering
- Incident reporting

---

# 158. Monthly Phishing Simulations

Monthly phishing simulations support ongoing awareness and identification of recurring weakness.

---

# 159. Simulation Purpose

Phishing simulations are intended to:

- Reinforce awareness
- Identify training needs
- Measure trends
- Improve reporting behavior

They are not intended as a substitute for technical controls.

---

# 160. Annual Refresher

Personnel must complete annual security-awareness refresher training as required by [[POL-006 Security Awareness & Training Policy]].

---

# 161. Training Assessment

The annual awareness program includes the required knowledge assessment established by policy.

---

# 162. Training Records

Training records should identify:

- Person
- Required training
- Completion
- Date
- Assessment result where applicable

---

# 163. Training Exception

Required training not completed within the established period must be followed up and escalated where appropriate.

---

# 164. Personnel Security Records

Personnel-security records should be maintained according to legitimate:

- Human Resources
- Security
- Legal
- Business

requirements.

---

# 165. Personnel Record Protection

Sensitive personnel records must be protected according to their information classification.

---

# 166. Minimum Necessary Access

Access to personnel-security records must be limited to personnel with legitimate business need.

---

# 167. Personnel Information Privacy

Personnel-security records must not be unnecessarily distributed or exposed.

---

# 168. Security Metrics

Personnel-security metrics may include:

- Termination access actions completed
- Termination exceptions
- Access reviews completed
- Unnecessary access identified
- Unnecessary access removed
- Company property not returned
- Required training completion
- Contractor access expirations
- Personnel dependencies identified
- Cross-training completed
- Overdue personnel-security findings

---

# 169. Key Performance Indicators

Key Performance Indicators (KPIs) may include:

- Percentage of required termination actions completed
- Percentage of access reviews completed

# 170. Key Risk Indicators

Key Risk Indicators (KRIs) may include:

- Terminated personnel retaining active access
- Unreturned sensitive company property

- Repeated awareness failure
- Overdue material personnel-security corrective action

# 173. R011 Treatment Relationship

This procedure supports R011 through:

- Human Resources authoritative status
- Prompt termination notification

- Exception follow-up

---

# 174. R012 Malicious Insider

Personnel security supports R012 while preserving the distinction between:

- Unauthorized access
- Excessive access
- Malicious use of legitimate access

---

# 175. R019 Infinity Contractor

R019 has:

- Inherent Risk: **12 High**
- Current Residual Risk: **8 Moderate**
- Target Residual Risk: **4 Low**

Personnel controls support R019 through:

- Sponsorship
- Time-limited access
- Confidentiality
- Physical access control
- Company ownership of documentation and source code
- Transferability
- Prompt access removal

---

# 176. R026 Personnel Loss

This procedure directly supports treatment of R026 through:

- Documentation
- Cross-training
- Alternate personnel
- Delegated authority
- Emergency access
- Company control of essential operational information

---

# 177. R031 Payroll Fraud

Human Resources' authoritative employee-status records support detection and prevention of unauthorized payroll activity involving:

- Inactive employees
- Unauthorized employee changes
- Unexpected personnel records

---

# 178. R032 Inventory Theft

Personnel access and accountability controls support R032 without replacing physical security and inventory reconciliation controls.

---

# 179. Finding

A finding is a documented conclusion supported by evidence concerning whether a requirement, control, process, or expected outcome was satisfied.

---

# 180. Personnel Security Findings

Potential findings include:

- Access not removed after termination
- Unnecessary access after transfer
- Missing termination evidence
- Unreturned company property
- Expired contractor access
- Missing required confidentiality protection
- Required training not completed
- Critical personnel dependency without adequate mitigation
- Missing Restricted-area access removal

---

# 181. Corrective Action

Material personnel-security deficiencies must identify:

- Finding
- Required corrective action
- Accountable owner
- Due date
- Validation method
- Retest where appropriate

---

# 182. Finding Register

Material findings and corrective actions are tracked in [[REG-004 Finding & Corrective Action Register]].

---

# 183. Corrective Action Validation

A personnel-security corrective action is not complete merely because an administrative task was marked complete.

Validation must determine whether the underlying weakness was corrected.

---

# 184. Assessment

Personnel-security controls are subject to assurance under [[PRO-008 Security Assurance & Continuous Improvement Program]].

---

# 185. Assessment Methods

Assessment may use:

- Inspection
- Interviews
- Sampling
- Record review
- Observation
- Independent Performance

---

# 186. Independent Performance

An assessor may independently perform an authorized personnel-security procedure.

For example:

1. Select a sample of personnel.
2. Determine current employment status.
3. Identify current role.
4. Identify required logical and physical access.
5. Compare actual access with business need.
6. Identify unnecessary or unexplained access.
7. Document the result.

---

# 187. Joiner Testing

An assessor may select new employees and determine whether:

- Employment status was established.
- Access was authorized.
- Access matched job need.
- Required security awareness was completed.
- Required physical access was appropriately issued.

---

# 188. Mover Testing

An assessor may select transferred employees and determine whether:

- Prior access was reviewed.
- Unnecessary prior access was removed.
- New access was authorized.
- Privileged access was appropriately addressed.
- Physical access was updated.

---

# 189. Leaver Testing

An assessor may select separated employees and determine whether:

- Logical access was disabled.
- Physical access was revoked.
- Keys were recovered.
- Access cards were disabled or recovered.
- Company equipment was recovered.
- Smart cards were recovered or invalidated where applicable.
- Exceptions were documented.

---

# 190. Contractor Testing

An assessor may select contractor personnel and determine whether:

- Sponsor remains valid.
- Engagement remains active.
- Access remains necessary.
- Access duration remains authorized.
- Required confidentiality protection exists.
- Expired access was removed.

---

# 191. Personnel Dependency Testing

An assessor may select an important business or technical function and determine whether a qualified alternate can reasonably perform the function using available:

- Procedures
- Documentation
- Credentials
- Configuration information
- Vendor information
- Delegated authority

---

# 192. Training Testing

An assessor may sample personnel and determine whether required security-awareness training was completed within the required period.

---

# 193. Evidence Integrity

Assessment evidence must reflect actual conditions.

Personnel must not:

- Backdate termination records.
- Backdate access reviews.
- Invent training completion.
- Create fictional screening evidence.
- Conceal unreturned property.
- Record access removal that did not occur.
- Invent cross-training activity.
- Alter findings to make controls appear effective.

---

# 194. Control Effectiveness

Personnel-security controls may be assessed as:

- Effective
- Partially Effective
- Ineffective
- Not Implemented

---

# 195. Effectiveness Evidence

Control-effectiveness conclusions should consider:

- Employment records
- Access records
- Access reviews
- Termination records
- Equipment records
- Contractor records
- Training records
- Personnel dependency documentation
- Findings
- Corrective-action validation

---

# 196. Current and Proposed Controls

Personnel-security reporting must distinguish among:

- Current controls
- Partially implemented controls
- Planned controls
- Implemented controls awaiting validation
- Validated controls

---

# 197. No Premature Risk Credit

Current residual risk must not be reduced merely because:

- A new personnel procedure is documented.
- Cross-training is planned.
- Training is scheduled.
- An access review is scheduled.
- A termination checklist is created.
- A corrective action is assigned.

Risk reduction requires implementation and appropriate validation.

---

# 198. Target Residual Risk

Target residual risk represents expected risk after planned treatment.

It is a prediction.

---

# 199. Validated Post-Treatment Risk

After personnel-security controls are implemented and validated, applicable risks may be reassessed.

Actual validated risk may be:

- Higher than target
- Equal to target
- Lower than target

---

# 200. Historical Integrity

The original May 2013 risk assessment must remain preserved.

Later validated assessments may establish a new current residual risk without rewriting the historical assessment.

---

# 201. Exceptions

Exceptions to this procedure must be:

- Documented
- Justified
- Risk evaluated
- Approved under appropriate authority
- Time-limited where practical
- Reviewed when conditions change

---

# 202. Emergency Exceptions

A genuine emergency may require temporary deviation from normal personnel-security procedures.

Any material deviation should be:

- Necessary
- Limited
- Documented
- Reviewed afterward

---

# 203. Procedure Review

This procedure must be reviewed when material changes affect:

- Employment processes
- Contractor processes
- Access management
- Security-awareness requirements
- Facility access
- Personnel dependencies
- Business Continuity
- Personnel-security risk

---

# 204. Traceability

This procedure supports the enterprise traceability model:

**Business Requirement → Asset / Process → Classification / BIA → Risk → Treatment → Policy / Standard → Control → Procedure → Implementation → Evidence → Test / Audit → Finding → Remediation → Retest → Risk Reassessment**

Personnel security is not established merely by hiring trustworthy people.

Effective personnel security requires:

**Appropriate Selection → Defined Responsibility → Appropriate Access → Awareness → Review → Change Management → Timely Separation → Validation**
